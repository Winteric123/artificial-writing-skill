# PMID 32649874 — language-retention note

## Vocabulary and collocations

- **deep-scale proteogenomic characterization** — Abstract/Methods wording for coordinated genomics, proteomics, and PTMs; list modalities rather than using it decoratively.
- **paired tumor–normal-adjacent-tissue analysis** — Results wording that keeps the within-patient comparison explicit.
- **attenuation from copy number to protein abundance** — Results wording for weakened propagation across molecular layers.
- **cascading cis-effects** — Results wording for coordinated methylation/CNA, RNA, protein, and phosphosite associations.
- **immune-cold behavior** — Discussion shorthand; pair it with the actual inferred-cell and pathway evidence.
- **proteomically visible but transcriptionally silent** — useful for neutrophil-granule proteins; does not mean RNA was technically missing.
- **mutation-associated phosphosite outlier** — Results wording for discovery signals that have not been functionally validated.
- **hypothesis-generating therapeutic nomination** — Discussion boundary for database-supported target candidates.

## Synthetic sentence frames

1. “The genomic event propagated strongly to RNA but was attenuated at the protein and phosphosite levels, underscoring the value of direct proteomic measurement.”
2. “STK11 mutation was associated with lower inferred **[cell type]** signatures in tumors, whereas the corresponding NATs showed the opposite direction.”
3. “The pathway was evident in protein abundance but not RNA expression, consistent with post-transcriptional storage or release rather than altered cell number.”
4. “Although the outlier is pharmacologically tractable, it remains a target nomination because dependency and treatment response were not tested in patients.”
5. “Because geography, smoking, sex, and driver genotype were correlated, the subgroup association should not be interpreted as an independent demographic effect.”

## Paragraph architecture

A strong proteogenomic Results paragraph moves from genomic event → RNA consequence → protein/PTM consequence → pathway enrichment → orthogonal annotation or validation → explicit causal boundary. An immune paragraph should distinguish estimated composition, measured protein abundance, matched NAT direction, and clinical meaning.

## Safe transfer

- Use for multi-omic cohort reporting when each assay’s sample count and measurement level remain explicit.
- “Associated with” is appropriate for STK11 mutation comparisons; “suggests” is appropriate for neutrophil-degranulation mechanism.
- Separate original CPTAC measurements from external TCGA, Connectivity Map, xCell, ESTIMATE, and DepMap reanalyses.

## Unsafe transfer

- Do not call xCell estimates flow-cytometric measurements or the 94% internal classifier a clinically validated diagnostic.
- Do not equate neutrophil-degranulation proteins with increased neutrophil abundance.
- Do not present database-linked kinases as clinically effective targets.
- Do not attribute geographic or sex effects independently when correlated driver/smoking structure remains.

## Indexed language units

- `STK11 mutation was associated with lower inferred [cell-type] signatures in tumors, whereas the corresponding NATs showed the opposite direction.`
- `genomic event → RNA effect → protein/PTM effect → pathway enrichment → orthogonal support → causal boundary`
