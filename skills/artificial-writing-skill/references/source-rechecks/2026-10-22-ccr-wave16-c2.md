## PMID 32102950 — Baseline Plasma Tumor Mutation Burden Predicts Response to Pembrolizumab-based Therapy in Patients with Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

- Identity: Aggarwal et al., *Clinical Cancer Research* (2020); DOI `10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-19-3663`; supplied author manuscript, 15 physical pages; SHA-256 `995748fef60f2be677f31e65f16d4b7f60476761460dc0e4b49cd55bef67c82c`.
- Question/design: Prospective, single-center observational biomarker study of pretreatment plasma TMB in 66 patients beginning first-line pembrolizumab alone (`n=31`) or pembrolizumab plus platinum-pemetrexed (`n=35`). Regimen allocation was nonrandom and largely determined by tissue PD-L1; the study therefore evaluates outcome association, not treatment-effect modification.
- Coverage: Abstract pp1–2; Introduction pp2–3; Materials and Methods pp3–4; Results pp4–6; Discussion/closing synthesis pp6–8; references and administrative material pp8–10; Translational Relevance p11. Figure 1 and caption p12, Figure 2 and caption p13, and the two-page Table 1 on pp14–15 were visually checked in full.
- Analysis sets and assay: Plasma TMB was evaluable in 52/66 patients (78.8%; 26 per regimen), and week-9 response was evaluable in 45. The 500-gene, 2.145-Mb assay used study-specific counting, shedding, coverage, clonal-hematopoiesis, driver/resistance, and germline filters. Fourteen unevaluable samples must not be relabeled low-TMB or mutation-negative.
- Response and PFS: Week-9 responders had median pTMB 21.5 versus 13.9 mut/Mb (`P=.037`), and the durable-clinical-benefit comparison was 21.3 versus 12.4 (`P=.003`). Continuous pTMB was associated with PFS (HR `.93` per mut/Mb, 95% CI `.90–.97`; `P=.001`). The binary cutoff of 16 mut/Mb was outcome-informed in the same dataset: median PFS was 14.1 versus 4.7 months (HR `.30`, 95% CI `.16–.60`; `P<.001`).
- Regimen and endpoint uncertainty: The monotherapy PFS estimate was HR `.24` (95% CI `.09–.66`; `P=.005`), whereas the combination estimate was HR `.39` (95% CI `.15–1.01`; `P=.053`). Overall OS favored the high-pTMB group numerically but remained inconclusive (HR `.48`, 95% CI `.22–1.03`; `P=.061`); both regimen-specific OS comparisons were also nonsignificant. Different subgroup significance levels are not an interaction test.
- Exploratory genomic refinement: Six pTMB-high patients lacked durable benefit. Nine putative negative-predictor **mutation events** involving `STK11`, `KEAP1`, `PTEN`, or `ERBB2` were not significant for PFS alone (`P=.110`); fraction genome aneuploidy was also nonsignificant for response (`P=.198`). The same-cohort composite (pTMB-high without a selected negative mutation, `n=23`, versus pTMB-low or any selected negative mutation, `n=29`) showed favorable PFS and OS associations, but it did not establish incremental performance, calibration, causal resistance, or clinical utility.
- Safety and omitted domains: The main article did not provide a detailed safety analysis, matched clinical plasma–tissue TMB comparison, transcriptomic/single-cell/proteomic analysis, or evaluation of the tumor microenvironment, MHC status, immune competence, or microbiome.

Source alerts and quarantine:

1. Physical p5 prints a week-9 response OR of `1.09` with 95% CI `1.02–1.08` and `P=.018`; the point estimate lies outside the printed interval. The page was visually rechecked. The exact OR/CI pair is withheld rather than silently repaired.
2. Discussion p7 calls 52/66 `79.8%`, whereas the Abstract and Results correctly print `78.8%`. Preserve the fraction and the internally consistent percentage; retain the source discrepancy.
3. Methods define durable clinical benefit as CR/PR/SD lasting more than six months, whereas the Figure 1B caption describes CR/PR/SD “as of 6 months” and reduces non-durable benefit to progressive disease. Use the formal Methods definition and disclose the caption mismatch.
4. The cutoff was selected partly by maximizing the current cohort's PFS separation, and the molecular composite was constructed and tested in the same small cohort. Neither is externally validated here.
5. Despite predictive wording in the title and Discussion, a treated-only nonrandomized cohort cannot distinguish general prognosis from treatment-specific prediction.

Claim ceiling: Prospective ascertainment supports an association between assay-specific baseline pTMB and response/PFS during pembrolizumab-based therapy, plus a hypothesis-generating mutation-informed composite. It does not validate the cutoff, prove a pTMB-by-regimen interaction, establish OS benefit, or demonstrate any individual mutation as a causal or predictive resistance marker.

Source-language record:

- Original sentence (Discussion, physical p8): “These are small numbers and the analysis should be considered purely exploratory.”
- Literature: Aggarwal et al., PMID `32102950`, DOI `10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-19-3663`.
- Result type: favorable exploratory genomic composite after nonsignificant individual-feature analyses.
- Applicable scenario: a small, same-cohort molecular refinement appears stronger than the original biomarker but lacks external validation and formal incremental-performance testing.
- Safe rewrite template: `Although [exploratory composite] was associated with [endpoint], the small same-cohort analysis remains hypothesis-generating and requires independent validation before use for treatment selection.`

Gates: `coverage_check=pass`; `evidence_check=pass`; `results_check=pass`; `language_check=pass`; `traceability_check=pass`; `transfer_check=pass`.

Verdict: `passed` with the OR/CI pair, percentage discrepancy, DCB-definition mismatch, internally selected cutoff, same-cohort composite, and non-predictive design boundary quarantined.

## PMID 31694835 — Organoid Cultures as Preclinical Models of Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

- Identity: Shi et al., *Clinical Cancer Research* (2020); DOI `10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-19-1376`; publisher-typeset PDF, 14 physical pages (13 article pages plus one publisher service page); SHA-256 `f4d4126c44129452041a136420de1da6d53b3abc65eae903fe395791b4ec3dba`.
- Question/design: Preclinical model-development study using surgically resected primary NSCLC and established patient-derived xenografts, followed by histology/IHC, species-aware purity assessment, WES/CNV, bulk RNA-seq, targeted protein assays, in-vitro drug testing, organoid xenografts, and matched PDX experiments.
- Coverage: Abstract/Introduction pp1–2; Methods pp2–3; Results pp3–9; Discussion/closing synthesis pp9 and 11; references/administrative material pp11–13; publisher service page p14. Figures 1–5 and all captions on pp4, 6, 7, 9, and 10 were visually checked in full. The main article contains no standalone main table.
- Establishment and purity: Of 76 processed specimens, 11 were excluded and 65 were eligible (30 primary tumors and 35 PDXs). Initiation was 57/65 (88%), short-term maintenance 47/65 (72%), and long-term maintenance 10/65 (15%). Normal-cell outgrowth was assessed in only 12 evaluable short-term cultures and occurred in 7/12; it must not be generalized to all 65. Long-term purity was reported using different histologic and flow-cytometric measures, which are not interchangeable.
- Molecular fidelity: WES/CNV analyses comprised nine model groupings and 23 displayed specimens (three patient–organoid pairs, one PDX–organoid pair, and five patient–PDX–organoid triplets). Bulk-RNA comparison of 893 selected genes yielded patient–organoid correlation `.59` (95% CI `.54–.64`), PDX–organoid `.80` (`.75–.85`), and patient–PDX `.66` (`.57–.75`). These assay- and source-pair-specific correlations support partial preservation, not complete tumor fidelity or preservation of the immune microenvironment.
- Targeted-drug examples: Four early-passage organoids supported an EGFR–erlotinib proof of principle. In long-term models, the single `KRAS G13C`/amplified PDXO426 model was more sensitive to MEK inhibition than three wild-type models; its matched PDX showed trametinib growth inhibition (`n=5`/arm), whereas the PDXO274 wild-type PDX result was nonsignificant (`n=6`/arm). This is a model-specific association without isogenic causal proof or a pan-KRAS biomarker claim.
- FGFR/MEK combination: PDXO274 showed high FGFR1 expression/phosphorylation but little response to BGJ398 monotherapy. BGJ398 plus trametinib had combination indices below `.5` at ED50 and ED75, whereas BGJ398 plus BKM120 was antagonistic at ED50 (index above `1`) and only synergistic at ED75 (between `.5` and `1`). The matched-PDX combination experiment used 4–6 mice per arm and a day-38 endpoint comparison (`P<.05`) without exact effect size, confidence interval, group-specific sample count, or survival analysis.
- Clinical and immune boundary: The study tested model establishment and preclinical pharmacology, not patient treatment selection, clinical response, prognosis, or safety. Organoids lacked histologically detected stromal/immune cells and xenografts used immunodeficient hosts; the platform does not validate immune-checkpoint efficacy.

Source alerts and quarantine:

1. Physical p6 first states that nine long-term organoid cultures were compared by WES, then refers to mutation burden in “five long-term established organoids”; Figure 3 enumerates the nine groupings/23 specimens. The five-versus-nine prose denominator is not silently reconciled.
2. Discussion p11 calls the MEK result a short-term-culture finding, whereas the Results p8 heading and Figure 4 caption identify the tested panel as long-term organoids. Use the experiment-specific Results/Figure classification and disclose the mismatch.
3. Figure 4's unusual legend explicitly defines one star as `P>.05` and two stars as `P<.05`; the PDXO274 single-star comparison is therefore negative, consistent with the body text.
4. The text's general “weaker synergy” wording for BGJ398 plus BKM120 is effect-level dependent: Figure 5 shows antagonism at ED50 and synergy only at ED75. Do not summarize the pair as uniformly synergistic.
5. Tumor-cell enrichment, engraftment, culture adaptation, and absence of immune/stromal compartments can change clonal and expression composition. Correlation and matched-PDX response do not establish patient-level predictive accuracy.

Claim ceiling: The study establishes a workable collection of short- and long-term NSCLC organoid models that preserve selected histologic and molecular features and reproduce selected matched-PDX drug responses. It does not demonstrate complete molecular fidelity, an intact tumor microenvironment, universal mutation-specific sensitivity, or clinical treatment prediction.

Source-language record:

- Original sentence (Discussion, physical p11): “However, it did appear that the gene expression correlations were better for organoids derived from PDXs than patient tumors.”
- Literature: Shi et al., PMID `31694835`, DOI `10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-19-1376`.
- Result type: positive bulk-transcriptomic concordance with source-dependent attenuation.
- Applicable scenario: omics profiles correlate across matched samples, but concordance differs between direct patient-derived and intermediary-model-derived specimens.
- Safe rewrite template: `Gene-expression profiles were correlated across matched specimens, with stronger concordance for [intermediary-derived model/source] than for direct patient–model pairs; this pattern may reflect cell-composition heterogeneity or model selection and does not establish complete molecular fidelity.`

Gates: `coverage_check=pass`; `evidence_check=pass`; `results_check=pass`; `language_check=pass`; `traceability_check=pass`; `transfer_check=pass`.

Verdict: `passed` with the five-versus-nine WES denominator, short-versus-long culture label, unusual Figure 4 significance key, effect-level-dependent combination result, and preclinical-to-clinical boundary explicitly quarantined.
