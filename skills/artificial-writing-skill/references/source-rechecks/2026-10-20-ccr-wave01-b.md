## PMID 41378983

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Efficacy and CNS Toxicity of Nivolumab and Ipilimumab in Rare Cancer Brain Metastases: A Multicenter Basket Trial Analysis (NCI/SWOG S1609)*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `not_classified_by_header`.
- Registered PDF: `Ahluwalia_Efficacy_and_CNS_Toxicity_of_Nivolumab_and_Ipilimumab_in_Rare_Cancer_Brain_Metastases_A_Multicenter_PMID41378983.pdf`; SHA-256 `b244855ca3eecba87cf8b74885e4367a350f60c9e0c70a382eeab746be83a756`; 6 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`; cited supplementary material was not reviewed.
- Section map: abstract/introduction and start of Patients and Methods p1; methods and start of Results p2; Results and start of Discussion p3; Discussion pp3-5; data availability/admin p5; references pp5-6. Translational Relevance is on p2.
- Main displays visually checked: Table 1 p3; Table 2 and Figure 1 p4; Table 3 p5. All were readable.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: exploratory subgroup comparison within the open-label, single-arm phase II DART basket trial, not a randomized brain-metastasis comparison. Patients received nivolumab plus low-dose ipilimumab.
- Analysis sets: 727 response-evaluable patients, 707 without and 20 with brain metastases (BM). Intracranial imaging was evaluable in only 12/20 BM patients. No formal power calculation governed this exploratory analysis.
- Systemic efficacy: ORR was 10.0% with BM versus 11.5% without BM (`P=0.76`; Table 2, p4). Adjusted PFS was HR 1.29 (95% CI 0.81-2.07; `P=0.28`) and adjusted OS was HR 1.36 (0.81-2.27; `P=0.24`; Table 3, p5). Figure 1 reports unadjusted log-rank `P=0.80` for PFS and `P=0.60` for OS; these are different estimands/tests, not contradictory values.
- Intracranial result: no intracranial CR or PR was observed among 12 evaluable patients; 6/12 had stable disease. Two unusually durable stable-disease observations (1,841 and 1,094 days) are individual cases, not response-rate evidence. The steroid comparison was only 3 evaluable steroid-treated versus 9 evaluable untreated patients.
- Safety: grade >=3 nervous-system treatment-related toxicity was 1/20 (5%) with BM versus 19/707 (3%) without BM (`P=0.43`); grade 5 treatment-related toxicity was 1/20 (5%) versus 12/707 (2%; `P=0.31`). The BM death was a cycle-1 paraneoplastic syndrome (Table 2, p4).
- Precision/negative result: the paper states that the study had at least 80% power only for HR >=2.19; the PFS CI includes potentially important harm and benefit (p5). Therefore, nonsignificance does not establish similarity, equivalence, or noninferiority.
- Claim ceiling: in this small, heterogeneous, nonrandomized subgroup, no statistically significant between-group differences were detected, while intracranial objective responses were not observed in the limited assessable subset. Prior brain radiotherapy in 16/20, local rather than centralized imaging, histologic imbalance, sparse steroid strata, and missing uniform biomarkers constrain inference.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: Results uses the concession pattern “Although no intracranial responses were observed...” before reporting stable disease (p3). Discussion explicitly links the absence of significance to limited “power and precision” (pp4-5). These are appropriate only when the negative endpoint and uncertainty immediately follow.
- Safe template: “Although no statistically significant difference in [endpoint] was detected between [group A] and [group B] ([estimate], 95% CI [CI]; `P=[value]`), the interval remained compatible with [clinically relevant direction], precluding an equivalence claim.”
- Unsafe transfer: “The treatments/groups were comparable” or “dual ICI was intracranially effective” based on nonsignificant P values or stable disease alone.

## PMID 41537704

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Landscape of Genomic Mechanisms of Resistance to Selective RET Inhibitors in RET-Altered Solid Tumors: Analysis of the RETgistry Global Consortium*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `author_manuscript`.
- Registered PDF: `Waliany_Landscape_of_Genomic_Mechanisms_of_Resistance_to_Selective_RET_Inhibitors_in_RET-Altered_Solid_PMID41537704.pdf`; SHA-256 `bfbfba3545e7b853aecf493f8b6d841c12e06e1862a78140a7f0624cfe5ba50f`; 26 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`; cited supplemental tables/figures were not reviewed.
- Section map: title/admin p1; abstract p2; introduction p3; Patients and Methods pp4-5; Results pp5-8; Discussion pp8-11; data availability/admin pp12-13; references pp13-17; Translational Relevance p17; display pages pp18-26.
- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 p18; Figure 2 p19; Figure 3 p20; Figure 4 pp21-22; Figure 5 pp23-24; Table 1 pp25-26. Captions and multipanel continuations were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: retrospective international RETgistry analysis of molecular findings after progression on selective RET inhibitors (SRI), with paired pre/post analyses where available. This is a resistance-landscape study, not a randomized drug comparison or functional validation study.
- Analysis sets: 109 patients supplied 143 postprogression biopsies (91 tissue, 52 plasma); 121 biopsies were from lung and 22 from thyroid cancer. Paired pre/post data comprised 99 biopsy pairs from 79 patients; the paired-tissue subset comprised 76 pairs from 68 patients.
- Overall outcomes on the first SRI: median PFS 13.9 months (95% CI 10.1-16.6) and median time to treatment discontinuation (TTD) 17.3 months (14.0-20.2); the lung subset had PFS 13.0 and TTD 15.7 months (Figure 1, p18).
- On-target findings: secondary RET mutations appeared in 20/143 postprogression biopsies (14.0%): 15/121 lung (12.4%) and 5/22 thyroid (22.7%). Frequencies did not significantly differ by tissue versus plasma (12.1% vs 17.3%; `P=0.45`) or prior multikinase inhibitor exposure (15.2% vs 13.4%; `P=0.80`).
- Paired landscape: 72/99 pairs acquired an alteration. The mutually described categories were on-target only 2.0%, both on- and off-target 10.1%, off-target only 60.6%, and no acquired alteration 27.3% (Figure 2, p19).
- Off-target positive result: MET alteration was acquired in 18.2% of paired samples and MET amplification in 15.2%. In paired tissue, MET alteration frequency increased post-SRI versus pre-SRI overall (17.6% vs 2.0%; `P=0.022`) and in lung cancer (19.1% vs 2.1%; `P=0.022`; Figure 3, p20). Other recurrent genes did not show a statistically significant pre/post increase. Histologic transformation was found in 1/75 lung tissue samples and none of 13 thyroid samples.
- Outcome boundaries: post-SRI RET mutation presence was not associated with PFS (19.7 vs 13.8 months; `P=0.42`) or TTD (20.5 vs 16.8 months; `P=0.61`). MET amplification was not significantly associated with PFS (7.8 vs 14.8 months; `P=0.25`), although TTD was shorter (8.4 vs 19.0 months; `P=0.025`; Figure 5, pp23-24). The isolated TTD result does not independently prove causal resistance.
- Claim ceiling: the study maps candidate postprogression alterations. Referral/selection bias, heterogeneous assays and specimen types, unmatched biopsy sites, a small thyroid subset, and progression-conditioned sampling prevent causal or drug-specific resistance claims. Functional confirmation and comparative treatment evidence remain necessary.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results states that “no statistically significant difference was observed” for the RET-positive outcome comparison (p7); Discussion uses “highlights the need to explore” for future strategies rather than asserting efficacy (p10). The limitations paragraph identifies the retrospective design and nonuniform sampling (p10).
- Safe template: “In paired [specimen type] samples, [alteration] was more frequent after [exposure] than before it ([x%] vs [y%]; `P=[value]`), supporting it as a candidate resistance mechanism that requires functional validation.”
- Unsafe transfer: calling every acquired alteration a proven resistance driver, or interpreting shorter TTD as proof that the alteration caused early progression.

## PMID 41563386

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Camonsertib, an ATRi, in Combination with Low-Dose Gemcitabine in Solid Tumors with DNA Damage Response Aberrations: Preclinical and Phase Ib Results*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `publisher_typeset_pdf`.
- Registered PDF: `Rosen_Camonsertib_Low-Dose_Gemcitabine_DDR_PMID41563386.pdf`; SHA-256 `14c637b3c4955099d238af9ed4f300ff869c04c827cdf4c2912c7cd94e0eb75e`; 13 physical pages.

- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 pp4-5; Table 1 p6; Table 2 p7; Figure 2 p8; Table 3 p9; Figure 3 p10. All captions were readable.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: cell and xenograft combination experiments followed by the module-4 phase Ib TRESR dose/schedule study. Clinical objectives were safety, tolerability, dose/schedule selection, pharmacodynamics, and preliminary activity; no randomized comparator was used.
- Analysis sets: 76 patients with advanced DDR-altered solid tumors in the safety set; 63 DLT evaluable; 67 efficacy evaluable; 26 received the proposed expansion dose/schedule. Preclinical ATM- or BRCA1-deficient models are separate evidence layers.
- Preclinical result: low-dose gemcitabine plus camonsertib showed greater activity than either component in selected cell/xenograft models with tolerability in mice (Figure 1, pp4-5). This cannot be transferred as human efficacy or safety.
- Safety: every patient had a treatment-related adverse event; grade >=3 TRAEs occurred in 59/76 (77.6%). Frequent grade >=3 events were neutropenia 50.0%, anemia 31.6%, and thrombocytopenia 13.2% (Table 2, p7). DLTs occurred in 23/63 (36.5%), chiefly neutropenia (11/63, 17.5%); 7/26 (26.9%) had a DLT at the proposed expansion regimen.
- Schedule observation: dose reductions were less frequent with the 1-week-on/1-week-off schedule (2/28, 7.1%) than with the 21-day schedules (18/38, 47.4%), but schedules/doses were not randomized, so this is descriptive rather than comparative proof.
- Activity: combined RECIST/CA-125 ORR was 8/67 (11.9%); RECIST PR occurred in 7/66 (10.6%), six confirmed, and one additional CA-125 response occurred without target-lesion shrinkage. The composite clinical benefit rate was 30/67 (44.8%) and included prolonged stable disease; median PFS was 19 weeks. The gynecologic subset (n=40) had ORR 17.5%, CBR 50%, and median PFS 32.6 weeks; at the proposed expansion regimen, gynecologic ORR was 2/24 (8.3%).
- Biomarker/negative results: reversion-positive versus reversion-negative groups did not significantly differ in duration on treatment (1.5 vs 2.9 months; `P=0.24`) or PFS (4.0 vs 4.2 months; `P=0.099`).
- Source inconsistency: the molecular-response paragraph says “Among 35 patients” but reports 22/36 (61%). The 35/36 denominator is unresolved in the main PDF; that numerical rate is withheld from reuse. This disposition permits a main-PDF pass because the conflict is explicit and not needed for the central safety/activity conclusions.
- Claim ceiling: safety/tolerability and preliminary antitumor activity in a heterogeneous phase Ib population. The study cannot establish efficacy, superiority, synergy in patients, an independently validated DDR biomarker, or the contribution of each drug. Small biomarker groups, dose/schedule heterogeneity, incomplete molecular confirmation, and possible gemcitabine priming remain material limitations.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the abstract labels clinical activity “preliminary” (p1); the Results reports schedule tolerability descriptively (pp7-8); Discussion uses “despite the lack...” when linking an unresolved mechanism to observed activity (p10), not as proof.
- Safe template: “In this phase [I/Ib] dose-finding cohort, [regimen] showed [descriptive activity estimate] with [key grade >=3 toxicity]; these findings support further evaluation but do not establish comparative efficacy.”
- Unsafe transfer: “The combination was synergistic and effective in patients,” or reuse of the conflicted 22/36 molecular-response percentage.

## PMID 41587109

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *FDA Approval Summary: Tarlatamab for the Treatment of Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `author_manuscript`.
- Registered PDF: `Liu_FDA_Approval_Summary_Tarlatamab_for_the_Treatment_of_Extensive-Stage_Small_Cell_Lung_Cancer_PMID41587109.pdf`; SHA-256 `9b72e0427f0104845a88825ed201d6329d771c82dfa0590ab4c55797cc8f06c0`; 11 physical pages.

- Main displays visually checked: Table 1 p9; Table 2 p10; Table 3 p11. No main figures were present.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: FDA benefit-risk review supporting accelerated approval, centered on the open-label multicohort DeLLphi-301 study; it is not a randomized efficacy comparison.
- Primary efficacy set: 99 patients with relapsed/refractory extensive-stage SCLC after platinum-based chemotherapy and at least one additional prior line. BICR-confirmed ORR was 40% (95% CI 31%-51%): CR 2% and PR 38%. Median duration of response was 9.7 months (95% CI 6.9-not estimable); 68% of responders maintained response >=6 months and 40% >=12 months at 15.1 months' median follow-up (Table 2, p10).
- Platinum-sensitivity subgroups: ORR was 52% (95% CI 32%-71%) for resistant disease (n=27), 31% (18%-47%) for sensitive disease (n=42), and 43% (25%-63%) for unknown status (n=30). Overlapping CIs and lack of a randomized interaction preclude a true subgroup-difference claim.

Common adverse reactions included CRS 55%, fatigue 51%, pyrexia 36%, dysgeusia 36%, decreased appetite 34%, musculoskeletal pain 30%, constipation 30%, anemia 27%, and nausea 22%. Serious adverse events occurred in 58%, including CRS in 24%; fatal adverse events occurred in 2.7%, and 7% discontinued because of adverse reactions (Table 3, p11). The boxed warning addresses CRS and neurologic toxicity including ICANS.
- Dose/regulatory boundary: exposure-response and comparative descriptive data supported 1-to-10 mg over 1-to-100 mg. Approval was accelerated on ORR/DoR, with continued approval contingent on verification of clinical benefit in randomized DeLLphi-304. The indication extended beyond the studied efficacy population through FDA's totality-of-evidence reasoning.
- Claim ceiling: as of the article's regulatory snapshot, tarlatamab had substantial single-arm response durability with important acute toxicities. The paper does not verify survival benefit, comparative benefit, or current 2026 regulatory status; the latter requires a contemporaneous regulator check outside this source recheck.

## PMID 41779007

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Association of Blood Levels of Forever Plastics with Lung Cancer Mortality among Ever Smokers in the Prostate, Lung, Colorectal, and Ovarian (PLCO) Cohort Study*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `author_manuscript`.
- Registered PDF: `Irajizad_Association_of_Blood_Levels_of_Forever_Plastics_with_Lung_Cancer_Mortality_among_Ever_Smokers_PMID41779007.pdf`; SHA-256 `abfd3ae200ba390a738ca9f2caa7f7104c606eb223e908657bcebfb0909f2378`; 19 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`; cited supplemental analyses were not reviewed.
- Section map: abstract pp1-2; introduction pp2-3; Materials and Methods pp3-5; Results pp5-7; Discussion pp7-8; admin/references pp8-12; Translational Relevance p12; display pages pp13-19.
- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 p13; Figure 2 p14; Table 1 p15; Table 2 p16; Table 3 p17; Table 4 pp18-19. All captions and continued table pages were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: observational biomarker analysis nested in PLCO ever-smokers. Targeted serum assays covered 29 chemicals; five PFAS and three plasticizer metabolites were detected. The work measures selected circulating monomers/metabolites, not polymer particles or total microplastics.
- Analysis sets: 245 prediagnosis sera from 177 lung-cancer cases and 1,200 sera from 791 noncases. Mortality analyses were among cases. Repeated samples and the nested design must remain distinct from a conventional one-sample-per-person cohort.
- Incidence negative result: no individual detected analyte showed a statistically significant association with lung-cancer incidence (Table 2, p16).
- Individual mortality associations: highest versus lowest quartile PFHA was associated with cause-specific lung-cancer mortality HR 1.86 (95% CI 1.18-2.93) and subdistribution HR 1.838 (1.166-2.895). The PFOS isomer result was cause-specific HR 1.55 (1.025-2.344) and subdistribution HR 1.577 (1.043-2.383). Mono-iso-nonyl phthalate Q4 CIs crossed 1 (cause-specific HR 1.543, 0.972-2.449; subdistribution HR 1.534, 0.967-2.433), so the highest-quartile contrast is not statistically significant despite broad abstract wording (Table 3, p17).
- Composite signature: PFOS isomer, PFHA, and mono-iso-nonyl phthalate formed PFAP. The Cox-LASSO model was trained in five PLCO centers and evaluated with a fixed model in the other five centers, a geographic held-out internal evaluation rather than an external cohort. In the full cohort, per-SD cause-specific HR was 1.184 (1.058-1.325) and subdistribution HR 1.185 (1.070-1.313); Q4 versus Q1 HRs were 1.857 (1.179-2.925) and 1.821 (1.153-2.876), respectively (Table 3, p17; Figure 2, p14).
- Subgroups: several estimates were imprecise or nonsignificant. For example, the early-stage Q4 cause-specific HR was 2.597 with CI 0.926-7.286, whereas the heavy-smoker Q4 estimate was 1.922 (1.188-3.109) (Table 4, pp18-19). “Pronounced” patterns cannot replace CI-based interpretation.
- Source inconsistency: text/Table 1 state 158 lung-cancer deaths, while Tables 3-4 label `N=159`. The death denominator is unresolved and is withheld from reuse unless corrected against source data.
- Claim ceiling: selected circulating analytes and an internally held-out composite were associated with subsequent lung-cancer mortality among PLCO ever-smokers. Old stored specimens, residual confounding, repeated sampling, multiple analyte/subgroup testing, uncertain generalizability, and absence of true external validation preclude causal, exposure-intervention, or clinically validated prediction claims.

- Safe template: “Although [individual exposure/endpoint] was not associated with [incidence endpoint], higher [exposure/signature] was associated with [mortality endpoint] in this observational cohort ([HR], 95% CI [CI]); external validation and causal evaluation are required.”

## PMID 41805895

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Decoding B-cell Signatures of Complete Pathologic Response to Perioperative Chemoimmunotherapy in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `not_classified_by_header`.
- Registered PDF: `Sierra-Rodero_Decoding_B-cell_Signatures_of_Complete_Pathologic_Response_to_Perioperative_Chemoimmunoth_PMID41805895.pdf`; SHA-256 `3e2d608d146248fa068845698e53f2d8ceec92d920853cf6f1a19852ec1fa65d`; 14 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_recorded`; no separate supplement was treated as reviewed.
- Section map: abstract p1; introduction pp2-3; methods pp2-4; Results pp4-10; Discussion pp10-12; admin/references pp12-14.
- Main displays visually checked: Table 1 p4; Figure 1 p5; Figure 2 pp6-7; Figure 3 p7; Figure 4 p9; Figure 5 p11. Captions and continuation text were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: exploratory translational integration of BCR repertoire, multiplex immunofluorescence, bulk/spatial transcriptomics, and single-cell RNA-seq in samples from NADIM and NADIM II. The full analytic population was 123 (98 chemoimmunotherapy, 25 chemotherapy control), but each modality used a different subset: tissue BCR 87 patients, blood BCR 25, mIF 67, bulk RNA 15, spatial 12, and scRNA-seq 15 enrolled/13 after QC.
- Clinical context: CPR was observed in 45/98 (46%) chemoimmunotherapy patients versus 1/25 (4%) controls. PFS/OS favored the experimental group (`P<0.001`, HR 3.693, 95% CI 1.60-8.53; and `P=0.003`, HR 3.347, 1.02-11), but the HR reference direction is not stated in the caption and the comparison pools a single-arm NADIM cohort with randomized NADIM II data. Preserve the direction in words; do not transplant the HR orientation as a generic effect estimate.
- Established biomarkers: baseline PD-L1 was higher with CPR (`P=0.001`; ROC AUC 0.697), whereas TMB did not differ (`P=0.382`; AUC 0.595) (Figure 1, p5).
- Tissue BCR: clone count did not differ at baseline. CPR tumors had higher clonality (`P=0.030`) and Top-10 clonal space (`P=0.043`), with a nonsignificant lower-diversity trend (`P=0.069`). Shared clone space was greater with CPR at baseline (`P=0.020`) and surgery (`P=0.031`); the baseline Top-10 clone contribution at surgery did not differ (`P=0.503`), while preexisting clones occupied more of the postsurgery Top-10 space in CPR (`P=0.025`; Figure 2, pp6-7).
- Blood BCR: global clone count, diversity, and Top-10 space did not differ; CPR was associated with lower blood clonality at baseline (`P=0.013`) and 6 months (`P=0.001`). Class-switched baseline clones were proportionally enriched, more diverse, and less clonal in CPR, with reported AUCs above 0.75 for selected isotype metrics. These are same-cohort ROC estimates, not independent validation.
- TLS/spatial results: chemoimmunotherapy was associated with greater postsurgery TLS density than chemotherapy (`P=0.034`), including among NCPR tumors (`P=0.012`), but TLS density did not differ between CPR and NCPR within the experimental arm (`P=0.129`; Figure 3, p7). Spatial/GSEA and deconvolution findings in only 4 CPR and 8 NCPR specimens implicated more mature antigen-presentation and adaptive-immune programs, but inferred cell abundance is not direct cell counting or mechanism.
- Single-cell result: 13 paired patients (6 CPR, 7 NCPR) remained after QC. B-cell subset proportions did not significantly differ; class-switched memory B cells showed only a trend (`P=0.101`), while pathway enrichments differed by response/timepoint (Figure 5, p11).
- Source inconsistency: Methods/Results report 11,053 B cells after reprocessing, whereas the Figure 5 caption reports 12,651 B cells. The exact B-cell count is withheld pending source clarification; patient counts and qualitative pathway results remain usable with their exploratory ceiling.
- Claim ceiling: multiomic features are response-associated and hypothesis-generating candidate biomarkers. ### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: retrospective breast- and lung-cancer brain-metastasis (BM) cohorts establish prognostic immune correlates; an exploratory tissue endpoint from an ongoing randomized phase III preoperative versus postoperative SRS/SRT trial evaluates radiation-associated molecular changes. These are distinct evidence layers.
- Retrospective sets: 153 breast BM and 153 lung BM specimens had blinded semiquantitative TIL grading. Breast TCR-seq began with 118 samples and retained 84 after downsampling; the multivariable complete-case TCR model used 72. A separate previously generated lung BM TCR set contained 39 samples.
- TIL prognosis: high versus low TIL separated unadjusted postcraniotomy OS in breast (`P=0.0369`) and lung BM (`P=0.0159`; Figure 1, p4). After adjustment, breast TIL high versus low was not significant (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.334-1.08; Dunnett `P=0.16`), whereas lung TIL remained associated (HR 0.54, 0.341-0.85; Dunnett `P=0.014`; Tables 3-4, pp7-8).
- TCR prognosis: in breast BM, high versus low TCR diversity was associated with postcraniotomy OS in the multivariable model (HR 0.25, 0.115-0.54; unadjusted `P=0.0005`, Dunnett-adjusted `P=0.0009`; Table 3, p7). TCR density was not associated with OS. In the 39-sample lung set, the high/intermediate-versus-low survival signal was test-dependent (Mantel-Cox `P=0.0445`; Gehan-Breslow-Wilcoxon `P=0.1550`; Figure 3, p6), so it is not a clean independent validation.
- Trial tissue sets: 88 samples were analyzed (41 preoperative/irradiated, 47 postoperative/unirradiated); RNA-seq retained 86 after QC. TCR after downsampling used 35 per group. This is an exploratory molecular endpoint with unpaired tissue, not the trial's deferred primary/secondary clinical endpoint.
- Radiation-associated result: TCR diversity was higher in preoperative than postoperative samples (`P=0.0043`), while clonality and density were not different; histology-specific trends were nonsignificant (Figure 5, p10). GSEA showed enrichment of T-cell, myeloid, and antigen-processing/presentation pathways; MCP-counter did not show broad abundance differences, with a monocytic-lineage signal (`P=0.0253`) (Figure 4, p9).

Treat Figure 5A as the overall trial tissue comparison; withhold the breast-only label. The text also repeats “diversity” where one of the three repertoire metrics appears to be density; no result is reconstructed from that typo.

Retrospective prognostic cohorts, missing matched primary/blood/normal controls, cross-sectional pre/post arms, selection into tissue analysis, and correlative pathways prevent causal survival, mechanism, or ICI-predictive claims.
