## PMID 42485106

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Utilizing machine learning to identify multimodal signatures for patients who would benefit from the addition of tremelimumab to durvalumab and chemotherapy (TRIDENT)*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `not_classified_by_header`.
- Registered PDF: `Skoulidis_Utilizing_machine_learning_to_identify_multimodal_signatures_for_patients_who_would_benefit_f_PMID42485106.pdf`; SHA-256 `4844b3589ded1523689287e2216b87d50240e0ff44a9581c55c6e6972dfdffee`; 13 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_recorded`; no separate supplement was treated as reviewed.
- Section map: abstract/introduction pp1-2; methods pp2-6; Results pp6-8; Discussion pp8-10; data/admin/references pp10-13; Translational Relevance p2.
- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 p3; Figure 2 p4; Table 1 p6; Table 2 p8; Figure 3 p9. All legends and table footnotes were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: post hoc machine-learning analysis of heterogeneous treatment effects in the randomized phase III POSEIDON trial. TRIDENT included 974 participants across all three POSEIDON arms, but the treatment-effect models compared only tremelimumab plus durvalumab plus chemotherapy with durvalumab plus chemotherapy; they did not estimate the effect versus chemotherapy alone.
- Analysis sets: the two-arm clinical set included 652 patients (323 versus 329); radiomics was available for 616, genomics for 557, and all three modalities for 526. The genomics analysis contained 212 squamous and 345 nonsquamous tumors. These missing-data-defined sets must not be interchanged.
- Method chain: the unobservable individual treatment effect was operationalized as a conditional average treatment effect, defined as the predicted between-regimen difference in 24-month restricted mean survival time. A new “super T-learner” used repeated nested cross-validation (3-fold outer and 5-fold inner loops, each repeated five times), with Bayesian model averaging across modalities. This is internal validation; there was no independent external test cohort.
- Overall and clinical-only findings: in the full clinical set, the randomized-arm OS HR was 0.89 (95% CI 0.75-1.06). Within the model-ranked top 50% and top 30%, clinical-only HRs were 0.76 (0.51-1.13; `P=0.18`) and 0.73 (0.44-1.22; `P=0.23`), respectively. These are negative subgroup results, not evidence of benefit enrichment.
- Modality findings: clinical plus radiomics yielded an HR of 0.75 (0.58-0.98; `P=0.04`) in the top 50% but 0.75 (0.55-1.02; `P=0.07`) in the top 30%; radiomics alone was positive in the top 50% (HR 0.79, 0.66-0.95; `P=0.01`) but not the top 30%. Clinical plus genomics yielded HRs of 0.75 (0.57-0.97; `P=0.03`) and 0.63 (0.44-0.91; `P=0.01`) in the top 50% and 30%, despite an overall-set HR of 0.96 (0.79-1.16).
- Important negative result: adding all three modalities did not improve performance. In the 526-patient tri-modal set, top-50% and top-30% HRs were 0.80 (0.55-1.16; `P=0.24`) and 0.73 (0.41-1.31; `P=0.29`). Thus “more modalities” did not translate into a significant enriched subgroup.
- Histology boundary: no predictive subgroup was demonstrated in squamous disease; for clinical plus genomics, top-50% and top-30% HRs were 0.99 (0.57-1.74; `P=0.97`) and 0.93 (0.53-1.61; `P=0.80`). In nonsquamous disease, corresponding estimates were 0.56 (0.33-0.97; `P=0.04`) and 0.48 (0.24-0.95; `P=0.04`), with an overall nonsquamous HR of 0.88 (0.68-1.12). These post hoc internally selected results are hypothesis-generating, not a validated histology-specific treatment rule.
- Feature interpretation: EGFR, FGFR3, CDKN2A, KRAS, and STK11 ranked among influential genomic features in one selected model. Importance in a multivariable learner does not establish an individual gene-by-treatment interaction, causal biology, or utility of any single alteration; variant-level effects and co-mutations were not evaluated. Trial-stratification subgroups such as stage, histology, and PD-L1 did not show significant interaction signals, whereas higher tumor burden appeared associated with a larger estimated effect in an exploratory forest plot.
- Safety was not analyzed. Models used baseline features and excluded treatment-emergent toxicity, so the signature cannot be used to infer net clinical benefit or tolerability.
- Claim ceiling: an internally cross-validated, post hoc multimodal signature may enrich for differential benefit of adding tremelimumab to durvalumab plus chemotherapy, most clearly in the clinical-plus-genomic nonsquamous analysis. It is not an externally validated predictive biomarker or a basis for treatment selection. Missing modality data, gene-level coding, limited sample sizes, linear handling of PD-L1, trial-population selection, numerous model/subgroup comparisons, and lack of independent validation materially constrain transfer.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: Results/Discussion notes that adding genomic data improved estimated predictive performance but immediately cautions that the smaller analysis set limits interpretation (p8). The Discussion characterizes reported performance as an almost-unbiased internally validated estimate rather than clinical predictive accuracy (p10).
- Safe template: “Although [multimodal model] identified a subgroup with a more favorable [treatment-effect estimate] ([estimate], 95% CI [CI]), performance was derived from internal cross-validation and requires external validation before clinical use.”
- Unsafe transfer: claiming a validated personalized-treatment tool; attributing benefit to EGFR, FGFR3, CDKN2A, KRAS, or STK11 individually; stating that tri-modal integration outperformed the smaller modality combinations; or generalizing the comparison to chemotherapy alone.

## PMID 42507545

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *The genomic landscape and treatment outcomes associated with CDKN2A/MTAP loss in patients with non-small cell lung cancer*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `not_classified_by_header`.
- Registered PDF: `Vokes_The_genomic_landscape_and_treatment_outcomes_associated_with_CDKN2A_MTAP_loss_in_patients_with_no_PMID42507545.pdf`; SHA-256 `54b667455f7662b67a11643f6e741a7cdab3dac36965a549f956c578e89ddf7b`; 14 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_recorded`; no separate supplement was treated as reviewed.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: retrospective, real-world Tempus Lens analysis of concordant CDKN2A/MTAP copy loss (`CMdel`) in NSCLC, integrating paired tissue DNA and RNA profiling, inferred immune features, first-line treatment outcomes, and exploratory external comparison with MYSTIC trial data. It is observational and cannot separate prognostic from predictive effects without treatment interaction testing.
- Cohorts and molecular definition: 27,204 records entered initial screening. After assay, purity, and concordance filters, the primary analytic cohort included 16,947 tumors, of which 1,991 (11.7%) were `CMdel`; a temporally distinct validation cohort included 10,996 tumors, of which 1,676 (15%) were `CMdel`. Copy number zero at both loci defined the main group; CDKN2A/MTAP-discordant cases were excluded from the primary analysis. The validation cohort supported genomic patterns but was too immature to validate treatment outcomes.
- Outcome sets/methods: 6,951 patients had documented first-line therapy, 4,588 had physician-curated best overall response, and 4,779 met the risk-set-adjusted time-to-next-treatment (TTNT) analysis rules. TTNT is a real-world surrogate, not protocol RECIST PFS. Analyses excluded treatment starts more than three months before sequencing and were truncated at 18 months. ICI analyses excluded major targetable drivers but retained several other oncogenic groups; best response was not centrally reviewed.
- Genomic/immune findings: `CMdel` tumors showed lower PD-L1, lower TMB, and lower inferred immune-cell/signature measures. More than half carried a co-occurring actionable alteration, with enrichment of EGFR, ALK, and RET and depletion of KRAS in the primary cohort. The temporal validation was broadly concordant but did not reproduce the negative KRAS enrichment. These are cohort-level associations, not evidence that the deletion directly causes an immune-cold state.
- Treatment results: unadjusted TTNT did not differ for ICI monotherapy (`P=0.818`) or targeted therapy (`P=0.578`), but was shorter for `CMdel` with ICI plus chemotherapy (`P=0.010`) and chemotherapy (`P=0.019`). After multivariable adjustment, the ICI-plus-chemotherapy association attenuated and was no longer significant (`P=0.075`), whereas the chemotherapy association remained (`P=0.043`). Physician-curated response comparisons were nonsignificant across ICI monotherapy (`P=0.068`), ICI plus chemotherapy (`P=0.078`), chemotherapy (`P=0.5`), and targeted therapy (`P=0.7`).
- IFNK stratification was exploratory and thresholded at the 25th percentile of `CMwt`. The shortest TTNT estimates included `CMwt`/IFNK-low under ICI monotherapy (HR 1.43, 95% CI 1.03-1.98; `P=0.032`) and `CMdel`/IFNK-low under ICI plus chemotherapy (HR 1.89, 1.33-2.69; `P<0.001`). Under chemotherapy, `CMdel`/IFNK-high was significant (HR 1.62, 1.04-2.52; `P=0.034`) but `CMdel`/IFNK-low was not (HR 1.59, 0.95-2.68; `P=0.079`); no targeted-therapy stratum was significant. These within-treatment associations do not establish a predictive interaction.
- Deletion architecture: broad 9p21 loss involving MLLT3 occurred in 26.5%; the ICI association appeared concentrated in focal rather than broad loss, and CDKN2A-only discordant cases had intermediate TTNT. These post hoc subdivisions are candidates for future validation.
- Source inconsistency with direct disposition: the Results text and rendered Figure 4F show worse OS with complete 9p21 loss under durvalumab alone (HR 2.20, 95% CI 1.33-3.62), but not durvalumab plus tremelimumab (HR 1.13, 0.70-1.83) or standard of care (HR 1.31, 0.83-2.06). A later Discussion sentence says the adverse association occurred with both durvalumab and the combination. The combination claim is withheld; the result is reported according to the Results section and rendered figure. A second wording inconsistency describes never-smoker enrichment in the primary cohort but “smoking history” enrichment in validation; smoking-direction claims must remain cohort-specific.
- Claim ceiling: `CMdel` marks a distinct genomic and immune-associated NSCLC subgroup and may carry adverse prognostic associations in selected treatment settings. The data do not establish causal immune resistance, a treatment-selection biomarker, or efficacy of PRMT5/MAT2A-directed therapy. Residual confounding, low-purity exclusion, proprietary assay/inference methods, multiple exploratory subgroup analyses, TTNT limitations, and lack of mature external outcome validation remain material.

### Language and transfer

- Safe template: “Although [unadjusted outcome] was shorter in [biomarker group] (`P=[value]`), the association attenuated after covariate adjustment (`P=[value]`), supporting a prognostic hypothesis rather than a validated treatment-predictive effect.”
- Unsafe transfer: claiming that CDKN2A/MTAP loss causes ICI resistance, predicts benefit from or failure of a specific regimen, or supports targeted-drug efficacy; repeating the contradicted durvalumab-plus-tremelimumab statement.

## PMID 41945500

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Retreatment with First-Generation Selective RET Inhibitors in RET-Rearranged NSCLC Pretreated with Selpercatinib or Pralsetinib: Results from the RET-MAP Registry*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `not_classified_by_header`.
- Registered PDF: `Marinello_RET_Inhibitor_Retreatment_RET-MAP_PMID41945500.pdf`; SHA-256 `5a85b85cd6b5dcb8ba5a9f1e4e4b8f3940d2b69e4273f1ad79ebd6fb64462df0`; 19 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`; no external eTables/eFigures were treated as reviewed.

- Main displays visually checked: Table 1 p16; Figure 1 p18; Figure 2 p19; the standalone legend page p17 was checked against both figures.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: retrospective 48-institution RET-MAP registry analysis of first-generation selective RET-inhibitor retreatment after selpercatinib or pralsetinib discontinuation. Of 411 initially treated patients, only 41 (10%) underwent retreatment, creating strong clinician/patient-selection and survivorship constraints. Fourteen stopped the first inhibitor for toxicity and 27 for progression; 14/41 received an intercurrent therapy, most commonly chemotherapy (11/14).
- Endpoint methods: objective response was investigator assessed “in reference to RECIST principles,” without central review; PFS and time to treatment failure were measured from retreatment. Missing values were not imputed. No randomized comparator or formal powered hypothesis test was present.
- Context for first-drug discontinuation: toxicity-related discontinuation occurred in 21/284 (7%) selpercatinib-treated and 19/102 (19%) pralsetinib-treated patients. Progression occurred in 145/284 (51%) and 58/102 (57%), respectively. Isolated CNS progression was recorded in 14/145 (10%) versus 13/58 (22%; `P=0.021`), but this nonrandomized drug comparison cannot establish differential CNS efficacy.
- Switch after toxicity (`n=14`): all received the alternate first-generation inhibitor. Reported ORR was 67%, median PFS 9.9 months, 6-month PFS 80.3%, and median TTF 13 months. Adverse events recurred in 9/14 (64%), dose reduction was required in 4/14 (28%), and grade ≥3 events occurred in 3/14 (21%); all 14 had restarted at full dose. “Feasible” therefore does not mean toxicity-free, and the authors’ dose-adjustment recommendation is based on a selected retrospective series.
- Monotherapy after progression (`n=13`): ORR was 23%, median PFS 7 months, 6-month PFS 61.5%, and median TTF 8 months. Among seven longer-PFS cases, clinical narratives included CNS-only or limited systemic progression and prior dose reduction; six switched to selpercatinib, and none had a documented on-target RET resistance mutation. These observations identify selected cases, not a validated retreatment rule.
- Combination after progression (`n=14`; 11 targeted combinations and 3 chemotherapy combinations): the all-combination abstract/Figure 2 values were ORR 39%, median PFS 4 months, 6-month PFS 27.3%, and median TTF 4 months. The targeted-combination subset had an ORR of 36%. These denominators must remain distinct. Six of 14 had grade 2-3 adverse events, and only three individual cases had prolonged benefit.
- Resistance finding: 9/11 targeted combinations addressed MET amplification. Exploratorily, MET-amplified patients had shorter first-inhibitor PFS than patients with other resistance mechanisms (6.4 versus 15.6 months; `P=0.015`). This progression-selected comparison is confounded and supports only a candidate resistance/aggressive-biology hypothesis.
- Claim ceiling: switching agents after toxicity showed activity in a small selected cohort, while rechallenge after progression had limited overall activity with occasional longer benefit. The study cannot establish standard-of-care sequencing, causal CNS differences, or which clinical/molecular subgroup benefits. Small subgroups, heterogeneous practice/imaging, lack of central response review, intercurrent therapy, absent imputation, and selection for indolent/oligoprogressive cases restrict transfer.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Discussion states that findings should be interpreted cautiously because of selection bias (p12), and the conclusion describes postprogression efficacy as limited overall while allowing only a selected-patient possibility (p13).
- Safe template: “In a selected retrospective cohort, [retreatment strategy] yielded [response/time-to-event estimate], but the small, clinician-selected population and absence of a comparator preclude a standard sequencing recommendation.”
- Unsafe transfer: recommending same-class retreatment as standard care, treating isolated-CNS progression rates as causal drug superiority, or merging the all-combination ORR with the targeted-combination denominator.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: single-center, single-arm phase II study in treatment-naïve advanced NSCLC with PD-L1 TPS ≥1%, no EGFR/ALK/ROS1 alteration, and at least one metastatic lesion suitable for stereotactic ablative radiotherapy (SBRT). One to ten lesions could be irradiated; atezolizumab plus tiragolumab began within seven days after SBRT. Without a control arm, the study cannot isolate the contribution of SBRT, tiragolumab, or atezolizumab.
- Analysis set: 53 patients were assessed, 45 enrolled, and 41 treated/analyzed; all were Asian. Table 1 gives PD-L1 TPS 1%-49% in 25/41 (61%) and ≥50% in 16/41 (39%). The Results text instead calls the lower group 25 (56%); 56% is withheld and the rendered table denominator is retained.
- Baseline source conflict: Table 1 shows one, two, three, and four metastatic sites in 16 (39%), 16 (39%), 4 (10%), and 5 (12%) patients. The adjacent text says all patients had more than one metastatic site. That sentence is contradicted by the rendered table and is withheld.
- Endpoint-definition alert: the abstract, endpoint description, Results, and Figure 1 report PFS from SBRT initiation, whereas one statistical-analysis sentence defines it from the start of atezolizumab plus tiragolumab. The reported estimate is retained as “from SBRT initiation,” with the internal timing inconsistency disclosed; it must not be silently harmonized.
- Efficacy: at 9.8 months median follow-up, median PFS from SBRT was 9.3 months (95% CI 6.0-not reached); 18 deaths had occurred and OS was immature. In an exploratory PD-L1 subgroup, median PFS was 11.4 versus 6.4 months (HR 0.39, 95% CI 0.16-0.95; `P=0.029`) and OS was not reached versus 9.2 months (HR 0.22, 0.09-0.55; `P=0.007`) for TPS ≥50% versus 1%-49%. The small post hoc subgroup and lack of treatment interaction prevent a predictive claim.

- Safety: SBRT-related adverse events occurred in 12/41 (29%), most often esophagitis (9), with no grade 3 SBRT event. Atezolizumab/tiragolumab-related adverse events occurred in 29/41 (71%); common events included urticaria (19), rash (8), ALT increase (8), and AST increase (7). Two pneumonitis events included one grade 3 case; five patients interrupted treatment and two discontinued. No grade 4 or 5 treatment-related event was shown.
- Safety-table source alert: Table 2 reports one any-grade case each of dry mouth, dyspepsia, and decreased platelet count, but all listed grade columns are zero. Any-grade occurrence can be reported; the precise grade is withheld.
- Omics/method boundary: single-cell RNA-seq involved only five patients and eight tissue specimens—three paired patients plus two single-timepoint patients, with samples from different anatomical sites. Cell-state, pseudotime, and CellChat results suggested CD8 TPEX, Treg, NECTIN/TIGIT, ICAM, and galectin changes, but are exploratory inferred relationships, vulnerable to tiny-patient and cell-level pseudoreplication. Figure 2’s LUAD `n=4` and LUSC `n=4` refer to specimens, not eight independent patients.
- Claim ceiling: the regimen showed descriptive activity and manageable but nontrivial toxicity in a small single-arm selected cohort. It does not prove that SBRT improves PFS, that triple therapy is superior to immunotherapy, or that the single-cell programs mediate benefit. Cross-trial comparisons in Discussion are explicitly noncomparative. The Translational Relevance assertion of improved PFS and a conclusion that omits tiragolumab exceed or inconsistently describe the design and should not be transferred.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

In addition, Figure 1B labels pancreatic and urothelial cancer frequencies using the full NGS-set denominators, while its caption/body report slightly different frequencies/counts apparently derived from the outcome-analytic subset. This is a prognostic association, not causal evidence.

For biliary tract cancer, the Results text and forest plot give HR 2.58 (1.08-6.16; `P=0.03`), whereas the Figure 4 caption says 2.56. The plot/text value is retained and the caption discrepancy is disclosed; precision should not be overstated.
- ICB outcome: among 243 ICB-treated patients (21 loss, 222 wild type), median PFS was 3.6 versus 5.6 months (HR 1.69, 1.06-2.70; `P=0.025`). When restricted to primary-tumor specimens, the difference attenuated to 3.6 versus 4.2 months (HR 1.50, 0.86-2.63; `P=0.147`). This negative sensitivity analysis is necessary context. A melanoma subgroup contained only five loss cases versus 42 wild type (unadjusted HR 4.05, 1.28-12.81; `P=0.01`; adjusted HR 5.08, 1.39-18.58; `P=0.014`) and is highly uncertain.
- Transcriptomic findings: xCell analysis was pooled across urothelial, gastric, and breast cancer because individual tumor samples were limited. Plots show lower inferred CD4 T-cell, CD8 T-cell, and M2-macrophage scores with exact `P=0.011`, `0.023`, and `0.025`; text/caption instead round or alter these to `0.05`, `0.02`, and `0.02`. The qualitative direction is retained, but conflicting exact values—especially CD4—are not copied without a figure-specific qualifier. Regulatory T cells, dendritic cells, B cells, monocytes, M1 macrophages, and NK cells did not differ; B cells were only a trend (`P=0.06`).

MTAP-loss tumors were enriched for cell-cycle and RNA/DNA-repair programs, whereas wild type showed immune/metabolic, B-cell-receptor, type-I-interferon, and 9p21-associated programs. Bulk RNA deconvolution and gene-set enrichment do not directly measure cell number/function or establish an MTAP-driven mechanism; pan-cancer composition remains a confounder.
- Claim ceiling: MTAP loss was associated with adverse prognosis, shorter ICB PFS in the overall observational set, and distinct bulk-transcriptomic programs. The primary-specimen sensitivity was nonsignificant, tumor-specific samples were small, and no treatment interaction or experiment established ICB resistance. Retrospective outcome analysis, tumor mixing, specimen origin, absent MTAP-protein validation, lack of functional experiments, and immature cohort-B outcomes constrain transfer.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: prospective, single-center, single-arm phase II trial in China of tislelizumab 200 mg every three weeks plus anlotinib 10 mg on days 1-14 as first-line treatment for EGFR/ALK-negative advanced pulmonary sarcomatoid carcinoma. A Simon two-stage design tested a null ORR of 21.9% against a target of 45% (`alpha=0.05`, `beta=0.20`), with 29 planned after dropout allowance; at least four responses among the first 17 were required to continue, and nine were observed.
- Analysis set: all 29 treated patients were efficacy- and safety-evaluable; 23 had pleomorphic carcinoma and six carcinosarcoma, four had stage III and 25 stage IV disease, 17 had PD-L1 TPS ≥50%, and only four were PD-L1 negative. Sparse subgroups limit biomarker interpretation.
- Efficacy: best response was CR in 2, PR in 14, SD in 12, and PD in one. ORR was 55.17% (95% CI 35.69%-73.55%) and disease-control rate 96.55% (82.24%-99.91%); median duration of response was 9.80 months (2.30-13.53). At 16.73 months median follow-up, median PFS was 9.40 months (3.67-13.30) and median OS 14.37 months (9.03-17.50). Twenty-three had progressed, 17 died from cancer, one died from venous thromboembolism, and five remained on treatment.
- Subgroups: post hoc/exploratory analyses did not detect significant outcome differences across examined strata (all `P>0.05`). Small groups such as ECOG 2 and PD-L1-negative each contained four patients, so nonsignificance does not show equivalence or absence of heterogeneity.
- Safety: all 29 were analyzed. Eight (27.59%) had grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events: myocarditis (1), myositis (1), hepatitis (2), pneumonitis (2), hypophysitis (1), and rash (1). Three of these eight discontinued; the other five resolved to grade ≤1. A separate grade 2 myocarditis event also led to discontinuation, and one patient required anlotinib dose reduction. No treatment-related death occurred. Table 3’s common TEAEs (including anemia in 48.28%) must not be relabeled as treatment-related; Table 4’s most frequent TRAEs included hyperuricemia and rash (17.24% each).
- Claim ceiling: the combination met its prespecified single-arm activity target and had a characterized toxicity profile in 29 patients, supporting further comparative testing. It does not establish superiority to chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or either component; synergy and component contribution are unidentifiable. Historical/external comparisons in Discussion do not replace a control arm, and “no new safety signal” remains a descriptive judgment in a small cohort.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: pooled report of three open-label phase I dose-escalation studies of the SOS1 inhibitor BI 1701963: monotherapy in the United States/Europe, Japan, and China, and combination with trametinib in NCT04111458. Ninety-four heavily pretreated patients with heterogeneous KRAS-mutant solid tumors were treated—75 with monotherapy (50-800 mg) and 19 with combination therapy. Only 8 and 3 patients, respectively, had NSCLC.
- Dose method/analysis: escalation used Bayesian logistic regression with overdose control. MTD was the highest dose with ≤25% posterior risk that the true DLT rate was ≥33% in cycle 1. Monotherapy DLTs occurred in 6/75 (8%); combination DLTs in 6/19 (31.6%). The 800-mg monotherapy MTD was determined specifically within NCT04111458 from two DLTs among nine patients; it must not be generalized across the separate Asian studies. Combination MTD was declared at the first investigated level, BI 1701963 100 mg plus trametinib 1 mg.
- Critical safety result: three monotherapy patients in the Japanese/Chinese studies developed investigator-attributed drug-related ILD during cycle 1 and died (one at 600 mg, two at 800 mg). Available exposure data were higher on average in the Asian studies, but the three cases did not permit a dose/exposure-risk conclusion; BI 1701963 causation or contribution could not be excluded. The two Asian monotherapy programs were discontinued, as was further clinical development of BI 1701963.
- Broader safety: every monotherapy patient had an adverse event; grade 3, 4, and 5 any-cause events occurred in 31/75 (41.3%), 3/75 (4.0%), and 16/75 (21.3%). Drug-related events occurred in 60/75 (80%), including 19 grade 3, one grade 4, and the three fatal ILD cases; 17/75 (22.7%) discontinued because of an event. All 19 combination patients had drug-related events, including 11 grade 3 and one grade 4; 8/19 (42.1%) discontinued for an event. Two combination patients had fatal any-cause events, neither assessed as drug-related.
- Efficacy denominators: response percentages used only patients with postbaseline assessment, not all treated. Among 55/75 evaluable monotherapy patients, one had a confirmed PR (1.8%), 12 SD (21.8%), and 38 PD (69.1%); disease control was 23.6%. Among 12/19 evaluable combination patients, none responded, four had SD (33.3%), and eight PD. One combination patient remained treated through day 319, an individual observation rather than proof of durable efficacy.
- Pharmacology/omics: exposure rose approximately dose-proportionally through 600 mg and more than proportionally at 800 mg, with moderate-to-high interpatient variation and higher average Asian-study exposure. Tumor Ki-67 and pERK H-scores generally changed only slightly by day 16. The authors interpret this as little target engagement, but note that low baseline Ki-67 and pERK signal decay during biopsy retrieval could obscure a signal. No exposure-response or KRAS-mutation/activity correlation was seen.
- Interpretation boundary: the paper discusses a possible pharmacologic “synergistic effect” of combined MEK/SOS1 pathway inhibition based on the adverse-event profile, but there were no objective responses and the highest explored 200 mg/1 mg combination dose was below the predicted active dose. Clinical synergy was not demonstrated. The authors explicitly state that high discontinuation and sparse exposure prevent firm efficacy conclusions and that development was prematurely stopped.
- Claim ceiling: the studies characterize dose-limiting toxicity, pharmacokinetics, limited pharmacodynamic modulation, and very limited antitumor activity. The 800-mg MTD is study-specific and cannot outweigh the fatal ILD seen elsewhere; no RP2D, clinical target validation, synergistic efficacy, NSCLC-specific activity, or SOS1-class failure can be inferred from this heterogeneous early-phase program.
