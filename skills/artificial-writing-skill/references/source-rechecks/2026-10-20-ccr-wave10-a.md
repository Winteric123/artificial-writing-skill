## PMID 38526394 — Measurement of ctDNA Tumor Fraction Identifies Informative Negative Liquid Biopsy Results and Informs Value of Tissue Confirmation

### Identity and coverage

- Year/version/pages: 2024; publisher typeset PDF; 9 physical pages.
- Registered PDF: Rolfo_Measurement_of_ctDNA_Tumor_Fraction_Identifies_Informative_Negative_Liquid_Biopsy_Results_and_Inf_PMID38526394.pdf.

There were no main tables. Every panel, caption, denominator label, and outcome curve was checked.

### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: retrospective observational analysis of paired Foundation Medicine tissue and liquid comprehensive genomic profiling, followed by a separate Flatiron Health–Foundation Medicine advanced-NSCLC cohort. It asks whether an assay-derived circulating tumor DNA tumor fraction can distinguish an informative negative liquid biopsy from a result that should trigger tissue confirmation.
- Analysis sets: the paired cohort contained 3,854 patients (NSCLC 1,753; breast 835; colorectal 859; pancreas 407), with a median 357-day interval between samples. The clinical NSCLC flow was 6,810 patients, 2,265 tested before first-line treatment, 1,355 with a negative liquid biopsy, 505 with subsequent tissue profiling, 474 in the outcome cohort, and only 80 with a reportable tumor fraction (56 below 1% and 24 at least 1%). These sets must not be merged.
- Method chain: both assays used hybrid-capture panels; liquid tumor fraction combined aneuploidy, somatic-variant, fragmentomic, and clonal-hematopoiesis filtering signals. The 1% threshold and performance estimates are assay-specific and not reducible to the highest variant allele fraction.

- Negative/uncertain findings: tumor fraction was unavailable in most of the 505-patient tissue-confirmation cohort; 0/24 does not establish zero risk; copy-number performance at low tumor fraction remains insufficiently calibrated; the outcome analysis lacks a randomized comparator; and biopsy burden, delay, complications, and downstream safety were not quantified.
- Safety findings: this was a diagnostic-pathway study, not a therapeutic safety study. It did not measure biopsy complications, harm from delayed treatment, harm from a missed actionable alteration, or comparative safety of treatment pathways.
- Source alerts: (1) Results calls the paired analysis the “five most prevalent diseases” but lists four; (2) high-tumor-fraction PPA is 98% in the abstract and 97.3% in Results, consistent with rounding but not silently interchangeable; (3) Figure 5A’s caption says 41 matched-therapy recipients whereas the bar and prose give 43; (4) Figure 5B’s caption says 63% versus 27%, whereas bars and prose give 53% versus 18%; (5) Figure 5C’s caption says 24.2-month real-world PFS, whereas curve and prose give 15.9 months; (6) Figure 5A’s “other therapy” bar is printed as 43 (17%) although the complementary count should be 211 (83%); (7) the abstract’s 505-patient phrasing can be read as though all tissue-positive patients had known tumor fraction, but only 80 had tumor-fraction data; (8) the outcome cohort of 474 must not be relabeled as the low-tumor-fraction cohort; (9) ERBB2 tissue findings extend beyond the seven-gene initial negative definition, so they do not necessarily represent analytic liquid-biopsy false negatives; (10) the paper’s Bradford Hill/causal language exceeds the observational design; (11) supplements were not supplied.
- Limitations: retrospective and nonrandomized sampling; long and variable intertest interval; evolving tumor genotype; selected patients undergoing both assays; incomplete tumor-fraction availability; vendor-specific assays and threshold; alteration-class heterogeneity; low high-tumor-fraction clinical denominator; no direct biopsy-harm evaluation; residual confounding in treatment timing/outcomes; sponsor involvement and intellectual-property interests.
- Claim ceiling: on the evaluated Foundation Medicine platform, reported tumor fraction helped stratify the informativeness of a negative liquid biopsy, and low/unknown tumor fraction supported timely tissue confirmation. The data do not prove causal benefit of the workflow, universalize a 1% cutoff, eliminate tissue confirmation in every high-tumor-fraction case, validate copy-number exclusion at low tumor fraction, or quantify patient-level safety.

Early cfDNA decline and MAPK-transcript suppression were exploratory response-associated measures. The trial does not establish superiority, formal synergy, a validated ctDNA surrogate, benefit in NSCLC/colorectal/pancreatic cancer, or the independent contribution of either drug.
