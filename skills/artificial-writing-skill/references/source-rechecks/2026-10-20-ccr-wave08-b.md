- Year/version/pages: 2025; publisher main PDF; 17 physical pages.
- Registered PDF: Michel_LINE1_hypomethylation_multicancer_PMID39620930.pdf.
- SHA-256: 49f763c630f8436daf29b1356d72682f010c4be033d55e6e1487ca32d457e6ec.

- Main visuals checked: Figure 1, assay design and LINE-1 coverage, p4; Figure 2, methylation in controls, tissues, cell lines, and discovery plasma groups, p9; Figure 3, discovery-set classifiers and stage models, p10; Figure 4, cohort composition and independent validation, p12; Figure 5, stage, CNA, methylation–CNA relation, and two-step classification, p14. No main table was present. Every visible panel, legend, denominator, threshold, AUC, sensitivity, specificity, and P value was checked.

### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: retrospective, multi-source case–control assay-development study of a targeted bisulfite-sequencing method (DIAMOND) for LINE-1 methylation and CNA signals in plasma. Discovery cohort C1 comprised 350 cancers and 123 healthy donors (N=473); independent validation cohort C2 comprised 214 cancers and 60 healthy donors (N=274), for N=747 total.
- Cancer mix/comparator: colorectal, breast, lung, ovarian, gastric, and uveal melanoma samples with uneven localized/metastatic representation were compared with generally younger healthy controls. C2 did not contain every C1 cancer-stage subgroup. It was not a prospective asymptomatic screening or diagnostic-workup cohort, and benign, inflammatory, autoimmune, and differential-diagnosis controls were absent.
- Analysis sets: global methylation, 30 single-CpG features, 372 haplotype features, random-forest classifiers, and a genome-wide CNA z-score were analyzed. Discovery performance used 5,000 repeated random splits of the same C1 participants; validation trained on all C1 and tested C2. Cancer-type “expert” models presuppose cancer type/stage knowledge. Integrated “all” and “stack” models pooled C1+C2 for later analyses and are not independent validation.

- Positive findings: LINE-1 methylation/haplotype features distinguished many cancer groups from healthy donors; independent C2 validation retained an all-cancer AUC of 0.88; a combined methylation/CNA signal improved case detection in this retrospective dataset.
- Negative/uncertain findings: global methylation was null in metastatic lung and localized breast cancer; validation sensitivity was only 49% in metastatic lung cancer at 99% specificity; C1/C2 ovarian M0 distributions differed; survival evidence lacked main-PDF effect estimates and adjustment; intended-use calibration, PPV, prospective thresholds, and clinical utility were not evaluated.
- Safety findings: this was an assay study, not a treatment study. No clinical safety endpoint was applicable, and harms from false positives, downstream imaging/biopsy, overdiagnosis, or false reassurance were not assessed.
- Source alerts: (1) the abstract/discussion move from retrospective case–control validation to “blood screening in asymptomatic patients,” “applied as is in the clinic,” and routine diagnostic use; these claims exceed the design. (2) The survival association is called prognostic despite no main-PDF HR, CI, P value, endpoint definition, time origin, or adjustment. (3) Integrated/leave-subgroup-out analyses pool C1 and C2 and are not independent validation. (4) Expert models use cancer-type/stage labels and are not a general screening classifier. (5) C1/C2 ovarian M0 methylation differs significantly. (6) Repeated-split CIs do not represent independent participant resampling. (7) study-wide multiplicity was not controlled. (8) benign/inflammatory controls, ethnicity control, and prospective intended-use sampling were absent. (9) patents cover the method. (10) supporting survival, age, subgroup, and external-WGBS analyses were in an unsupplied supplement.
- Limitations: retrospective convenience case–control cohorts; heterogeneous cancer/stage composition; small or missing subgroups; healthy-control age imbalance; no benign/differential-diagnosis controls; no prospective asymptomatic cohort; cutoff tuning in discovery data; repeated-split uncertainty; no study-wide multiplicity correction; pooled later models; incomplete clinical covariates; supplement not supplied.
- Claim ceiling: DIAMOND provides retrospective proof of concept that LINE-1 methylation/haplotype and CNA features carry multicancer signal, with an independently tested all-cancer AUC of 0.88 and highly variable subgroup sensitivity. The paper does not establish population screening, diagnostic utility, clinical readiness, independent prognosis, tumor-burden monitoring, MRD/relapse detection, or superiority to other tests.

### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: prospective multicenter, nonrandomized DRUP basket/umbrella cohort of crizotinib 250 mg twice daily for advanced NSCLC with MET exon 14 skipping or another pathogenic MET mutation. Thirty patients started treatment; the protocol replaced six who received <28 days for the primary efficacy/biomarker set, leaving n=24. Safety included all 30.
- Primary rule/comparator: Simon-like two-stage rule tested a clinical-benefit rate (confirmed CR/PR or stable disease ≥16 weeks) of 10% versus ≥30%; ≥5/24 with benefit supported further study. There was no concurrent control.

- Positive findings: the n=24 efficacy set exceeded the prespecified clinical-benefit rule; 15 confirmed responses occurred; all-started ORR remained 50%; one p.H1094Y patient achieved CR.
- Negative/uncertain findings: efficacy estimates fell in the all-started set; no intracranial lesion disappeared; no genomic or transcriptomic biomarker survived testing; WGS/RNA coverage was low; the MET-TKD inference rests on one patient; no control group was present.
- Safety findings: grade ≥3 treatment-related events occurred in 40%, treatment stopped for toxicity in 10%, hepatobiliary events were prominent, and one fatal opioid interaction was attributed to crizotinib-mediated CYP3A4 inhibition.
- Source alerts: (1) the abstract gives NCT0295234, whereas Introduction/Translational Relevance give NCT02925234. (2) Excluding/replacing six <28-day patients materially inflates ORR/CBR/PFS/OS relative to all-started estimates. (3) the conclusion “highly effective” is stronger than warranted for a small uncontrolled cohort. (4) newer-MET-inhibitor efficacy/toxicity comparisons are unadjusted cross-trial comparisons. (5) one H1094Y CR cannot “confirm” the entire TKD-mutation class as a target. (6) biomarker nulls are underpowered because only 11 WGS and 10 RNA profiles were available. (7) no routine intracranial imaging was required and no intracranial lesion disappeared. (8) lower-grade safety burden is not fully displayed. (9) sponsor supplied drug/financial support. (10) screening log, clinical subgroups, and RNA results reside partly in an unsupplied supplement.
- Limitations: nonrandomized single arm; n=30 safety/n=24 headline efficacy; informative early-treatment exclusion/replacement; wide CIs; predominantly treatment-naïve cohort; no blinded central-response statement in the main PDF; incomplete CNS imaging; sparse biomarker tissue; no longitudinal sampling; multiple exploratory tests; cross-trial comparison; supplement not supplied.
- Claim ceiling: crizotinib showed prospective single-arm activity in MET-mutated advanced NSCLC and met the cohort’s clinical-benefit decision rule, but all-started efficacy was lower and clinically important grade ≥3/fatal toxicity occurred. The study does not establish superiority/equivalence to selective MET inhibitors, comparative safety, intracranial efficacy, first-line advantage, or a class-wide predictive role for MET TKD mutations.
