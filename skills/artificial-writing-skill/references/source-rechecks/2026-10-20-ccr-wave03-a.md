### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: open-label, single-center phase I/II study of olaparib plus temozolomide in relapsed or recurrent SCLC. Two schedules were enrolled sequentially and were not randomized: cohort 1 received both drugs on days 1-7 of each 21-day cycle, whereas cohort 2 received temozolomide on days 1-7 and continuous olaparib on days 1-21. The cohort-1 recommended phase II dose was olaparib 200 mg twice daily plus temozolomide 75 mg/m² daily. The cohort-2 phase II expansion was not opened because of limited preliminary activity.
- Analysis sets/endpoints: 66 patients were treated (50 in cohort 1 and 16 in cohort 2), with 48 and 14 response-evaluable, respectively. Phase I evaluated dose-limiting toxicity and dose selection; phase II used confirmed objective response as its primary endpoint. The multistage design had 87% power for a 30% response rate and approximately 9% type-I error under a 10% true rate, requiring at least six responses to support further study.
- Systemic efficacy: cohort 1 produced 20 confirmed responses among 48 evaluable patients (ORR 41.7%), plus four unconfirmed partial responses. Cohort 2 produced one confirmed response among 14 (7.1%), plus two unconfirmed partial responses. Median PFS was 4.0 versus 1.5 months and median OS 9.0 versus 9.3 months. These sequential, differently scheduled cohorts cannot support a causal schedule comparison.
- CNS subset: 29/66 patients had baseline brain metastases, including 15 with untreated lesions. Intracranial assessment was a post hoc exploratory analysis, lesions of at least 5 mm were measured by an independent radiologist using modified RECIST, and protocol-mandated surveillance imaging was absent. One of the 15 was unevaluable because of systemic progression.
- Intracranial-response metric boundary: Table 3 reports six CR, four PR, three SD, one PD, and one nonevaluable case among the 15, yielding a disease-control rate of 13/15 (87%; 95% CI 59.5%-98.3%). The displayed 67% intracranial ORR includes unconfirmed responses. Only four patients had confirmed intracranial responses; two unconfirmed responders subsequently had CNS progression, and four lacked follow-up CNS imaging. Median duration of intracranial response was 6.0 months (2.3-not reached) when confirmed and unconfirmed responses were pooled. The 67% value must not be relabeled as confirmed ORR.
- Exploratory phenotype analysis: tumor immunohistochemistry was available for only 22/66 patients: ASCL1-dominant in seven, NEUROD1-dominant in one, co-dominant in nine, pan-negative in five, and no POU2F3-dominant tumor. The study could not identify a response association; absence of a detected association in this sparse subset is not evidence of no biological heterogeneity.
- Safety: in cohort 1, maximum treatment-related toxicity was grade 1/2 in 13/50 (26%), grade 3 in 25/50 (50%), grade 4 in 8/50 (16%), and grade 5 in 2/50 (4%). The two treatment-related deaths were attributed to pneumonitis and neutropenic sepsis. In cohort 2, corresponding counts were 13/16 (81%), 2/16 (12%), 1/16 (6%), and zero. Sequential enrollment and schedule differences preclude a comparative safety conclusion.
- Claim ceiling: the intermittent regimen showed single-arm antitumor activity in relapsed SCLC. The CNS observations are hypothesis-generating because they were post hoc, include unconfirmed responses, and lack mandated follow-up. The data do not establish CNS efficacy, schedule superiority, or a phenotype-response biomarker. The paper itself states that the cohorts were not randomized, offers no established biological or pharmacokinetic explanation for the schedule difference, and emphasizes the small tissue subset.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: single-center phase I feasibility study of induction tremelimumab plus durvalumab before concurrent chemoradiotherapy (cCRT) in unresectable locally advanced NSCLC. Of 24 screened patients, 17 enrolled and two withdrew consent, leaving 15 in the per-protocol population: five, six, and four in cohorts I-III. The cohorts differed in tremelimumab dose and number of induction cycles; cohort III stopped early for safety.
- Feasibility: 13/15 patients completed immunotherapy and cCRT within the protocol-specified timeframe. Feasibility violations were zero of five in cohort I, one of six in cohort II because of progressive disease, and one of four in cohort III because a pulmonary abscess prevented complete radiotherapy. All cohorts met the prespecified feasibility criterion, but this procedural endpoint does not override the high-dose safety signal.
- Safety: nine grade ≥3 immune-related adverse events occurred in seven of 15 patients. Safety violations occurred in 1/5, 3/6, and 3/4 patients across cohorts I-III, respectively, and cohort III was stopped. Four colitis cases required infliximab, although cCRT initiation was not delayed. Eleven patients began adjuvant durvalumab (median 19 cycles), six completed one year, and discontinuations included progression in three, pneumonitis in one, and COVID-19 in one. The two 300-mg tremelimumab schedules cannot be described as acceptably safe.
- Descriptive activity: after induction immunotherapy, seven patients had a RECIST partial response and six a metabolic partial response. Among 11 patients with multilevel N2 or N3 involvement, eight were downstaged to N0, N1, or single-level N2. Two underwent surgery: one had a pathologic complete response and one residual N2 disease. These small, noncontrolled observations are not an efficacy comparison.
- Survival: at 35.7 months median follow-up, median DFS was 26.1 months and median OS 52.7 months. A source inconsistency affects the lower DFS confidence bound: the abstract/body report 11.5 months, whereas the rendered Figure 3 caption reports 11.1 months. The exact lower bound is withheld unless source location is specified; the median itself is unaffected.
- Imaging analyses: serial PET was available at all three timepoints for 11 patients. Baseline tumor-positive nodes had higher total lesion glycolysis than tumor-negative nodes (19.4 versus 6.2 g; `P=0.02`), and after induction matched positive nodes remained higher than negative nodes (11.3 versus 0 g; `P=0.04`). MRI was available for ten patients: the apparent diffusion coefficient change did not differ between downstaged and nondownstaged patients (+12% versus +13%; `P=0.94`), and the matched positive-versus-negative-node comparison (+44% versus +6%; `P=0.20`) was also nonsignificant.
- Method/interpretation boundary: PET contributed to the nodal downstaging determination, so its later relation to downstaging is not an independent predictive validation. MRI morphology suggested downstaging in five versus no downstaging in two patients, but numbers were very small. No multiplicity correction was used. Positive metabolic comparisons and negative diffusion comparisons must both be retained rather than converted into a unified imaging-biomarker claim.
- Claim ceiling: lower-dose induction was feasible, whereas the high-dose cohorts had unacceptable immune toxicity. Antitumor activity, downstaging, imaging, DFS, and OS are exploratory in 15 selected patients and do not establish benefit over standard cCRT or identify a validated imaging predictor. Larger controlled evaluation is required.

- Registered PDF: `Cappuzzo_MAPK_SQUINT_immunotherapy_PMID39836372.pdf`; SHA-256 `208c2164219030d690c494474e477bbfea5fe9178ec5c5a9b4025b5b2270312d`; 10 physical pages.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: open-label randomized phase II SQUINT trial of nivolumab plus ipilimumab (NI) versus platinum chemotherapy plus nivolumab (N-CT) in 91 chemotherapy-naïve patients with advanced or recurrent lung squamous-cell carcinoma (45 versus 46). The protocol was amended to a noncomparative design; each arm independently tested a 12-month OS target. It was not powered for a head-to-head regimen comparison or biomarker-by-treatment interaction.
- Trial activity: 12-month OS was 62% for NI and 50% for N-CT; median OS was 14.7 versus 12.0 months. The exploratory between-arm HR was 0.66 (95% CI 0.39-1.12), so no statistically established efficacy difference was shown. Nominal subgroup findings in current smokers and patients with bone metastases were not multiplicity-controlled and lacked interaction confirmation.
- Sequencing/molecular definition: 87 tumors yielded libraries and 74 passed final biomarker analysis. “MAPK alteration” aggregated mutation or copy-number change across 15 prespecified pathway genes (BRAF, EGFR, ERBB2, FGFR1/2/3, JAK3, KRAS, MET, NF1, NRAS, NRG1, PIK3CA, PIK3R1, and RAF1). This biologically heterogeneous composite must not be reduced to a single-gene mechanism.
- Primary biomarker associations: among 74 profiled patients, 51 MAPK-altered versus 23 wild-type patients had median OS of 16.3 versus 10.7 months; the displayed HR of 2.96 (1.54-5.51; `P<0.001`) is oriented as wild type relative to altered. Median PFS was 6.3 versus 4.2 months (HR 1.82, 1.05-3.16; `P=0.03`). Mutation-only analyses also favored altered tumors for PFS (HR 2.48; `P=0.001`) and OS (HR 2.45; `P=0.005`), with response rates 43% versus 15%.
- Predictive boundary: associations were present across both randomized treatments, and the reported magnitude was statistically stronger in the N-CT arm. No formal treatment-interaction test established specificity to one immunotherapy regimen. Thus the data support prognosis/association within ICI-containing cohorts, not a validated treatment-selection interaction.
- External cohorts: in a Dana-Farber ICI-treated LSCC cohort (n=63; 29 altered, 34 wild type), median OS was 23.4 versus 9.1 months. The prose reports `P<0.001`, whereas rendered Figure 4 prints `P>0.001` alongside the same HR of 2.96; the exact inequality is internally inconsistent and is withheld. PFS was not significantly different (6.2 versus 4.1 months; HR 1.38, 0.80-2.37; `P=0.24`). In the combined ICI-treated set (n=137), OS remained longer in altered disease, but pooled observational replication does not replace prospective validation.
- Negative control: among 79 Dana-Farber patients not treated with ICI, MAPK status was not associated with OS (HR 0.74, 0.43-1.27; `P=0.28`). This pattern supports a treatment-related hypothesis but does not itself prove prediction because the ICI and non-ICI cohorts were not randomized against one another.
- Immune profiling: multiplex immunofluorescence was available in 60 cases. Altered tumors showed nominally higher CD8-positive/PD-1-positive measurements and related ratios in selected analyses (`P` values approximately 0.04-0.047); after excluding copy-number-only alterations, one ratio was nonsignificant (`P=0.07`). Small numbers, multiple marker comparisons, and absence of functional validation make these mechanistic signals exploratory.
- Claim ceiling: composite MAPK alterations were associated with better outcomes in SQUINT and an external ICI-treated LSCC cohort, with no OS signal in a separate non-ICI cohort. The result identifies a candidate biomarker for validation, not a clinically validated predictor. The exploratory biomarker analysis, 74-patient discovery set, heterogeneous 15-gene composite, no treatment interaction, and source-level Figure 4 `P`-operator conflict constrain transfer.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Discussion emphasizes that SQUINT became noncomparative and cannot establish an efficacy difference between its regimens (p9). It explicitly describes biomarker findings as exploratory, limited by small numbers, and requiring confirmation.
- Safe template: “Although [altered group] experienced longer [endpoint] than [wild-type group] ([HR], 95% CI [CI]), the composite biomarker analysis was exploratory and lacked a treatment-interaction test; it should therefore be considered a candidate predictive signal rather than a validated treatment selector.”

- Issue year/version: 2025; `publisher_main_pdf`.
- Registered PDF: `Zhang_Follow-up_Analysis_Enhances_Understanding_of_Molecular_Residual_Disease_in_Localized_Non-Small_Ce_PMID39853318.pdf`; SHA-256 `3ce365040f2b48f5b03720e1d6f3dc1fcd786ad5da6d00681c0be66d064d506c`; 10 physical pages.

- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 p3; Figure 2 p4; Figure 3 p5; Figure 4 p6; Figure 5 p7; Figure 6 p8. All legends, risk sets, forest plots, and assay-timing diagrams were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: prospective observational follow-up of a previously reported tumor-informed ctDNA cohort after curative-intent resection of stage I-III NSCLC. The study analyzed 261 patients and 948 postoperative plasma samples over a median 43.4 months. Landmark and longitudinal definitions were prespecified, but sampling schedules were not always followed. There was no randomized ctDNA-guided intervention.
- Cohort/outcomes: stages I, II, and III included 163, 53, and 45 patients; 77 recurred, but only four deaths occurred, so OS could not be meaningfully analyzed. The prognostic endpoint is DFS rather than mortality.
- MRD performance and prognosis: landmark ctDNA was detectable in 23 and undetectable in 238, with PPV 91.3% and NPV 76.5%. Longitudinal ctDNA was detectable in 69 and undetectable in 192, with PPV 92.8% and NPV 93.2%. DFS HRs for negative versus positive status were 0.12 (95% CI 0.04-0.37) at landmark and 0.05 (0.03-0.08) longitudinally, both `P<0.001`; a guarantee-time-adjusted analysis remained strongly associated (HR 0.03, 0.016-0.056). These are prognostic associations, not proof that acting on ctDNA improves outcomes.
- False negatives/“cure” boundary: 13 patients recurred despite longitudinally undetectable ctDNA; seven had brain-only relapse, and four lacked a blood draw within six months before recurrence. False-negative patterns varied with stage and sampling. High observed NPV and a low observed recurrence rate do not prove that ctDNA-negative patients are cured or can safely omit therapy or surveillance.
- Positive-without-relapse cases: five patients had detectable MRD yet remained disease-free for a mean 31.1 months and all subsequently cleared ctDNA spontaneously; sequencing error or delayed recurrence could not be excluded. Persistent versus intermittent positivity was not statistically separated (HR 1.81, 0.93-3.52; `P=0.093`). This negative result must accompany any claim that persistence severity is established.
- Lead time: median molecular lead time was 5.2 months (3.9-8.9). A maximum VAF above 0.55 and ctDNA concentration above 13 hGE/mL were associated with shorter lead time (HR 3.73, 1.95-7.11 and HR 2.51, 1.42-4.41). Recurrence site was not associated (`P=0.139`). Rendered Figure 3C additionally prints “HR 0.05, 95% CI 0.33-0.7, `P<0.001`,” an internally impossible point estimate/interval pairing. That affected HR/CI is withheld; the median lead-time result is retained.
- Adjuvant-treatment analyses: among 238 landmark-negative patients, 49 received adjuvant therapy. The unadjusted comparison appeared worse with treatment, consistent with confounding by indication. After propensity matching (22 versus 22), no significant difference remained (HR 0.69, 0.22-2.15; `P=0.529`). Among 23 landmark-positive patients, treatment versus no treatment showed only a nonsignificant trend (HR 0.56, 0.22-1.38; `P=0.193`). In EGFR-mutant landmark-negative patients, adjuvant TKI likewise was nonsignificant (HR 0.57, 0.22-1.47; `P=0.209`). These analyses do not establish predictive utility.
- Additional source alert: p7 prints `P = 575` for an NPV comparison involving TKI exposure, evidently malformed. The exact value is withheld rather than silently repaired. The fitted temporal recurrence peak near 18 months and suggestion of at least two years of monitoring are descriptive scheduling hypotheses, not tested clinical-management benefits.
- Claim ceiling: serial postoperative ctDNA status was a strong prognostic marker in this observational cohort, and longitudinal sampling improved observed classification. The data do not validate ctDNA as a surrogate endpoint, prove cure when negative, or justify treatment escalation/de-escalation. Nonuniform sampling, brain-only false negatives, very few deaths, selection/confounding in adjuvant comparisons, and source-level numerical defects limit transfer.

The Discussion treats monitoring duration and adjuvant-treatment implications as questions for prospective trials rather than settled clinical recommendations (pp8-9).

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: open-label, 75-center randomized phase III trial in Japan comparing continuous EGFR TKI with the same TKI intercalated with three cycles of cisplatin/pemetrexed in advanced or recurrent EGFR exon 19-deleted or L858R nonsquamous NSCLC. Stable CNS disease was allowed. The experimental schedule used TKI on days 1-56, a two-week break, chemotherapy on days 71, 92, and 113, and TKI restart on day 134.
- Analysis sets/backbone change: 501 patients were randomized (250 versus 251), 491 were eligible, 245 per arm received protocol therapy and entered safety analysis, and 223 versus 232 were response-evaluable. The original gefitinib cohort contained 308 patients; after protocol amendment, 193 received osimertinib. The TKI-backbone cohorts arose in different calendar periods and were not randomized against each other.

Median OS was 48 months in each arm and the reported primary comparison was negative (`P=0.4496`). The HR/CI and displayed two-sided value may be reported with explicit source labeling, but no single one-sided value should be silently selected.

Exploratory exon 19-versus-L858R patterns lacked an interaction test and should not be converted into genotype-specific treatment recommendations.
- Safety: chemotherapy intercalation increased expected hematologic, renal, and gastrointestinal toxicity. Pneumonitis incidence in the reported gefitinib and osimertinib strata was approximately 1.0% and 7.9%, respectively; the severe case occurred with osimertinib monotherapy. One treatment-related death in the monotherapy osimertinib group followed cerebral infarction and respiratory failure after *Mycoplasma* infection and must not be omitted or reclassified as unrelated. Adverse-event discontinuation rates varied by backbone and arm.
- Table-label source alert: the rendered caption for Table 2 calls it “Subsequent treatment,” although its rows contain the safety summary, while Table 3 is captioned “Safety summary” despite containing subsequent-treatment categories. Content and Results prose were used to interpret the tables; the swapped labels must not be propagated. Approximately 80% in each arm received subsequent therapy, a plausible contributor to OS dilution but not a tested causal explanation.
- Claim ceiling: intercalated chemotherapy improved PFS in the pooled randomized population but did not improve OS, the primary endpoint. The osimertinib subgroup did not show a statistically significant PFS difference. The trial did not compare intercalated with concurrent chemo-TKI schedules, and cross-backbone temporal subgroups cannot determine which TKI benefits from chemotherapy.

- Paired testing: among 186 patients with both evaluable modalities, 74 were positive by both, 106 TBx-positive/LBx-negative, and six TBx-negative/LBx-positive. Another 98 were LBx-positive with unavailable tissue, 28 TBx-positive with unavailable plasma, and one was a protocol violation. Discordance can reflect shedding, assay, specimen adequacy, biology, and sampling time rather than a simple sensitivity ranking.
- Baseline phenotype: LBx-positive patients had greater observed disease burden, including sum of longest diameters 67.1 versus 55.2 mm and at least three target lesions in 27.5% versus 18.8%, with poorer health-related quality of life. LBx status therefore marks a selected higher-shedding/higher-burden population and may confound outcome comparisons.
- Matched TBx-positive, treatment-naïve subgroup (n=94): LBx-negative versus LBx-positive patients had ORR 57.7% versus 64.3%, median duration of response not estimable versus 19.4 months, PFS 22.1 versus 12.1 months, and OS 32.7 versus 28.5 months. In 86 previously treated TBx-positive patients, corresponding ORRs were 44.4% versus 53.1%, duration 12.6 versus 9.9 months, PFS 13.8 versus 8.2 months, and OS 20.8 versus 19.8 months. These are descriptive estimates with overlapping uncertainty, not evidence that biopsy modality modifies tepotinib effect.
- Baseline plasma-genomic context: among 165 patients with evaluable Guardant360 plasma results, 140 had at least one detectable alteration and 25 did not. Median PFS was 9.5 versus 19.4 months and OS 19.3 versus 20.9 months. Detection is strongly related to shedding and disease burden; this is a prognostic association, not a treatment-predictive comparison.
- Concomitant alterations: among 114 LBx-positive patients, 38 had no reported concomitant alteration and 76 had at least one. Outcomes were numerically more favorable without concomitant changes (ORR 65.8% versus 43.4%; duration 19.4 versus 9.7 months; PFS 13.8 versus 6.9 months; OS 24.6 versus 14.4 months). Heterogeneous co-alterations, descriptive analysis, and no untreated control preclude causal resistance claims.
- Timing boundary: median time from tissue biopsy to treatment was 85 days, with a range of 13-2,838 days. In 61 dual-positive cases, tissue preceded plasma testing by a median 44 days, range 8-1,111. Temporal evolution and old tissue can contribute to discordance. Safety was not analyzed in this report and cannot be inferred from the efficacy subsets.
- Claim ceiling: LBx complements TBx and can identify METex14 disease when tissue is unavailable, while LBx-negative results should be reflexed to tissue where feasible. The data suggest that plasma positivity identifies a higher-shedding/higher-burden, less favorable prognostic group, but do not establish platform sensitivity or equivalence, biopsy-defined treatment-effect modification, or a prognostic mechanism independent of burden.

It also treats outcome differences as descriptive associations within a single-arm treatment cohort.
- Safe template: “Patients identified by liquid biopsy had [higher burden characteristic] and numerically [outcome], but the overlapping biopsy groups and single-arm design indicate a prognostic selection pattern rather than differential treatment efficacy.”
- Unsafe transfer: treating TBx- and LBx-positive sets as independent randomized cohorts; claiming that LBx is more or less sensitive without a reference standard; attributing shorter PFS to plasma testing itself; or inferring treatment safety from this analysis.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: pooled exposure-response modeling using phase I DeLLphi-300 and phase II DeLLphi-301 in previously treated SCLC. Of 430 enrolled patients, 420 had pharmacokinetic data; eight combination-treated patients were excluded, leaving 412 for exposure-response. Efficacy sets were 377 for ORR/DCR/PFS/OS and 317 for best tumor-size reduction, while safety used 412. DeLLphi-301 randomized 10 mg versus 100 mg in one part, but the broader pooled dose range included nonrandomized phase I escalation.
- Model chain: doses ranged from 0.003 to 100 mg every two weeks, with a 200-mg every-three-week schedule also represented. Population pharmacokinetics generated first-cycle average, maximum, and trough exposures. Emax models were used for response/disease control, an Imax model for tumor-size reduction, and Cox models for PFS/OS. Covariate selection used forward `P<0.05` and backward `P<0.001`, with BIC and predictive checks; there was no independent external validation.

- Time-to-event findings: the lowest exposure quartile had worse PFS and OS, with little modeled improvement beyond exposures approximating the 10-mg dose. Each log-unit increase in average exposure was associated with approximately 8% and 15% lower hazards for PFS and OS, respectively. Baseline lesion burden predicted OS independently; no other examined covariate showed a retained modifier. Sparse racial, ethnic, organ-impairment, and body-size strata limit any broad “no dose adjustment” claim.

Neutropenia rose from 4% and 5% to 10% and 17%, with a positive exposure relationship (`P=0.00260`). Grade ≥3 neurologic events including ICANS were 10%, 10%, 15%, and 6%, without a monotonic pattern.

Regression found statistically significant inverse associations (`P=0.012` and `P=0.0297`) despite only 16 events. The abstract’s “no exposure relationship” is therefore defensible only as “no positive exposure-toxicity gradient”; it must not be reported as no statistical association. The authors attribute the inverse pattern to event scarcity and variability.

They do not prove superiority of 10 mg to another randomized dose or therapy, guarantee efficacy for an individual exposure, or establish universal absence of dose adjustment. Model assumptions, pooling of randomized and nonrandomized doses, sparse high-grade safety events, and no external validation constrain transfer.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: retrospective patient-level aggregate analysis of eight trials comprising 11 cohorts of oncogene-positive advanced NSCLC treated with a TKI (EGFR, ALK, MET, or RET). Of 1,590 submitted patients, 940 met analytic eligibility and were randomly split two-thirds/one-third into training and test sets. Distinct endpoint sets included 716 for OS, 843 for PFS, 828 for early RECIST, and 662/788 for combined RECIST plus ctDNA OS/PFS analyses; cohorts with fewer than 20 patients were excluded.
- Assay/sampling heterogeneity: studies used ddPCR or NGS panels of 4-523 genes with variable limits of detection. Baseline (`T0`) could be up to 14 days before treatment and on-treatment (`T1`) within 70 days. When multiple T1 samples existed, the lowest VAF or a nondetectable result was chosen as the “best” sample, potentially favoring clearance and linking classification to sampling density.
- Landmark and plan boundaries: a 70-day landmark excluded patients with early events, including seven poor OS outcomes and 44 poor PFS outcomes. This survivorship/guarantee-time constraint must accompany prognostic claims. The statistical plan was largely prespecified, but the initial objective-1 analysis informed the final categorization; no multiplicity correction was applied, and analyses were exploratory.
- Category development: percentage-change categories did not show consistent outcome separation. Detection-state categories were then used: nondetectable at both timepoints, detectable-to-nondetectable (“clearance”), nondetectable-to-detectable (“emerging,” too sparse for stable inference), and persistent detection. Baseline detectability itself was prognostic, so clearance analyses must distinguish baseline risk from added on-treatment information.
- Main prognostic result: in combined analyses, persistent detection versus clearance was associated with worse OS (adjusted HR 2.12, 95% CI 1.50-3.01; `P<0.001`) and PFS (HR 1.52, 1.17-1.98; `P=0.002`). The paper’s plotted combined groups included 187 never-detectable, 394 clearance, and 135 persistent cases for OS, and 218, 431, and 194 for PFS. These associations do not validate ctDNA clearance as a surrogate for treatment effect.
- RECIST comparison: early RECIST response (CR/PR, n=540) versus nonresponse (SD/PD, n=288) was not associated with OS (HR 1.21, 0.87-1.67; `P=0.254`) or PFS (HR 0.96, 0.77-1.20; `P=0.728`). Adding RECIST to ctDNA did not significantly improve model likelihood (`P=0.195`). This means early ctDNA carried more prognostic information in these models, not that imaging is clinically unnecessary.
- Response-stratified results: within RECIST SD, persistent versus cleared ctDNA was associated with worse OS (HR 4.15, 2.07-8.33; `P<0.001`) and PFS (HR 2.55, 1.50-4.31; `P<0.001`). Within PR, the differences were nonsignificant for OS (HR 1.62, 0.95-2.75; `P=0.077`) and PFS (HR 1.39, 0.99-1.94; `P=0.054`). These near-threshold negative results must not be described as significant. In persistent ctDNA overall, RECIST response related to OS (HR 1.93; `P=0.037`) but not PFS (HR 1.29; `P=0.296`).
- Source/analysis caveat: rendered Table 1 displays a baseline nondetectable/detectable denominator of 257/1,015 despite the stated 940-patient eligible cohort. Because the table denominator is not readily reconciled to the unique-patient analytic cohort, that descriptive count is not transferred into the central result; endpoint-specific denominators are used instead. Safety was not analyzed.
- Claim ceiling: early ctDNA clearance is a patient-level prognostic marker that can refine early risk stratification during TKI therapy. The analysis does not establish a trial-level surrogate endpoint, predict comparative treatment benefit, or justify changing therapy on clearance status. Heterogeneous assays/drivers/trials, selected “best” T1 sample, landmark exclusions, sparse emerging ctDNA, incomplete covariates, no multiplicity correction, and no prospective intervention validation restrict transfer.
