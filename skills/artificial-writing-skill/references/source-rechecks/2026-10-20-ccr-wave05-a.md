### Identity and coverage

- Issue year/version: 2026; `not_classified_by_header`.
- Registered PDF: `Cipriani_Intratumoral_Immune_Heterogeneity_Drives_Divergent_Outcomes_to_PD-(L)1_Blockade_in_Lung_Cancer_PMID42456046.pdf`; SHA-256 `c795f9d233acd688cca4a87407fe7a0a2c8e863c69b8c9833d696982ced28855`; 44 physical pages.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: spatial immune labels from multiregion, resectable stage I-IIIB TRACERx421 NSCLC were used to train a CatBoost classifier that infers four intratumoral immune-heterogeneity (ITIH) states from one bulk-RNA sample. Biological patterning was examined in TCGA and IRE digital pathology, and outcome associations were assessed retrospectively across three advanced-NSCLC cohorts treated with PD-(L)1 inhibitors. This is classifier development plus retrospective treated-cohort validation, not prospective clinical-utility validation.
- Discovery denominator conflict: Methods reports 746 regions from 246 eligible multiregion patients after excluding single-region and uncommon-histology cases (pp10-11), whereas Results/Figure 2 states that ITIH occurred in 80/285 cases (about 30%; pp17 and 40). The PDF does not reconcile the 246-versus-285 case denominators. Both are retained, and prevalence wording is withheld beyond “80/285 in the Results/Figure 2 display.”
- Classifier chain and boundary: 29 ssGSEA immune signatures were discretized at plus/minus 0.75 and the region-level dataset was split 80/20; ten-fold cross-validation was performed within training and final parameters were manually selected. Hom-IE recall was 0.694, precision 0.658, F1 0.676, and global accuracy 0.613. The split unit is not stated clearly enough to exclude correlated regions from the same tumor appearing across partitions, and no independent multiregion ground-truth cohort was available. The external cohorts test biological/outcome patterns from single samples, not recovery of true spatial heterogeneity.
- Clinical sets: cohort A comprised 291 patients (SU2C 132, Gustave Roussy 59, IRE 100; 154 first-line); cohort B comprised 439 second-line atezolizumab-treated OAK/POPLAR patients; cohort C comprised 355 SMC patients with RNA-seq and survival data only. KEAP1/STK11 status was available for 155 cohort-A and 251 cohort-B patients; observed PD-L1 was available for 200 and 180, respectively. The three clinical cohorts totaled 1,085, but covariate availability and treatment line differed.
- Digital-pathology evidence: TCGA NSCLC (n=863) showed a TIL-pattern gradient across ITIH classes (`P<0.001` both with and without MFP stratification). In the independent IRE set (n=64), the cell-composition gradients were directionally consistent but nonsignificant: Figure 3 gives `P=0.074` and `P=0.061` (text rounds to 0.07 and 0.06). These data support a compatible pattern, not statistically confirmed independent orthogonal validation.
- Treated-cohort survival: in cohort A, median OS for Hom-IE, Het-IE, Het-D, and Hom-D was 34.6, 16.6, 13.7, and 12.1 months; Hom-IE pairwise log-rank `P=0.026`, `0.006`, and `<0.001`, with HRs 0.57 (0.34-0.94), 0.52 (0.32-0.84), and 0.48 (0.32-0.73). BOR distribution was not significant (`P=0.191`). In cohort B, medians were 21.9, 13.3, 10.6, and 8.4 months; Figure 4 gives Hom-IE pairwise `P=0.047`, `0.001`, and `<0.001`, while BOR differed (`P=0.014`). In cohort C, medians were 22.6, 13.4, 11.3, and 13.3 months; Figure 4 gives Hom-IE pairwise `P=0.005`, `0.049`, and `0.002`. These are survival associations in ICI-treated cohorts; they do not by themselves establish treatment-specific prediction.
- PD-L1 analysis: among 380 patients with observed PD-L1, 75/129 PD-L1-high tumors were non-Hom-IE and nearly half of Hom-IE tumors were low/negative. The four-group OS comparison was significant (`P=0.0029`). Hom-IE/PD-L1-low had median OS 22.4 versus 10.8 months for non-Hom-IE/PD-L1-low (`P=0.012`). Comparisons of Hom-IE/low with Hom-IE/high (`P=0.50`) and non-Hom-IE/high (`P=0.49`), and Hom-IE/high with non-Hom-IE/high (`P=0.24`), were nonsignificant and do not demonstrate equivalence or comparability.
- PD-L1 multivariable model: with non-Hom-IE/PD-L1-low as reference (n=193), HRs were 0.82 (0.57-1.18; `P=0.287`) for non-Hom-IE/high (n=75), 0.65 (0.43-0.97; `P=0.035`) for Hom-IE/low (n=58), and 0.57 (0.38-0.87; `P=0.010`) for Hom-IE/high (n=54). Histology and sex were nonsignificant; second-or-later line carried HR 1.59 (1.17-2.15; `P=0.003`). This supports adjusted prognostic separation in the pooled treated set, not a treatment-interaction claim.
- PD-L1 imputation boundary: SVM performance was reported as cohort-A AUC/recall/F1 of 0.80/0.75/0.73 and cohort-B 0.88/0.78/0.71. Cohort A included best overall response among the imputation features, a post-treatment outcome that prevents interpreting the imputed model as a deployable baseline biomarker. Pairwise survival claims after imputation appear only in unreviewed supplementary figures and are not promoted here.
- MFP and genomic context: within pooled MFP-immune-enriched tumors, Hom-IE had median OS 23.4 versus 14.5 months (`P<0.001`). KEAP1/STK11 mutation distributions did not differ across ITIH classes in cohort A (`P=0.594`) or B (`P=0.807`). Figure 6E shows an overall four-group survival `P<0.001`, but the exact within-Hom-IE mutation comparison and LUAD-restricted results are deferred to unreviewed supplementary material; the main PDF therefore supports an adverse genomic-context signal, not a fully verified pairwise estimate.
- Predictive-claim hold: the Methods defines cohort B as 439 atezolizumab-treated patients, whereas the asserted atezolizumab-versus-docetaxel comparison and its exact comparator denominator/interaction evidence are only in unreviewed Supplementary Figures S11/S17. The main PDF does not permit independent reconstruction of that treatment comparison. “Predictive value,” treatment de-escalation/intensification, and immunotherapy selection are therefore withheld; the verified claim is outcome stratification in treated cohorts.
- Safety: the paper is a retrospective biomarker/omics study and does not report a treatment safety analysis.
- Claim ceiling: the data support a candidate single-sample RNA classifier associated with immune patterns and OS among PD-(L)1-treated patients. They do not establish true spatial heterogeneity from one specimen, a prospective treatment-selection tool, a validated treatment interaction, safe de-escalation, or clinical utility beyond PD-L1.

## PMID 42714874

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Ceralasertib plus durvalumab in Ras mutant and Ras wild type advanced non-small cell lung cancer: Two cohorts of the phase II platform National Lung Matrix Trial*.
- Issue year/version: 2026; `not_classified_by_header`.
- Registered PDF: `Middleton_Ceralasertib_Durvalumab_NLMT_PMID42714874.pdf`; SHA-256 `a639345437ffb86cf289777c1fb625b222681df410474ff87a82a0ecd7e12da3`; 38 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`; the cited protocol, supplementary methods, seven supplementary figures, and six supplementary tables were not treated as reviewed.
- Section map: title/admin pp1-6; Translational Relevance p7; abstract p8; Introduction pp9-11; Methods pp11-15; Results pp15-21; Discussion pp21-23; admin/references pp24-29; figure legends pp30-32; Figures 1-6 pp33-38.
- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 p33; Figure 2 p34; Figure 3 p35; Figure 4 p36; Figure 5 p37; Figure 6 p38. No main table was present. All experimental replicates, significance marks, blots, flow counts, swimmer/waterfall annotations, survival risk sets, Bayesian intervals, heat-map labels, captions, and footnotes were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: two preplanned prospective parallel cohorts within the phase II National Lung Matrix Trial assessed ceralasertib plus durvalumab after PD-(L)1 failure in advanced NSCLC/LUAD, comparing a KRAS-mutant cohort (J1) with a KRAS-wild-type/no-actionable-alteration cohort (NAJ). A Bayesian adaptive screening design was accompanied by a BJ-hTERT inducible-KRAS model, TCGA LUAD transcriptomics, and TRACERx immunohistochemistry. This was not a randomized J1-versus-NAJ comparison.
- Intervention/endpoints: ceralasertib 240 mg twice daily on days 15-28 plus durvalumab 1,500 mg on day 1 of each 28-day cycle continued until confirmed progression, with protocol-permitted treatment beyond progression for perceived benefit. Coprimary outcomes were confirmed RECIST v1.1 objective response and durable clinical benefit, defined as being progression-free at the fourth CT assessment at about 24 weeks; early loss to follow-up counted as failure.
- Analysis sets and curtailed recruitment: 43 patients were registered (20 J1, 23 NAJ), below the target of 30 per cohort because the trial closed at the preplanned funding end. Safety used all 43 registered patients. Six were excluded from per-protocol efficacy analyses, leaving 19 J1 and 18 NAJ. Median treatment exposure was eight cycles (range 1-21) in J1 and four (1-36) in NAJ; all per-protocol participants had been followed to death at the October 15, 2025 lock.
- Coprimary activity: Bayesian DCB estimates were 37.7% (95% CrI 19.1-59.2; 7/19) in J1 and 29.3% (12.6-51.2; 5/18) in NAJ. Projected probability of a “GO” decision at n=30 was 0.92 for J1. Two objective responses occurred, both in J1, for a Bayesian OR estimate of 13.1% (3.2-31.7); the posterior probability of a clinically relevant OR rate was only 0.04. NAJ had no responders. The waterfall plot showed no clear cohort segregation.
- Survival and negative between-cohort inference: median PFS was 6.4 months (95% CrI 4.2-10.4) in J1 and 3.9 (2.5-6.5) in NAJ; median OS was 15.7 months (10.3-25.7) and 14.2 (9.2-23.6). All endpoints numerically favored J1, but the credible intervals for PFS, OS, DCB, and OR overlapped and the paper explicitly calls the differences modest and nonsignificant.
- Safety denominator: 3,556 AEs and 1,651 adverse reactions were reported in 42 and 39 registered patients, respectively; 178 AEs (5.0%) and 115 reactions (7.0%) were grade 3 or higher. These are event counts, not patient incidence proportions. There were 34 SAEs in 20 patients, including seven serious adverse reactions in five and eight suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions in six. Figure 3 lists one death as a discontinuation reason in each cohort, but the main PDF does not establish treatment attribution for those deaths.
- Preclinical positive and negative results: acute KRASG12V activation alone increased micronuclei nonsignificantly. Ceralasertib increased micronuclei dose-dependently up to 1 micromolar, and KRAS activation significantly augmented this effect at doses up to 1 micromolar; at 5 and 10 micromolar the contrasts were nonsignificant. Cell counts showed no adverse viability effect through 1 micromolar under the reported conditions. KRAS increased MYC targets and reduced IFN-related genes/STAT1; ceralasertib made the KRAS-associated STAT1 reduction nonsignificant, but no appreciable pSTAT1 restoration was observed. Micronucleus experiments used n=2-4 and STAT1 quantification n=2, limiting mechanistic strength.
- Human omics: against a common five-gene-wild-type TCGA reference (n=90), the single-mutation groups were EGFR n=21, KRAS n=55, and TP53 n=113, with Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment. Only RORC was significantly increased in KRAS-alone tumors; IL17A/IL17RE, neutrophil programs, MYC-target, IFN-alpha/gamma, and CIBERSORT differences attributed to KRAS alone were largely nonsignificant. Stronger MYC/IFN differences appeared in small KRAS/STK11 (n=20), KRAS/KEAP1 (n=14), and triple-mutant (n=11) groups. Untreated resected primary tumors cannot directly model the post-ICI metastatic trial state.
- Exploratory KEAP1/STK11 claim alert: baseline ctDNA passed QC for 15/19 evaluable J1 patients and identified three KEAP1 co-mutations. One KEAP1/STK11 case achieved PR with duration over 12 months and remained treated over 18 months; two KEAP1 cases had progressive disease as best response but stayed treated for roughly over 12 and nine months under the treatment-beyond-progression rule. The text calls these observations “meaningful” and later “significant” benefit, but no inferential test, comparator, or adequate subgroup denominator supports that wording. It is retained only as a three-case hypothesis-generating observation.
- Claim ceiling: the study provides a small prospective signal screen for ceralasertib/durvalumab after ICI resistance and shows numerically, but not significantly, higher outcomes in KRAS-mutant patients. It does not establish superiority between molecular cohorts, efficacy versus standard care, benefit for KEAP1/STK11 co-mutant disease, or clinical confirmation of the preclinical mechanism.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Translational Relevance statement and Discussion explicitly pair “modestly/numerically higher” outcomes with “nonsignificant” differences (pp7 and 21-22). The Results distinguishes a high projected probability of a DCB-based continuation decision from the low posterior probability of a clinically relevant OR rate (p17).
- Context-checked omics frame: the Results states that KRAS mutation alone had a very limited effect, retaining nonsignificant IL17/neutrophil, MYC, IFN, CIBERSORT, and G12C-versus-G12D comparisons rather than describing all directional changes as established biology (pp19-21).
- Safe template: “Outcomes were numerically higher in [molecular cohort A] than in [cohort B], but overlapping uncertainty intervals and curtailed recruitment preclude a statistically supported between-cohort advantage.”
- Unsafe transfer: converting event counts into percentages of patients; calling DCB a response rate; interpreting a projected Bayesian “GO” probability as efficacy proof; claiming significant benefit in three KEAP1 cases; or transferring primary-tumor/preclinical mechanisms to post-ICI metastatic patients.

### Six gates and verdict

- `coverage_check=pass`; `evidence_check=pass`; `results_check=pass`; `language_check=pass`; `traceability_check=pass`; `transfer_check=pass`.
- Verdict: `passed`, with curtailed recruitment, nonsignificant between-cohort results, event-versus-patient safety denominators, and the unsupported three-case KEAP1 benefit wording explicitly bounded.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: this prespecified post-progression analysis of the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III LAURA trial evaluated subsequent treatment, time to first subsequent treatment (TFST), second progression-free survival (PFS2), time to second subsequent treatment (TSST), and a second interim OS analysis after definitive chemoradiotherapy in unresectable stage III exon 19 deletion/L858R EGFR-mutated NSCLC without progression. Randomization was 2:1 to osimertinib 80 mg daily or placebo; open-label osimertinib was offered after BICR-confirmed progression under protocol conditions.
- Analysis sets and timing: all 216 randomized patients were included (osimertinib 143, placebo 73). TFST/PFS2/TSST and subsequent-treatment data used the January 5, 2024 cutoff. The second interim OS analysis used November 29, 2024. Time-to-event tests were stratified by pre-chemoradiotherapy stage. Reported post-progression P values are nominal; the second interim OS analysis was assigned a two-sided nominal alpha of 0.000001.
- Established primary context: the previously reported BICR PFS was 39.1 versus 5.6 months (HR 0.16, 95% CI 0.10-0.24; `P<0.001`). This strong primary PFS result should not be conflated with the less mature post-progression or OS analyses. The first interim OS result at the primary cutoff was HR 0.81 (0.42-1.56; `P=0.53`).
- TFST: 53/143 (37%) osimertinib and 61/73 (84%) placebo patients had an event (53% maturity). Median TFST was 43.8 months (95% CI 38.9-NC) versus 9.5 (6.6-11.5), HR 0.13 (0.08-0.21), nominal `P<0.001`. This is a statistically clear delay in first subsequent therapy or death under the endpoint definition.
- PFS2 uncertainty and source alert: 34/143 (24%) and 24/73 (33%) patients were reported with PFS2 events (27% maturity). Median PFS2 was 48.2 months (44.4-NC) versus 47.4 (28.2-NC), HR 0.62 (0.35-1.08), nominal `P=0.088`. The CI includes 1 and the nominal test is nonsignificant; “improved PFS2” must therefore be rendered as a numerical trend. The Discussion further states that each median was determined by a single event at the Kaplan-Meier tail. Figure 2's caption also discloses that one osimertinib patient was misclassified as a PFS2 event because the second progression preceded subsequent treatment. The reported analysis is retained as published but not treated as a corrected estimate.
- TSST: 32/143 (22%) and 24/73 (33%) patients had events (26% maturity). Median TSST was not reached (95% CI 44.4-NC) versus 47.4 months (34.1-NC), HR 0.51 (0.28-0.91), nominal `P=0.022`. The result is favorable but remains a secondary, nominal, immature endpoint.
- Subsequent-treatment denominator chain: randomized treatment had been discontinued in 63/143 (44%) and 66/73 (90%). Of those discontinued patients, 42/63 (67%) and 57/66 (86%) received subsequent anticancer therapy. First subsequent EGFR-TKI use was 22/63 (35%) versus 56/66 (85%), primarily osimertinib in 14/63 (22%) versus 50/66 (76%). Figure 1 separately reports post-BICR-progression osimertinib among the progression denominators as 15/53 (28%) and 50/62 (81%). These are different denominator frames and must not be interchanged.
- Second interim OS: 40/143 (28%) and 26/73 (36%) deaths yielded 31% maturity. Median OS was 58.8 months (54.1-NC) versus 54.0 (42.1-NC), HR 0.67 (0.40-1.14), `P=0.140`. OS remained nonsignificant and far above the prespecified interim nominal alpha; “trend toward benefit” is acceptable only with immaturity, CI, and nonsignificance attached. Final OS was planned at about 60% maturity.
- Progression-pattern context: the main text reports new lesions in 24% versus 59% and new brain lesions in 13% versus 33%; detailed denominators reside in an unreviewed supplementary table. These descriptive results are not a substitute for a formally tested CNS/post-progression endpoint.
- Safety: this post-progression report does not present a new safety analysis; no safety conclusion is transferred from absence of tables.
- Claim ceiling: LAURA establishes a large primary PFS benefit and a clear TFST delay. This report additionally shows nominally favorable TSST and numerical PFS2/OS patterns, but PFS2 and OS are not statistically significant, PFS2 is immature with a disclosed event misclassification and unstable medians, OS is immature, and placebo crossover was high. It does not establish a significant OS or PFS2 benefit at these cutoffs.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results states the PFS2 estimate as a “38% reduction” but provides `P=0.088`; the Discussion adds that each median rests on one tail event and should be interpreted cautiously (pp15-16 and 20/Figure 2 p33). The OS paragraph uses “trend” while reporting 31% maturity, HR 0.67 (0.40-1.14), and `P=0.140` (p18/Figure 3 p34).
- Context-checked method frame: PFS2 allowed radiologic, symptomatic, or death events under local investigator practice, with no formal RECIST measurements collected after first BICR progression (pp13-14); it is not directly interchangeable with the primary BICR PFS endpoint.
- Safe template: “[Endpoint] numerically favored [treatment] (HR [estimate], 95% CI [interval]; nominal `P=[value]`), but the comparison did not reach statistical significance and remained immature, so the finding is best interpreted as a directional signal.”
- Unsafe transfer: saying PFS2 or OS improved significantly; omitting the one-patient PFS2 event misclassification; treating tail medians as robust; mixing discontinued-treatment, BICR-progression, and randomized denominators; or attributing post-progression patterns solely to randomized treatment despite crossover and subsequent therapies.

### Six gates and verdict

- `coverage_check=pass`; `evidence_check=pass`; `results_check=pass`; `language_check=pass`; `traceability_check=pass`; `transfer_check=pass`.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: EVOKE-02 is an open-label global multicenter multicohort phase II study. This report describes first-line sacituzumab govitecan (SG), pembrolizumab, and carboplatin in cohort C (nonsquamous) and D (squamous) metastatic NSCLC without known actionable alterations. There was no concurrent control arm. EGFR and ALK testing was mandatory; testing for other alterations was not mandated despite the no-known-actionable-alteration eligibility language.
- Regimen/dose decision: SG was given on days 1 and 8, pembrolizumab 200 mg and carboplatin AUC5 on day 1 of 21-day cycles, with carboplatin for four cycles. Five patients entered the initial 10-mg/kg safety run-in. Although the formal de-escalation criterion was not met because only one protocol DLT occurred (fatal sepsis), planned review led to a 7.5-mg/kg SG recommendation because grade 3 or higher neutropenia occurred in 19/29 (65.5%) patients treated at 10 mg/kg. This is a safety-driven dose selection, not evidence that toxicity was minimal.
- Analysis sets: 95 patients received treatment (cohort C 54, cohort D 41) and formed the safety set. Three cohort-C patients lacked measurable baseline disease by IRC and were excluded from efficacy, leaving 51 nonsquamous and 41 squamous patients. Median follow-up was 14.5 (range 12.2-22.3) and 14.2 (11.0-23.0) months. The 95-patient safety denominator and 92-patient efficacy denominator must remain separate.
- Efficacy by histology: IRC ORR was 23/51, 45.1% (95% CI 31.1-59.7), in nonsquamous disease and 16/41, 39.0% (24.2-55.5), in squamous disease; all responses were partial. Median PFS was 8.1 months (5.2-15.0) and 8.3 (4.3-11.2). Six-month PFS was 53.7% (37.8-67.2) and 64.6% (46.0-78.2). Median DOR was not reached (3.2-NR) and 11.5 months (5.6-NR). Seven and five patients were nonevaluable, respectively. OS was listed as a secondary endpoint but no OS estimate is reported in this main PDF.
- PD-L1 subgroup boundary: in the combined 92-patient efficacy set, ORR was 19/44, 43.2% (28.3-59.0), for TPS below 1%; 12/36, 33.3% (18.6-51.0), for TPS 1%-49%; and 8/12, 66.7% (34.9-90.1), for TPS at least 50%. Median PFS was 8.3 months (5.2-15.0), 6.8 (4.0-10.7), and NR (1.9-NR). These small descriptive strata had no formal comparison, adjusted model, or multiplicity framework; they do not validate PD-L1 as a predictive modifier of the triplet.
- Safety at 7.5 mg/kg: all 66 patients had a TEAE; 57/66 (86.4%) had grade 3 or higher events, 36/66 (54.5%) serious events, 12/66 (18.2%) an event leading to discontinuation of any study drug, and 27/66 (40.9%) an event leading to any dose reduction. Nine/66 (13.6%) died from a TEAE and five/66 (7.6%) had a treatment-related fatal TEAE. The most frequent grade 3 or higher events were anemia (31.8%), neutropenia (24.2%), and decreased neutrophil count (16.7%).
- Safety across doses: at 10 mg/kg (n=29), grade 3 or higher TEAEs occurred in 27 (93.1%), serious events in 18 (62.1%), discontinuation of any study drug in nine (31.0%), TEAE deaths in five (17.2%), and treatment-related fatal TEAEs in three (10.3%). Across all 95, the corresponding counts were 84 (88.4%), 54 (56.8%), 21 (22.1%), 14 (14.7%), and eight (8.4%). The fatal-event footnote lists more event diagnoses than patients, compatible with multiple fatal events in a patient; diagnoses must not be recounted as unique patients.
- Benefit-risk and comparator boundary: the Discussion appropriately cautions against cross-trial comparisons, then notes that observed activity did not appear superior to KEYNOTE historical results and that triplet therapy had more myelosuppression than a separately reported SG/pembrolizumab doublet. Because neither comparison was randomized within these cohorts, statements that carboplatin “did not confer additional efficacy” or that the triplet “failed” versus standard care are cross-trial interpretations, not causal comparative estimates. Likewise, calling adverse events “manageable” must be accompanied by the high grade 3-or-higher, serious, and fatal-event rates.
- Claim ceiling: the triplet showed uncontrolled antitumor activity and generated a 7.5-mg/kg SG dose choice after substantial myelosuppression at 10 mg/kg, but toxicity remained high at 7.5 mg/kg. The study does not establish superiority, added benefit from SG or carboplatin, a PD-L1 treatment interaction, or comparative safety against standard chemoimmunotherapy.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results explains that a formal DLT threshold was not reached yet the dose was reduced at planned safety review because of myelosuppression (p14). The Discussion explicitly says cross-trial comparisons require caution and describes efficacy as not superior/failed to show promise relative to historical standards (pp16-19).
- Context-checked safety frame: Table 4 reports grade 3-or-higher, serious, discontinuation, reduction, all-cause TEAE death, and treatment-related TEAE death separately by 7.5 mg/kg, 10 mg/kg, and all doses (p26); the abstract's 86.4% and 18.2% figures apply specifically to the 66-patient 7.5-mg/kg group.
- Safe template: “[Regimen] produced an ORR of [estimate] in this single-arm cohort; however, the absence of a concurrent comparator precludes attributing incremental efficacy to [added component], and interpretation must incorporate the observed grade 3-or-higher and fatal treatment-related toxicities.”
- Unsafe transfer: using all 95 patients as the efficacy denominator; claiming superiority or a causal lack of carboplatin benefit from cross-trial comparisons; calling the PD-L1-high subgroup predictive; describing the regimen as broadly tolerable without the fatal toxicity; or treating listed fatal-event diagnoses as distinct patients.
