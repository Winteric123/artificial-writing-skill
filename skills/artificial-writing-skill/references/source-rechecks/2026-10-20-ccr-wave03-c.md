## PMID 39887260 — Round-Robin Comparison of RET Rearrangement Detection in ctDNA: A Novel Method for Limited Clinical Samples

**Identity and provenance.** Issue year 2025; supplied version `publisher_main_pdf`; SHA-256 `abacdebcfa699a6b1d8078b9fcdb4a5bdfd96e7924b959e49ea110a44177be3c`; 7 physical pages. Supplement status is `not_supplied`; no supplement was reviewed.

**Coverage.** Abstract/Introduction: pp. 1–2; Materials and Methods: pp. 2–3; Results: pp. 3–5; Discussion: pp. 5–6; references/end matter: pp. 6–7. Main visuals checked with captions: Fig. 1 (p. 3), Fig. 2 (p. 4), and Table 1 (p. 5). All physical pages and main panels were legible.

**Question, design, and analysis sets.** This was a blinded round-robin analytic comparison using banked pretreatment plasma from 60 treatment-naive, tissue-confirmed RET fusion-positive advanced NSCLC cases enrolled in LIBRETTO-431. Six milliliters of plasma were split into two 3-mL aliquots, and each of three laboratories received 40 samples with 20-patient pairwise overlaps. Assays were Guardant360, an investigational lower-input FoundationOne Liquid CDx workflow, and Resolution ctDx-First. Sequencing succeeded for 40/40, 36/40, and 37/40 samples, respectively. The small, incompletely overlapping assay sets and below-specification input preclude a formal assay ranking.

**Conclusion-driving results.** RET rearrangements were detected in 24/40 (60.0%) Guardant360, 23/36 (63.9%) FoundationOne, and 25/37 (67.6%) ctDx-First evaluable samples. Across pairwise-evaluable cases, either assay detected RET in 40/55 (72.3%). Unblinded manual review increased calls to 27/40 and 28/37 for Guardant360 and ctDx-First, but used nonstandard retrospective adjudication and was not performed for FoundationOne. Plasma–tumor partner concordance was 15/18, 17/21, and 16/18 in assay-specific evaluable subsets. Fifteen cases were negative on both paired assays; ten had no other plasma alterations, compatible with low shedding, while five had another alteration at 0.2%–2.6% VAF. Without matched leukocytes, clonal hematopoiesis cannot be excluded for these ancillary calls.

Exploratory tumor-content analyses were directionally consistent with reduced sensitivity at low ctDNA fraction, but assay-specific measures and denominators were heterogeneous. For example, Guardant360 positive percent agreement was 14/15 when maximum VAF exceeded 1%, whereas negative agreement was 13/25 below 1%; Resolution classified 28 samples at purity at most 0.02, including 16 true positives and 12 false negatives; FoundationOne reported all 15 positives above 1% tumor fraction and all 13 negatives below 1%. These are within-assay exploratory observations, not common-threshold performance comparisons.

**Clinical context, limitations, and claim ceiling.** LIBRETTO-431 treatment outcomes in the sampled subset provide representativeness context only and do not test whether assay detection modifies selpercatinib benefit. The strongest defensible claim is that, under a constrained 3-mL plasma input, the three commercial workflows detected approximately 60%–68% of known tissue-positive RET rearrangements and that negative plasma testing should prompt tissue reflex when feasible. The study does not establish assay equivalence or superiority, and retrospective manual review is not a validated clinical workflow.

**Source alerts and disposition.** The abstract says discordant variants were usually below 0.5% allele frequency, whereas the Results and Discussion state below 0.05%. The exact discordance threshold is therefore withheld. The article also uses different tumor-content constructs across platforms, has no matched-WBC sequencing, and used lower plasma input than validated specifications for some assays. These limits are explicit, and no exact cross-platform threshold or ranking is transferred.

**Language samples and transfer.**

- Methods, pp. 2–3, constrained-input comparison: safe frame, “Aliquots from the same specimen were distributed across [assays], with assay-specific evaluability retained in all denominators.”
- Results, pp. 3–5, positive result plus boundary: safe frame, “[Target] was detected in [n/N] evaluable samples by [assay]; differences across platforms should be interpreted descriptively because input requirements and evaluable sets differed.”
- Discussion, pp. 5–6, negative-test implication: safe frame, “Although plasma testing identified [finding] in a majority of evaluable cases, incomplete sensitivity—particularly at low tumor content—supports reflex tissue testing after a negative result.”
- Unsafe transfer: “Assay A is more sensitive than assays B and C, and retrospective manual review should be used routinely.” The study was not powered or harmonized for ranking, and manual review was unequal and nonstandard.

## PMID 40553459 — Multidimensional Analysis of B7 Homolog 3 RNA Expression in Small Cell Lung Cancer Molecular Subtypes

**Identity and provenance.** Issue year 2025; supplied version `not_classified_by_header`; SHA-256 `f402ec2a720d530a7a06fe3d071c7b30ba027e13806ee9018a332cd2851d9fd5`; 7 physical pages. Supplement status is `not_recorded`; no separate supplement was supplied or reviewed.

**Question, design, and analysis sets.** This was a retrospective real-world transcriptomic study of 1,721 Caris-profiled SCLC tumors, with clinical annotations available for 930 and a separate 212-case pulmonary neuroendocrine tumor comparison set. FFPE whole-transcriptome sequencing, a gene-ratio subtype classifier based on 2,000 models, quanTIseq immune deconvolution, PD-L1 IHC, and tumor mutational burden were used. Samples with less than 50% subtype-model consensus were “equivocal,” leaving a sizeable unresolved group. Bulk RNA and inferred cell fractions are not cell-surface protein measurements.

**Omics results.** Subtypes were SCLC-A 848 (49.3%), SCLC-N 202 (11.7%), SCLC-P 142 (8.3%), SCLC-I 291 (16.9%), and equivocal 238 (13.8%). B7-H3 RNA was broadly expressed, with a median of 16.8 TPM (range 0–68.2), and neither the overall nor pairwise subtype comparisons were significant after the reported adjustment (`q>0.05`). This is a null subtype-difference result, not evidence of equivalence. DLL3 and SEZ6 varied across subtypes (`q<0.0001`), generally highest in SCLC-A and lowest in SCLC-P.

Prior-immunotherapy cases had a median B7-H3 value of 18.7 TPM (95% CI 16.5–21.2; n=118), versus 17.3 (16.4–18.1; n=812) without prior immunotherapy, but no comparison p value was presented. **Limitations and claim ceiling.** The strongest defensible claim is that B7-H3 transcript abundance was broadly distributed across this commercial SCLC cohort and did not differ significantly by assigned transcriptomic subtype, while exploratory bulk-RNA associations linked it to selected immune signatures. The study does not establish B7-H3 protein abundance, cell-surface localization, response to a B7-H3-directed ADC, treatment prediction, or prognosis. The 13.8% equivocal classifier group, bulk-tissue deconvolution, lack of independent validation, absence of outcome modeling, and unadjusted correlation screen all limit transfer.

**Source alerts and disposition.** The abstract states that B7-H3 was “not correlated with a T-cell signature,” but the Results report a significant correlation with the T-cell-inflamed signature (`r=.55`) and a nonsignificant IFN-gamma signature; the Discussion separately refers to absence of correlation with inferred T-cell infiltration. These are three different constructs. The abstract phrase is withheld; templates must name the exact signature or inferred cell compartment. Prior-immunotherapy and PD-L1-quartile summaries are not inferential comparisons.

- Results, pp. 3–5, null subtype comparison: safe frame, “[Marker] was broadly expressed across subtypes, with no significant overall or pairwise differences after adjustment; this null result does not establish equivalence.”

**Identity and provenance.** Issue year 2025; supplied version `not_classified_by_header`; SHA-256 `9a61e35a2b031e196a0a192f4eef601773583f9302686d7d56f5da783dd09d33`; 14 physical pages. Supplement status is `not_recorded`; no separate supplement was supplied or reviewed.

**Question, design, and analysis sets.** Two open-label pilot studies evaluated HLA-A*02-restricted, NY-ESO-1/LAGE-1a-directed letetresgene autoleucel after lymphodepletion in advanced NSCLC: study 208749 was single-arm lete-cel monotherapy, and study 208471 was phase Ib/IIa with monotherapy arm A and actionable-driver arm C assigned lete-cel plus planned pembrolizumab; arm B never opened. More than 2,500 patients were screened. In study 208471, 738/1,638 (45%) tested patients were HLA-A*02 positive; NY-ESO-1 and reflex LAGE-1a positivity were 62/525 (12%) and 15/348 (4%). Across studies, 43 patients underwent leukapheresis and 18 received lete-cel: five in the single-arm study and 13 in the multiarm study. Efficacy and treatment-dependent safety used the mITT treated sets. The multiarm study ended early without reaching interim-analysis criteria or its planned 15 evaluable patients per arm.

**Feasibility, activity, and pharmacokinetics.** In the single-arm n=5 study, one confirmed PR produced ORR 20% (95% CI 0.5%–71.6%), with one SD and three PD; the response lasted 18 months after first infusion. In multiarm A (n=7), six had SD and one PD; in arm C (n=6), two had SD, three PD, and one was nonevaluable, with no confirmed PR/CR. Median PFS was 1.81 months (95% CI 0.46–18.23), 5.32 months (1.45–5.52), and 1.48 months (0.62–2.83) in the single-arm, arm A, and arm C sets; these medians are descriptive and not arm comparisons. Cell expansion occurred across patients, but the one-responder versus four-nonresponder comparison cannot validate a kinetic correlate.

**Safety.** There were no fatal TEAEs. In the single-arm study, 4/5 (80%) had at least one serious TEAE and 2/5 (40%) had grade 3 or higher serious TEAEs; 3/5 (60%) had an AESI. In multiarm A and C, any AESI occurred in 6/7 (86%) and 6/6 (100%), grade 3 or higher AESI in 5/7 (71%) and 4/6 (67%), and serious TEAEs in 5/7 (71%) and 3/6 (50%). CRS occurred in 2/5 single-arm patients and 9/13 multiarm patients; ICANS and pancytopenia also occurred. All AESIs were considered related and reported resolved. Cytopenias reflect both the cellular product context and preceding lymphodepletion; the study cannot isolate component-specific causality. Seven of 13 multiarm patients received at least one pembrolizumab dose, with delays/nonreceipt in others, so cross-arm toxicity comparisons are unreliable.

**Limitations and claim ceiling.** The strongest defensible claim is that lete-cel delivery was feasible in a highly selected minority after extensive screening, with expected cytopenias/CRS and limited antitumor activity in 18 treated patients, including one durable PR. The study does not establish efficacy, pembrolizumab benefit, comparative safety, or an antigen-expression threshold. The 12% NY-ESO-1 estimate derives from an investigational assay and selected tissue submitters; the 4% LAGE-1a estimate is reflex-tested and is not population prevalence.
