### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: single-center phase I continual reassessment method dose-escalation study of olaparib given twice daily with consolidative thoracic radiotherapy (TRT; 30 Gy in 10 fractions) after induction platinum/etoposide for extensive-stage SCLC. Twenty-four patients were treated across eight three-patient cohorts; two additional enrolled patients were not DLT evaluable because one withdrew before treatment and one developed symptomatic brain metastases after one week of olaparib but before TRT.
- Comparator/analysis sets: no randomized efficacy comparator. DLT evaluability required completion of TRT and at least 90% of planned olaparib. The DLT window extended through three weeks after the last TRT fraction. Later toxicities were recorded but did not drive the CRM. Exploratory atezolizumab (n=10 versus n=14) and PCI (n=4 versus n=20) comparisons were nonrandomized and underpowered.

### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: retrospective International NUT Carcinoma Registry study of 116 centrally/pathologically confirmed NUT carcinoma cases with at least one standard-of-care broad-panel DNA (>80 genes), ctDNA (>80 genes), or RNA fusion test and an available primary report. The DNA cohort comprised 100 patients with qualifying tissue/ctDNA data; the exon-fusion cohort comprised 46 with at least one exact exon breakpoint.
- Comparator/analysis sets: assay “detection rates” were calculated among already diagnosed, selectively tested cases and varied by whether the assay covered NUTM1/common partners. Survival began at NC diagnosis and compared the first diagnostic modality, with adjustment only for thoracic versus nonthoracic primary site. Mutation denominators varied by gene coverage; “clinical interest” could include ClinVar VUS after manual review.

- Positive findings: within this selected registry, RNA fusion testing, NUT IHC, and NUTM1 FISH had higher observed diagnostic yield than DNA NGS; diverse fusion transcripts retained key domains; and the study catalogued available-case co-alterations/pathways.
- Negative/uncertain findings: survival did not significantly differ by diagnostic modality; no significant recurrent copy-number pattern was found; PD-L1 benefit remained uncertain; most cases lacked a clearly actionable second driver; multiple reported denominators conflicted; and no treatment-response analysis was performed.
- Safety findings: not applicable; this retrospective molecular/diagnostic study did not evaluate an intervention or adverse events.
- Source alerts: (1) BRD4 exon 4 in Translational Relevance conflicts with exon 11 in Results/Figure 2; exon 11 is retained; (2) PD-L1 is 13/66 in the abstract/figure but 16/73 in Results/Table 1; (3) TMB has n=71 in the abstract/figure and n=73 in Results/Table 1; (4) retrospective detection yield in preconfirmed selected cases is not formal sensitivity/specificity, and IHC 100% is vulnerable to incorporation/verification bias; (5) the Discussion's suggestion that IHC may improve survival is not supported by nonsignificant adjusted or unadjusted analyses; and (6) pathway/actionability wording must account for variable coverage and included VUS.
- Limitations: registry selection; requirement for a clinical molecular report; no raw sequencing; heterogeneous panels/coverage; variable gene denominators; ctDNA inclusion only when an NC fusion was detected; partial matched-germline testing; inclusion of VUS; retrospective testing and survival biases; possible incorporation/verification bias; no missed-case denominator; sparse subgroups; minimal confounder adjustment; no treatment-response data; no supplied supplement.
- Claim ceiling: in this selected registry of confirmed NUT carcinoma, RNA fusion assays and NUT-directed IHC/FISH showed higher observed fusion-detection yield than DNA NGS, and available-case profiling described heterogeneous fusion architecture and co-alterations. The study does not estimate population-level diagnostic accuracy, show a survival benefit from IHC-first diagnosis, or validate co-alterations/PD-L1 as therapeutic biomarkers.
