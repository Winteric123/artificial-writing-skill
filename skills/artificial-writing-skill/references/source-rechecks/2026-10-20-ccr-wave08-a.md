### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: retrospective two-center cohort assembled from 2,794 patients with advanced NSCLC treated from 2011–2022. The analytic discontinuation cohort comprised 271 patients who permanently stopped PD-(L)1 therapy, alone or with another immune-modulatory agent, for an irAE and had CR, PR, or SD as best response. PD-(L)1 plus chemotherapy/targeted-therapy regimens and grade 5 irAEs were excluded.
- Comparator/analysis sets: comparisons were descriptive and observational. Post-discontinuation PFS began at the last ICI cycle; patients who started another therapy without progression and those treated beyond progression before irAE discontinuation were excluded from that endpoint. Post-discontinuation OS also began at the last ICI cycle. The primary duration strata were less than 3, 3–6, and more than 6 months; a 12-month modified landmark and time-varying Cox model attempted to reduce time-dependent bias. PD-L1 and TMB were available only in subsets, and TMB missingness was addressed with center-specific inverse probability weighting.

- Positive findings: prolonged disease control occurred in a subset after irAE-driven ICI discontinuation; longer prior treatment, objective response, and selected clinicopathologic features were associated with better post-discontinuation outcomes.
- Negative/uncertain findings: sex, smoking, line, ECOG PS, ICI type, baseline autoimmune disease, steroid exposure, and immunosuppression type were not associated with post-discontinuation outcomes; TMB was not associated with OS; potentially life-threatening irAEs did not affect PFS; ICI rechallenge did not improve OS; and several subgroup estimates were borderline or imprecise.
- Safety findings: 40.8% of grade-evaluable discontinuations followed grade 3–4 toxicity; 42.4% were classified as potentially life-threatening. On rechallenge, 12/34 had immune toxicity and 7/34 discontinued again. These data describe toxicity after a selected clinician decision and do not establish a safe rechallenge policy.
- Source alerts: (1) Figure 1's panel footnote and Results state irAE grade was available for 267 patients, but the caption says 268; retain 267 and withhold 268; (2) Figure 2 uses 243 for PFS and 264 for OS, while the full cohort is 271; keep endpoint-specific denominators; (3) Table 1 totals 235 because eight PFS-evaluable patients were censored before six months; (4) treatment-duration groups intrinsically select patients who survived/responded long enough to enter later strata, and statistical adjustments do not create causality; (5) 33/189 and 34/271 rechallenge denominators describe different pathways and are not contradictory; and (6) the authors' generally-safe rechallenge language is stronger than a 34-patient selected series with recurrent toxicity.
- Limitations: retrospective academic-center cohort; response-enriched cohort excluding primary progressors and grade 5 irAEs; no continue-versus-stop comparator; guaranteed-time and residual confounding; incomplete PD-L1/TMB; TMB platform normalization and IPW assumptions; no steroid-dose data; heterogeneous ICI regimens; small rechallenge subgroup; selected clinical decisions; supplemental analyses not independently reviewed because supplement status was not recorded.
- Claim ceiling: among selected advanced NSCLC patients who permanently stopped ICI for irAEs after CR/PR/SD, prior response and longer exposure were associated with longer subsequent disease control and survival. The study does not establish a causal 6–9-month treatment threshold, identify a prospectively validated discontinuation rule, prove that stopping is noninferior to continuing, or show that rechallenge is broadly safe or survival-improving.

### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: retrospective quantitative immunofluorescence study of HER2, TROP2, HER3, and EGFR protein expression in three NSCLC tissue-microarray cohorts: EGFR-mutant n=83, EGFR-wild-type n=128, and EGFR-status-unknown n=232. Assays were converted from fluorescence scores to amol/mm² using LC-MS/MS-calibrated cell lines and assay-specific limits of detection/quantification.
- Comparator/analysis sets: EGFR-mutant versus wild-type, median-defined EGFR-low versus EGFR-high within annotated cohorts, and pairwise target correlations. Missing/excluded cores varied by target because of artifacts, low tumor, or tissue exhaustion; exact analyzable denominators were target-specific. The clinical-outcome analysis was restricted to 50 first-line EGFR-TKI-treated EGFR-mutant patients and used unadjusted log-rank tests/HRs with data-driven median cutoffs. No patient received an ADC in the evaluated cohort.

### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: multimodel preclinical study linking repeated intratracheal NNK/BaP exposure to lung inflammation/carcinogenesis and examining IRAK4 biology. Seventy-five male ICR mice were randomized 2:1 to carcinogens (n=50) or PBS (n=25); approximately three mice per arm were sampled at 4, 8, 12, and 18 months. - Positive findings: repeated intratracheal NNK/BaP exposure produced inflammation, worse survival, and lung cancers in ICR mice; IRAK4 expression was observed in mouse lesions and human SCLC; IRAK4 genetic/pharmacologic perturbation affected invasion, a MYH9 phosphosite, and H23 xenograft growth.
- Negative/uncertain findings: Table 1 did not document dysplasia/cancer at 8 months; exact early-exposure duration was internally inconsistent; the SCLC series had no comparator/outcome; phosphoproteomic replication and uncertainty were unclear; the xenograft result lacked an exact statistic; and the causal bridge between chronic carcinogen exposure and IRAK4 inhibition was never tested.
- Safety findings: no toxicity, pharmacokinetic, organ-pathology, hematology, or patient adverse-event analysis of emavusertib was reported. Tumor growth in five mice per arm is not a safety assessment.
- Source alerts: (1) repeated dosing is described as three times weekly for 110 weeks, yet serial animals were killed after 4, 8, 12, and 18 months; do not assign 110 weeks of exposure to those early samples; (2) text states 8-month dysplasia, whereas Table 1 records only congestion/NAD at 8 months and first dysplasia/cancer at 12 months; retain the table; (3) the IL-1β assay used commercial mouse lung macrophages exposed to NNK/BaP, while Discussion says lung epithelial cells exposed to cigarette-smoking condensate; that sentence is withheld; (4) the human TMA is SCLC n=43, not an unselected lung-cancer cohort, and smoking status/controls/outcomes are absent; (5) main-Figure 5 lacks uncertainty/exact P for MYH9, and the pooled phosphoproteomic design/multiplicity is insufficiently specified; (6) Figure 6 supplies no exact xenograft statistic; and (7) IRAK4 was never perturbed in the chronic carcinogen model, so the title/Discussion causal chain and prevention framing exceed direct evidence.
- Limitations: artificial high-frequency intratracheal exposure; inconsistent exposure-duration wording; only about three pathology mice per arm/time point; descriptive representative IHC; pooled time points; commercial macrophages rather than in-vivo myeloid perturbation; SCLC-only TMA n=43 without control/smoking/outcomes; microtubule-enrichment contamination not excluded; pooled phosphoproteomics and unreported biological replication; uncorrected feature-wise testing; missing-value imputation with normality assumed; one cell lineage/two clones without rescue; unrelated subcutaneous NSG xenograft n=5 per arm; no carcinogen-model IRAK4 intervention; no patient efficacy or safety; supplement not supplied.
- Claim ceiling: repeated NNK/BaP exposure produced lung inflammation and cancer in an ICR-mouse model, while separate cell and H23-xenograft experiments support IRAK4-associated invasion and drug-sensitive growth. These data generate a preclinical IRAK4 hypothesis but do not prove a single causal carcinogen→IRAK4→carcinogenesis pathway, demonstrate prevention/treatment in the carcinogen model, establish a smoking-specific human biomarker, or show clinical efficacy or safety.
