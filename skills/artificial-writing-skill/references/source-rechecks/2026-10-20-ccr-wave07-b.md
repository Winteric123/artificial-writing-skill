### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design/model chain: this is a preclinical medicinal-chemistry and translational-model study of the covalent EGFR/HER2 exon 20 inhibitor STX-721. The evidence chain combines X-ray crystallography and molecular dynamics, biochemical and chemoproteomic assays, engineered Ba/F3 and human cancer-cell systems, and cell line– and patient-derived xenografts. There is no treated-patient cohort.
- Mechanistic premise: structural and molecular-dynamics analyses supported mutation-dependent differences in kinase conformational states. Near-loop exon 20 variants such as ASV and SVD favored active/intermediate states, whereas wild-type EGFR more often sampled an out-state with pocket collapse; the reported pocket-volume range was approximately 70–103 Å³. These modeled states support a binding rationale but are not clinical target-engagement data.
- Biochemical/selectivity result: STX-721 engaged C797 and showed focused activity across EGFR/HER2 exon 20 mutant systems, with a kinome screen at 3 µmol/L supporting relative selectivity for EGFR-family targets. Covalent engagement and biochemical potency do not by themselves establish a therapeutic window in patients.
- Cellular selectivity: the stated desired benchmark was approximately 29-fold mutant-to-wild-type selectivity for osimertinib in a Ba/F3 L858R/T790M context and approximately sixfold in human cancer-cell comparisons. STX-721 showed its clearest selectivity for several near-loop variants. Selectivity was lower for far-loop H and HV insertions; the NPH human-cancer model showed about fourfold selectivity, below the cited benchmark. Adding C797S reduced or abolished the ASV selectivity advantage. These negative/boundary results preclude a pan–exon 20 claim.
- Xenograft result: dose-dependent tumor control or regression was reported across several EGFR and HER2 exon 20 models, including LU0387 NPH (n=10/group; pharmacodynamic subset n=3), CTG-2842 (n=8/group), MGH10080 (n=8/group), MGH10022 (generally n=10/group, vehicle n=9), and the HER2 model LU11717 (n=9/group). In LU0387, 12.5–25 mg/kg produced partial regression and 50–100 mg/kg full regression in the displayed experiment. These are model-specific tumor-volume results rather than objective response rates or patient efficacy.
- Isogenic model: Figure 6 used parental/wild-type and SVD knock-in tumor models with n=6/group to examine selectivity in vivo. The Results text cross-references Figure 6A and 6B in reverse: visual inspection shows Figure 6A as the wild-type/parental model and Figure 6B as the SVD knock-in model. The biological narrative is consistent with the plotted content, but the panel locator is source-internally wrong and should be traced by content, not the printed text reference.
- Tumor-volume formula alert: the Figure 6 caption prints a percentage tumor-volume formula using `TV_final` in the denominator. Because this is an atypical and potentially erroneous denominator, no derived value should be recalculated from that formula without source clarification.
- Tolerability boundary: the animal experiments include body-weight/tolerability observations under the tested schedules, but these do not constitute a clinical safety profile. The paper provides no human adverse-event, exposure–toxicity, or dose-optimization evidence.
- Positive result: the integrated structural, biochemical, cellular, and xenograft evidence supports variant-dependent preclinical activity and a mutant-selective design rationale, particularly for selected near-loop EGFR/HER2 exon 20 alterations.
- Negative/uncertain result: selectivity was not uniform across insertion classes; far-loop H/HV activity was weaker, NPH selectivity was below the cited human-cell benchmark, and C797S compromised ASV selectivity. The work does not establish CNS exposure/activity, patient response, clinical durability, or human safety.
- Claim ceiling: the source supports a preclinical mechanism-and-model statement: STX-721 exploited mutation-associated kinase-state differences and produced antitumor activity in selected engineered and xenograft systems. It does not support clinical efficacy, superiority to approved exon 20 therapies, a class-wide claim across all insertions, human tolerability, CNS efficacy, or a validated predictive biomarker.

### Language and transfer

- Methods/mechanistic triangulation (pp. 2–6): the paper moves from structural dynamics to biochemical target engagement, then to cellular selectivity and in vivo models. Reusable logic: identify what each assay contributes and avoid treating a modeled conformation as clinical proof.
- Results/variant boundary (pp. 8–10): favorable near-loop activity is reported alongside weaker far-loop selectivity and C797S escape. Reusable logic: name the molecularly defined positive subset and the observed exception in the same result paragraph.
- Results/model-specific efficacy (pp. 10–13): each tumor-volume claim is attached to model, dose, and group size. Reusable logic: use “tumor regression in [model]” rather than patient-response vocabulary.
- Discussion/translational restraint (pp. 13–15): the clinical promise is framed as a development rationale, not as demonstrated patient benefit. Reusable logic: separate evidence of a preclinical therapeutic window from evidence of a clinical therapeutic window.
- Safe transfer template: `[Agent] showed variant-dependent biochemical and cellular selectivity and induced tumor regression in selected xenograft models; weaker activity in [variant/model] and the absence of clinical data limit the finding to a preclinical development rationale.`
- Unsafe transfer: claiming efficacy or safety in patients; generalizing across every EGFR/HER2 exon 20 insertion; describing the Figure 6 panels using the inverted text locator without checking their content; calculating new tumor-control values from the caption’s suspect denominator; or claiming CNS activity, comparative superiority, or resistance prevention.

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Concordance between Tumor Tissue and Plasma DNA Genotyping in the NCI-MATCH Trial (EAY131)*.
- Year: 2025.
- Supplied version: author manuscript.
- Registered PDF: `Gouda_NCI_MATCH_tissue_plasma_concordance_PMID40388547.pdf`.
- SHA-256: `7e65c9ba1fa23e5a530c6200390493b9f54dd650e4181833d3b416572f53f1db`.
- Physical pages: 25/25 visually reviewed; no unreadable page.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`. Supplement-dependent assay and subgroup detail was not inferred.
- Section map: title/administrative material p. 1; abstract/Introduction p. 2; Introduction/Methods p. 3; Methods pp. 3–5; Results pp. 5–8; Discussion/closing synthesis pp. 9–11; references pp. 12–14; Translational Relevance p. 15; Figure 1 p. 16; Figure 2 p. 17 with caption continuation p. 18; Figure 3 p. 19; Figure 4 p. 20; Figure 5 p. 21; Table 1 pp. 22–23; Table 2 pp. 24–25. There is no standalone Conclusion.
- Main-display coverage: Figures 1–5, the continued Figure 2 caption, and Tables 1–2 were visually checked against the text.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design/population: this cross-tumor NCI-MATCH analysis asked how often plasma cell-free DNA detected an actionable mutation of interest already established by tissue testing, and explored associations with molecular burden and clinical outcomes. It is not a paired screening study of an unselected population and does not estimate bidirectional diagnostic agreement.
- Cohort flow: 397 selected patients had plasma available; 12 were not treated, leaving 385. Twenty-two samples fell outside the designated collection window, leaving 363; 57 actionable mutations were not covered by the plasma panel and six samples failed quality control, leaving 300 analytic pairs. Of these, 243 were contemporaneous/centrally collected and 57 were noncontemporaneous or externally collected.
- Assay/reference boundary: plasma was assayed with a 74-gene Guardant360 panel and tissue with an Oncomine panel; 65 genes overlapped. The tissue-positive actionable mutation was the reference target. Therefore, “concordance” in this report is the conditional plasma detection rate among tissue-positive cases, not symmetric positive/negative agreement, sensitivity/specificity in a diagnostic population, or the ability of a negative plasma test to exclude an actionable alteration.
- Primary detection result: plasma detected the tissue-defined actionable mutation in 197/243 central contemporaneous cases (81.1%) and 32/57 noncontemporaneous/external cases (56.1%). The lower latter estimate occurred with a median tissue-to-plasma interval of 207 days (IQR 361 days), so timing and collection context must remain attached to the comparison.
- Molecular burden: across the analytic cohort, median cell-free DNA input was 9.6 ng/mL. There were 2,409 called alterations in 298 patients, including 1,750 somatic and 659 germline calls; 280 patients had a somatic alteration. In central cases, maximum plasma variant allele fraction was higher when the actionable mutation was detected than when it was not (8.5% vs 0.6%, P<0.001), whereas tissue variant allele fraction did not differ materially (51.4% vs 48.7%, P=0.204). This supports a shedding/burden explanation, not a tissue-assay efficacy claim.
- Disease setting: detection was higher for metastatic than primary tumor presentation (84.8% vs 45.5%, P<0.001). Tumor-type estimates are descriptive and often have small denominators.
- Alteration-class result in the central set: Table 2 reports detection of 52/63 copy-number variants (82.5%; 95% CI, 70.5%–90.6%), 8/10 fusions (80.0%; 44.2%–96.5%), 12/16 indels (75.0%; 47.4%–91.7%), and 125/154 SNVs (81.2%; 73.9%–86.8%). The apparent similarity does not establish equivalence among classes.
- Source-internal confidence-interval error: Table 2 reports sarcoma detection as 2/6 (33.3%) with a printed 95% CI of 60%–75.9%, which cannot contain the point estimate and is mathematically incompatible with the numerator/denominator. The CI is withheld as a source error; it is not silently replaced by an inferred value.
- Response analysis set: clinical response data were available for 194 central cases, but only 160 were response-evaluable: complete response n=1, partial response n=18, stable disease n=83, and progressive disease n=58. This denominator distinction must be retained.
- Response associations: maximum plasma VAF was higher in progressive disease than in the other response categories in several exploratory comparisons, including 14.7% versus 3.8% for progressive versus nonprogressive groups (P<0.001), and 5.5% versus 2.9% for absence versus presence of CR/PR (P=0.037). However, conditional actionable-mutation detection did not differ significantly across PD, SD, and PR (87.9%, 78.3%, and 77.8%; P=0.084). No arm-specific association between plasma detection and clinical outcome was established.
- Time-to-event associations: patients without detectable plasma actionable mutations had longer unadjusted PFS (median 175 vs 111 days; P=0.016) and OS (465 vs 221 days; P=0.001). Because shedding correlates with disease burden and tumor type and these analyses were unadjusted, they are prognostic associations, not evidence that the plasma result predicts benefit from the matched therapy.
- Molecular response: only 17 of 23 relevant cases were evaluable for the molecular-response analysis (PD 0/2, PR 3/5, SD 6/10 meeting the stated molecular-response definition). The very small denominators and missing evaluations preclude a validated monitoring claim.
- Multiplicity: the paper reports numerous VAF, disease-category, outcome, and subgroup comparisons without a clearly established family-wise or false-discovery adjustment for these clinical exploratory tests. Nominal P values should remain exploratory.
- Positive result: a contemporaneous plasma sample detected the already known tissue actionable mutation in about four of five central cases, and detection correlated with measurable plasma molecular burden.
- Negative/uncertain result: a substantial minority of tissue-positive alterations were not detected in plasma; detection fell in noncontemporaneous/external samples; clinical-outcome associations were unadjusted and not arm-specific; actionability detection did not differ significantly by best response; and the study cannot quantify specificity, negative predictive value, or true diagnostic agreement.
- Safety: no treatment-safety analysis was the focus of this genotyping study.
- Claim ceiling: the source supports conditional detection of a tissue-established actionable alteration under specified sampling conditions and exploratory prognostic associations with shedding/burden. It does not validate plasma as a stand-alone rule-out test, establish symmetric tissue–plasma diagnostic concordance, show treatment-effect prediction, justify substituting plasma for tissue in every negative case, or establish molecular response as a clinical surrogate.

### Language and transfer

- Methods/denominator definition (pp. 3–5): the analytic flow separates panel coverage, sample quality, collection timing, and central versus outside samples. Reusable logic: make each exclusion and resulting denominator visible before reporting a detection rate.
- Results/conditional detection (pp. 5–8 and Tables 1–2): the source starts from tissue-positive actionable mutations. Reusable logic: write “plasma detected the tissue-defined alteration in x/y” rather than using unqualified “concordance.”
- Results/negative finding (pp. 7–8): response categories show no statistically significant difference in actionable-mutation detection despite VAF differences. Reusable logic: separate a burden-related quantitative association from a negative endpoint-specific comparison.
- Discussion/prognostic boundary (pp. 9–11): outcome associations are interpreted in light of ctDNA shedding and disease burden. Reusable logic: label unadjusted survival separation as prognostic/hypothesis-generating unless a treatment interaction supports prediction.
- Safe transfer template: `Among patients with a tissue-defined actionable alteration, contemporaneous plasma testing detected the same target in [x/y]; because tissue-negative cases were not evaluated and non-detection tracked with lower shedding, this conditional detection rate should not be interpreted as symmetric diagnostic concordance or a rule-out result.`
- Unsafe transfer: reporting sensitivity/specificity, NPV, or full diagnostic agreement; concluding that a plasma-negative result excludes the target; treating unadjusted PFS/OS separation as predictive of matched-therapy benefit; presenting the sarcoma CI as valid; or ignoring the 194-versus-160 response denominator and 17-patient molecular-response set.

### Six gates and verdict

- Coverage check: **pass** — 25/25 pages, Figures 1–5, caption continuation, and Tables 1–2 were visually reviewed.
- Evidence check: **pass** — cohort flow, assay overlap, reference condition, collection timing, analysis sets, and absent supplement were reconstructed.
- Results check: **pass** — detection fractions, variant classes, response and survival denominators, negative result, exploratory multiplicity, and the impossible sarcoma CI were checked and bounded.
- Language check: **pass** — conditional-detection, prognostic, and non-rule-out wording was verified in context.
- Traceability check: **pass** — exact source identity, hash, page count, display locations, and source error are recorded.
- Transfer check: **pass** — the safe conditional-detection template and prohibited diagnostic/predictive transfers are explicit.
- Verdict: **passed**. The paper is suitable for denominator-aware plasma-detection and prognostic-association language, provided that conditional detection is never relabeled as full diagnostic concordance and the source-error CI is withheld.

## PMID 40378060

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Phase I/II Study of Tifcemalimab, an Anti-B and T-Lymphocyte Attenuator Antibody, in Combination with Toripalimab in Previously Treated Advanced Lung Cancer*.
- Year: 2025.
- Supplied version: publisher main PDF.
- Registered PDF: `Cheng_Tifcemalimab_Toripalimab_Previously_Treated_Advanced_Lung_Cancer_PMID40378060.pdf`.
- SHA-256: `193099d4a3898b29e049b8caf03f33442fc0c1cf57e48829855d60546be4084f`.
- Physical pages: 9/9 visually reviewed; no unreadable page.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`. Supplement-dependent subgroup and protocol detail was not inferred.
- Section map: abstract/Introduction p. 1; Translational Relevance and Methods p. 2; Methods and Figure 1 p. 3; Results/Table 1 p. 4; Results/Table 2 p. 5; Table 3/Discussion p. 6; Figure 2/Discussion/conclusion p. 7; Table 4/administrative material/references p. 8; references p. 9.
- Main-display coverage: Figures 1–2, Tables 1–4, and every supplied caption/footnote were visually checked against the Results and Discussion.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design/population: this was an open-label, multicenter phase I/II study in China (NCT05000684) of tifcemalimab, an anti-BTLA antibody, plus the anti–PD-1 antibody toripalimab in previously treated advanced lung cancer. A six-patient safety run-in preceded expansion. Treatment was tifcemalimab 200 mg plus toripalimab 240 mg every three weeks.
- Treated population: 67 patients received treatment: 24 with NSCLC, all previously exposed to immune checkpoint inhibition, and 43 with SCLC, including 14 previously exposed and 29 not previously exposed to checkpoint inhibition. These disease cohorts and prior-immunotherapy strata must not be pooled into a single efficacy estimate.
- Statistical design: the NSCLC cohort planned 35 patients using a Simon two-stage design, with stage 1 n=17 and stopping if one or fewer responses occurred; the stated final criterion required more than four responses among 35 to reject the null. The SCLC cohort planned n=43, with stage 1 n=18 and stopping if two or fewer responses occurred; more than seven responses among 43 was the stated final threshold. Only 24 NSCLC patients were enrolled and one response was reported, so the published data do not support treating that cohort as a positive efficacy trial.
- Assessment timing: tumor imaging was scheduled every six weeks in the first year and every nine weeks thereafter. Safety observation extended through 90 days after the last treatment. Time origin and disease-specific analysis sets should accompany PFS/OS estimates.
- Response versus time-to-event denominators: NSCLC response was reported in 23 evaluable patients, whereas PFS and OS used all 24 treated patients. SCLC response used 40 evaluable patients, whereas PFS and OS used all 43 treated patients. These are intentional analysis-set differences, not interchangeable denominators.
- NSCLC activity: one of 23 response-evaluable patients had a partial response, yielding ORR 4.3%; the response was unconfirmed. Median PFS was 1.5 months and median OS 18.9 months in the 24-patient time-to-event set. The low, unconfirmed response fraction and short PFS are the central activity findings. OS cannot be used to rescue efficacy because the trial is single-arm and 70.8% of NSCLC patients received subsequent systemic therapy.
- NSCLC confidence-interval conflict: the Results narrative reports the ORR CI as 11.0%–22.0%, which cannot contain the 4.3% point estimate. Table 4 reports 0.11%–21.95%, which is compatible with 1/23. This source inconsistency is retained: if an exact CI is required, it must be explicitly attributed to Table 4; the narrative CI must not be silently corrected or blended.
- SCLC activity: 14/40 response-evaluable patients responded, ORR 35.0%; median PFS was 2.8 months and median OS 12.3 months in all 43 treated patients. This is preliminary single-arm activity rather than comparative benefit.
- Prior-immunotherapy subgroup: ORR was 12/25 (48.0%) among immunotherapy-naive response-evaluable SCLC patients and 2/14 (14.3%) among previously immunotherapy-treated patients. The small, nonrandomized subgroup comparison is vulnerable to baseline and treatment-history confounding and is not a treatment-interaction test.
- Safety set n=67: 91.0% experienced a treatment-emergent adverse event, 35.8% a grade ≥3 TEAE, 82.1% a treatment-related adverse event, 19.4% a grade ≥3 TRAE, and 25.4% a serious adverse event. Four patients discontinued because of adverse events, all judged treatment related.
- Fatal events: two TEAEs led to death. One fatal acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease was judged treatment related. The fatality must remain explicit; “well tolerated” is not a defensible unqualified summary.
- Biomarkers: HVEM results were available for only 17 NSCLC and 26 SCLC patients, with seven HVEM-positive cases in each disease group. Descriptive ORRs were 14.3% versus 0% in HVEM-positive versus negative NSCLC and 57.1% versus 47.1% in SCLC. Neither HVEM nor PD-L1 showed a statistically significant PFS improvement, and the small retrospective subsets do not validate either marker as predictive.
- Positive result: the SCLC cohort showed a response signal, especially numerically in the checkpoint-inhibitor–naive subgroup, and the combination was administrable in the studied setting.
- Negative/uncertain result: NSCLC activity was limited to one unconfirmed response with median PFS 1.5 months; SCLC PFS was 2.8 months; no randomized comparator isolated the contribution of BTLA blockade; biomarker analyses were small and negative for significant PFS separation; subsequent therapy complicates OS; and a treatment-related fatality occurred.
- Claim ceiling: the source supports preliminary single-arm activity in previously treated SCLC, low activity in the reported NSCLC cohort, and a complete descriptive safety account. It does not establish added benefit over PD-1 blockade, comparative efficacy, an OS benefit, a predictive HVEM/PD-L1 biomarker, superiority in immunotherapy-naive SCLC, or broad tolerability without the fatal and discontinuation events.

### Language and transfer

- Methods/two-stage design (pp. 2–3): null/alternative response assumptions and early stopping are specified separately for NSCLC and SCLC. Reusable logic: state the disease-specific decision framework before interpreting observed response counts.
- Results/analysis-set discipline (pp. 4–6 and Table 4): response and time-to-event outcomes use different denominators. Reusable logic: attach the denominator and evaluability status to each endpoint rather than carrying one cohort size through all results.
- Results/negative-efficacy sentence (pp. 4–5): the one unconfirmed NSCLC response and short PFS can be stated directly while noting that OS is confounded by subsequent therapy. Reusable logic: do not allow a later, noncomparative survival estimate to obscure a weak primary activity signal.
- Results/safety sentence (pp. 4–6): common and grade ≥3 events, discontinuations, serious events, and the treatment-related death are reported together. Reusable logic: any tolerability summary should preserve the highest-consequence event.
- Discussion/biomarker restraint (pp. 6–7): HVEM and PD-L1 trends are treated as exploratory and PFS was not significantly improved. Reusable logic: a numerical response contrast without a significant time-to-event association or interaction does not validate prediction.
- Safe transfer template: `In this single-arm study, the combination showed [response estimate] in [exact disease/analysis set], whereas [negative endpoint] and the absence of a comparator limit claims of added efficacy; safety interpretation must also account for [grade ≥3 burden/discontinuation/fatal event].`
- Unsafe transfer: presenting the NSCLC cohort as positive; mixing response and PFS/OS denominators; quoting the impossible narrative CI without an alert; inferring comparative OS benefit despite subsequent therapy; treating the SCLC prior-immunotherapy contrast as predictive; or omitting the treatment-related fatality from a tolerability claim.

## PMID 40299768

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Amplification of Extrachromosomal MYC Paralogs Shapes Immunosuppressive Tumor Microenvironment in Small Cell Lung Cancer*.
- Year: 2025.
- Supplied version: not classified by the PDF header.
- Registered PDF: `Zhang_Extrachromosomal_MYC_immune_microenvironment_PMID40299768.pdf`.
- SHA-256: `ec71c193a86866b66ca34dbedb600565cec934d6c96a3eacef27cc2715234ac8`.
- Physical pages: 14/14 visually reviewed; no unreadable page.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`. Supplement-referenced figures, tables, and validation analyses were not inferred.
- Section map: abstract/Introduction p. 1; Translational Relevance, Introduction close, and Methods start p. 2; Methods pp. 2–4; Results pp. 5–10; Discussion pp. 10–12; data availability p. 12; author/disclosure material and references pp. 13–14. There is no standalone Conclusion; the abstract conclusion and final Discussion paragraphs are conclusion-driving.
- Main-display coverage: Figures 1–6 and every supplied caption were visually checked against Methods, Results, and Discussion. There is no main table.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design/evidence chain: this multi-layer translational study examined whether high-copy extrachromosomal amplification of MYC paralogs (`ecMYC+`) was associated with immune-suppressed features in SCLC. It combined public cell-line WGS/expression, drug and knockdown perturbations, a retrospective resected treatment-naive SCLC cohort, FISH/IHC, public clinical bulk RNA-seq, imaging mass cytometry (IMC), and multiplex IHC. It did not include an immunotherapy-treated outcome cohort.
- Cell-line genomic definition: AmpliconArchitect was applied to 47 SCLC and 282 other CCLE cell lines. The reported ecDNA criteria included predicted copy number >5 and a 0.5–50 Mb amplicon size, and `ecMYC+` was assigned when the highest-copy ecDNA contained a MYC paralog. This computational classification is not interchangeable with IHC overexpression or conventional chromosomal copy gain.
- Cell-line prevalence/copy number: 80 ecDNAs were detected in SCLC and 566 in other lineages. Mean ecDNA copy number was higher in SCLC than other cell lines (15.3 vs 8.4; Mann–Whitney P=4.0×10⁻³). Highly amplified ecDNA with average copy number >20 was seen in 14/47 SCLC versus 19/282 other lines (Fisher P=2.5×10⁻⁵), and ecMYC+ occurred in 22/47 versus 15/282 (P=3.9×10⁻¹²). Cross-lineage frequency comparisons are database observations, not population prevalence estimates in patients.
- Cell-line transcriptomics: among 46 SCLC lines (22 ecMYC+ and 24 ecMYC−), 560 genes met the stated differential-expression criteria (FDR<0.05 and absolute fold change >2), including 433 downregulated genes. Antigen-presentation and immune pathways were lower, whereas cell-cycle, ribosomal, and nucleotide-biosynthesis programs were higher. The displayed pathway comparison included lower MHC-related activity (P=0.0025), higher cell-cycle activity (P=0.022), and a negative Pearson correlation (r=−0.52, P≈0.0002). These are cross-sectional associations.
- Perturbation experiments: gemcitabine and hydroxyurea reduced relative MYC/MYCN copy-number readouts in NCI-H524 and NCI-H69 cells. RNA-seq in NCI-H524 and flow cytometry supported increased antigen-presentation signatures and HLA-ABC after treatment; Figure 2G reports P=0.043 for HLA-ABC. Drug-induced ecDNA loss was associated with immune-program activation, but both agents are pleiotropic and no direct, selective ecDNA manipulation was performed.
- Mechanistic negative controls: MYC expression was not downregulated by the drug conditions, MYC knockdown did not induce MHC-I in NCI-H524, and an ecMYC− SCLC line did not show significant antigen-processing/immune-pathway upregulation after hydroxyurea. These findings argue against a simple MYC-expression explanation and provide specificity clues, but they do not prove that ecDNA loss itself caused the immune changes.
- Gemcitabine dose conflict: visual inspection of Figure 2C shows NCI-H524 gemcitabine groups labeled 5, 10, and 20 nmol/L. The Figure 2 caption instead states 5, 10, and 200 nmol/L. The RNA-seq condition in the same caption is 20 nmol/L for seven days. The 20-versus-200 nmol/L qPCR condition is therefore source-internally inconsistent and should not be silently resolved.
- Retrospective clinical cohort: the PKUPH series contained 42 surgically resected, treatment-naive stage I–III SCLC cases. High IHC expression of a MYC paralog occurred in 19/42: MYCL-predominant 12 (28.5%), MYC-predominant five (11.9%), and MYCN-predominant two (4.8%); 23 were triple-negative. High and low groups did not differ significantly in the reported clinicopathologic variables. IHC expression alone does not establish extrachromosomal structure.
- FISH boundary: among overexpression cases, eight showed MYC-paralog copy gain/amplification by the reported oncogene-to-control assessment. Only three cases (patients 29, 32, and 36) showed dispersed signals described as resembling ecDNA, with reported gene/control ratios of 4.7, 11.3, and 21.6. “Resembling ecDNA” by two-dimensional FISH is appropriately described as supportive morphology, not definitive circular-amplicon reconstruction.
- Clinical bulk RNA-seq: the institutional RNA-seq analysis used 40 samples. A public paired WGS/RNA-seq set included 61 SCLC samples, only six classified as ecMYC+. Immune-pathway suppression and nucleotide/ribosome activation were directionally similar. MCP-counter estimates in the public cohort showed lower inferred T-cell, B-cell, and NK-cell abundance (P=0.015, 0.047, and 0.010). These are bulk-deconvolution estimates, not direct cell counts or treatment-response biomarkers.

### Language and transfer

- Methods/omics specification (pp. 2–4): the paper states the WGS classification, differential-expression thresholds, Benjamini–Hochberg correction for relevant omics analyses, gene-set procedures, spatial marker panels, and neighborhood construction. Reusable logic: describe input, analytic unit, threshold/correction, and inferred output separately.
- Results/evidence layering (pp. 5–10): observations progress from cell-line WGS and expression to perturbation, clinical bulk RNA-seq, and spatial imaging. Reusable logic: state each layer as corroborative evidence without allowing convergence to erase the different units or biases.
- Results/negative controls (pp. 6–8): unchanged MYC expression, negative MYC knockdown, and a negative ecMYC− drug control constrain mechanism. Reusable logic: a mechanistic sentence should include the alternative explanation the control did and did not rule out.
- Results/spatial denominator (pp. 8–10 and Figure 5): 103,341 cells and 24 ROIs derive from only six independent patients. Reusable logic: report the patient count before the much larger cell/ROI count and disclose nesting.
- Discussion/causal limitation (pp. 10–12): the authors acknowledge the absence of direct ecDNA manipulation and possible off-target drug effects. Reusable logic: use “consistent with” or “associated with,” not “mediated by,” when the perturbation is indirect.
- Discussion/clinical ceiling (pp. 11–12): the paper calls for an immunotherapy cohort and further prognosis evidence. Reusable logic: an immune phenotype in untreated tissue is a candidate-resistance hypothesis, not a demonstrated predictive biomarker.
- Safe transfer template: `Across cell-line transcriptomic and small clinical spatial datasets, ecMYC-associated samples showed lower immune-program activity and reduced T-cell representation; however, the absence of direct ecDNA manipulation, the six-patient spatial analysis, and the lack of an immunotherapy-outcome cohort limit the finding to an exploratory mechanistic association.`
