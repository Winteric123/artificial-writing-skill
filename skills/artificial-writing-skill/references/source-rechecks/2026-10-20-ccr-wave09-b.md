### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design/population: this was a single-center, open-label phase I 3+3 dose-escalation study followed by a single-arm phase II expansion in recurrent SCLC. Valemetostat was given once daily after a seven-day run-in and irinotecan 125 mg/m2 was given on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle. Twenty-two patients were enrolled (phase I n=12; phase II n=10), one withdrew before treatment, and 21 received study treatment. Most had platinum-refractory disease (83%) and prior chemoimmunotherapy (83%). There was no randomized or contemporaneous control.
- Analysis sets and denominators: safety and pharmacokinetics used the 21 treated patients, although one patient never started irinotecan. The response analysis used 19 patients: one withdrew before treatment, one did not start irinotecan, and one discontinued during cycle 1 when the study closed. The phase II toxicity stopping rule used the first 10 phase II patients. Tumor IHC used 13 acceptable pretreatment and nine acceptable on-treatment samples, with only five paired samples for the displayed MHC-I comparison and seven paired subtype samples; only four subtype pairs came from the same disease site. These denominators are not interchangeable.
- Dose finding and stopping: the first three patients at 100 mg had no DLT. At 150 mg, grade 4 neutropenia with sepsis occurred, and grade 4 thrombocytopenia was initially counted as a DLT; after a protocol amendment excluded grade 4 thrombocytopenia lasting fewer than seven consecutive days, enrollment resumed and a subsequent grade 3 anemia DLT established that 150 mg exceeded the MTD. Valemetostat 100 mg was declared the RP2D. In phase II, three of the first 10 patients had DLTs, triggering the prespecified toxicity stop. The study was therefore not completed according to the planned efficacy design.
- Formal phase II design boundary: the Simon minimax design tested a null ORR of 16% against 25%, with type I error 0.05 and power 0.80. Sixteen patients were planned in stage 1, inclusive of six phase I patients treated at the RP2D, and 31 total patients were planned; success required at least nine partial responses. Because accrual stopped for toxicity after only 10 phase II enrollees, the observed response signal cannot be described as having met the planned primary efficacy objective.
- Efficacy: among 19 response-evaluable patients, four confirmed responses gave an ORR of 21% (95% CI, 6%-46%). One additional patient had more than 30% tumor shrinkage but left in cycle 1 for toxicity, so the response was unconfirmed. Seven patients had stable disease; the reported disease-control rate was 12/19 (63%; 95% CI, 38%-84%), which includes confirmed or unconfirmed responses plus stable disease. Median DOR was 4.6 months, median PFS 2.2 months (95% CI, 1.3-7.6), and median OS 6.6 months (95% CI, 4.3-not reached). One confirmed responder remained on treatment for 15.4 months and two for nearly eight months. These findings show activity in some patients but do not establish synergy, comparative benefit, or a successful phase II primary endpoint.
- Safety: among 21 treated patients, the most frequent treatment-related adverse events were diarrhea (57%), fatigue (38%), nausea (33%), and maculopapular rash (24%). Three patients discontinued treatment because of treatment-related toxicity. Irinotecan was reduced in 7/20 patients who received it (35%), and valemetostat in 4/21 (19%). The three phase II DLT cases comprised grade 3 febrile neutropenia; grade 3 abdominal pain, constipation, and nausea with grade 4 decreased white blood cells; and grade 3 febrile neutropenia with atrial fibrillation. The combination was explicitly judged not tolerated.
- Pharmacokinetics: exposure was higher at 150 mg than at 100 mg, but only two cycle-1-day-15 samples were available at 150 mg. Two phase II DLT patients at 100 mg had cycle-1-day-1 Cmax values of 1,820 and 2,760 ng/mL versus a mean of 1,590 ng/mL with 75% coefficient of variation; neither value was the highest observed, and the third DLT patient's value was unavailable. This is a descriptive exposure observation, not proof that high valemetostat exposure caused the DLTs.
- Correlative findings: mean SLFN11 H-score decreased from 49.0 (95% CI, 16.0-82.0) pretreatment to 23.5 (95% CI, -7.6 to 54.6) on treatment; two responders showed modest increases. Mean EZH2 H-score was 147.9 (95% CI, 101.3-194.5) pretreatment and 149.6 (95% CI, 93.7-205.5) on treatment. Mean MHC-I H-score increased descriptively from 100.2 (95% CI, 15.6-184.8) to 125.7 (95% CI, 23.6-227.8); four of five paired samples increased, including three responders. No formal paired effect estimate or P value was provided, so this is not a statistically established treatment effect.
- Subtype and biomarker negatives: SLFN11, EZH2, and pretreatment SCLC subtype did not show a clear response correlation. The two longest responders had different dominant subtype calls on treatment, but their pre- and on-treatment biopsies came from different disease sites; spatial heterogeneity is an explicit alternative to true temporal subtype switching. The data do not show that subtype switching mediated benefit or resistance.
- Source inconsistency: Table 1 reports phase I prior-line counts of 7/12 and 5/12 but prints 78% and 56%, which are arithmetically impossible and sum to more than 100%. The counts align with the overall narrative totals, so the erroneous phase-I percentages are withheld. The text states that nine on-treatment samples were sufficient for subtyping, but the displayed subtype counts (ASCL1 n=5, NEUROD1 n=2, marker-negative n=1) sum to eight; with no supplied supplement, the ninth sample's disposition cannot be resolved.
- Positive result: the combination produced four confirmed responses, including several lasting more than seven months, in a small heavily pretreated recurrent-SCLC cohort.
- Negative/uncertain result: the regimen triggered the prespecified toxicity stop, was not tolerated, did not complete the Simon efficacy design, did not establish the proposed SLFN11 mechanism in tumors, and yielded only descriptive MHC-I and subtype observations.
- Claim ceiling: the source supports a preliminary single-arm activity signal accompanied by prohibitive toxicity at the tested schedule and exploratory correlative observations. It does not establish clinical synergy, comparative efficacy, survival benefit, a positive phase II primary endpoint, a response-predictive biomarker, causal subtype switching, or an MHC-I-mediated immunotherapy strategy.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design/model chain: the study developed histology-associated epigenomic features in PDX tumors and then retrospectively tested whether epigenomic cfDNA profiles distinguished biopsy-confirmed transformed SCLC (tSCLC) from advanced EGFR-mutant LUAD without documented transformation. It combines PDX discovery, cell-line subtype assessment, a small clinical cfDNA case-control comparison, and two longitudinal patient vignettes. It is not a prospective diagnostic-accuracy study and does not test clinical utility.
- Discovery material and methods: 26 PDX models comprised 13 LUAD, four tSCLC, and nine de novo SCLC models. Across ATAC-seq, H3K27ac/H3K4me3/H3K27me3 ChIP-seq, MeDIP-seq, and RNA-seq, 126 epigenome-wide libraries were generated. Differential features used FDR-adjusted P<0.001 and absolute log2 fold change greater than 2, with white-blood-cell signal filtering before cfDNA classifiers were constructed. After filtering, the classifier feature sets included 10,907 H3K27ac, 833 H3K4me3, 1,210 methylation, and 9,995 accessible-chromatin sites.
- Clinical cohort and analysis sets: 48 plasma samples came from 32 patients: 20 with metastatic EGFR-mutant LUAD without tSCLC and 12 with biopsy-proven tSCLC; six tSCLC patients also had a pretransformation sample. Samples required estimated cfDNA tumor fraction greater than 3% plus analyte-specific QC, leaving 105 epigenomic libraries rather than 48 complete four-analyte profiles. Figure 4 used H3K27ac n=12 LUAD/n=9 tSCLC, H3K4me3 n=14/n=13, methylation n=16/n=13, and accessibility n=16/n=13. The integrated Figure 5 classifier used only n=12 LUAD and n=9 tSCLC samples. These analyte-specific denominators must accompany the performance estimates.
- Individual analytes: H3K27ac scores differed between groups (P=0.0042) and yielded AUROC 0.87 (P=0.0056). H3K4me3 trended in the same direction but was not significant (group P=0.076; AUROC 0.70, P=0.073). DNA methylation differed at P=0.00090 and yielded AUROC 0.85 (P=0.0015); chromatin accessibility differed at P=0.0023 and yielded AUROC 0.82 (P=0.0033). The H3K4me3 negative is mandatory and prevents describing every analyte as independently discriminative.
- Integrated classifier: the three-feature H3K27ac/methylation/accessibility score in the same small clinical cohort yielded AUROC 0.94, with a Youden-derived cutoff of -0.029, apparent sensitivity 89%, specificity 91%, and likelihood ratio 9.8. The ROC panel in Figure 5B prints P=0.00095, whereas the Results paragraph on the same page prints P=0.0095. Because the source is internally inconsistent, the exact classifier P value is withheld unless both values are shown; the AUROC and displayed operating characteristics are unaffected by that disposition.
- Validation boundary: PDXs used for feature discovery were independent of the patient cfDNA cohort, which reduces one source of leakage, but the integrated cutoff and apparent performance were derived and evaluated in the same 21 clinical samples. There was no independent patient-level validation cohort, prospective sampling schedule, calibration analysis, or comparison with tissue-biopsy clinical outcomes beyond case status.
- Tumor fraction: the integrated score correlated with cfDNA tumor fraction in tSCLC (R2=0.79; P=4.5x10^-5) but not in EGFR-mutant LUAD (R2<0.01; P=0.99). This pattern is compatible with histology-linked biology, but the strong within-tSCLC correlation also means tumor burden/ctDNA abundance remains relevant and cannot be declared eliminated as a source of signal.
- Subtype limitation: in 20 LUAD and 13 SCLC cell lines, SCLC-Y scores did not differ from LUAD (P=0.36) and were lower than those of the other SCLC subtypes (P=0.026). Excluding the two SCLC-Y lines produced AUROC 0.995 (P=6.8x10^-6) for the remaining SCLC-A/N/P lines versus LUAD. This post hoc-looking exclusion illustrates a clinically important failure mode rather than universal SCLC detection.
- Longitudinal observations: only two patients had serial samples. In one, the high score coincided with new metastases and biopsy-proven tSCLC. In the other, the authors report that a rise preceded clinical diagnosis by 92 days. Because sampling was retrospective and nonuniform, the paper explicitly states that it could not methodically determine lead time. The 92-day observation is a single-patient vignette, not an estimated early-detection interval.
- Negative/uncertain results and limitations: there was no difference in cfDNA tumor fraction between tSCLC and EGFR-mutant LUAD groups (P=0.10). The cohort was modest, collected across eight years at two institutions with variable tubes and extraction methods, and dominated by SCLC-A/N biology. Tumor biopsy remained the diagnostic gold standard; cfDNA was framed as complementary. No treatment-response, prognosis, survival, or patient-safety endpoint was evaluated.
- Positive result: multiple epigenomic layers derived from tumors captured tSCLC-associated biology, and a three-analyte score separated a small retrospective set of biopsy-defined tSCLC and EGFR-mutant LUAD plasma samples with high apparent discrimination.
- Claim ceiling: the source supports technical and diagnostic-feasibility evidence for epigenomic cfDNA classification of tSCLC. It does not establish independently validated diagnostic accuracy, a deployable cutoff, universal performance across SCLC subtypes, prospective lead time, replacement of tissue biopsy, improved outcomes, or treatment selection.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Source type/design: this is an FDA approval summary synthesizing the agency's review of TRIDENT-1, not a new randomized trial report. TRIDENT-1 was an ongoing open-label, multicenter, first-in-human, multicohort single-arm study. Repotrectinib was dosed at 160 mg once daily for 14 days then 160 mg twice daily. BICR-assessed confirmed ORR by RECIST 1.1 and DOR supported traditional approval in a rare molecular subgroup; no randomized comparative effect on PFS or OS was estimated.
- Primary efficacy sets: 71 ROS1-TKI-naive patients and 56 patients previously treated with one ROS1 TKI and no prior platinum chemotherapy or immunotherapy formed the principal efficacy groups. Table 2 baseline data used a June 20, 2022 cutoff, whereas Table 3 DOR used an updated December 19, 2022 dataset. Timing must be attached when comparing maturity.
- TKI-naive efficacy: confirmed ORR was 79% (95% CI, 68%-88%), comprising 6% complete and 73% partial responses. Median DOR was 34.1 months (95% CI, 25.6 months-not evaluable), and 70% of responders had observed DOR at least 12 months. These are durable single-arm response results, not comparative survival benefit.
- TKI-pretreated efficacy: confirmed ORR was 38% (95% CI, 25%-52%), comprising 5% complete and 32% partial responses. Median DOR was 14.8 months (95% CI, 7.6 months-not evaluable), and 48% of responders had observed DOR at least 12 months. The result applies to the defined one-prior-TKI/no-prior-chemotherapy-or-immunotherapy cohort and cannot be generalized to all post-TKI settings.
- Intracranial subsets: seven of eight TKI-naive patients and five of 12 TKI-pretreated patients with BICR-measurable CNS lesions had intracranial responses. Three responding TKI-naive patients had received brain irradiation, including two within 60 days of repotrectinib; two responding pretreated patients had received brain irradiation within 60 days. The very small subsets and recent radiation preclude a clean repotrectinib-only intracranial-effect estimate.
- Resistance-mutation subset: eight of 56 pretreated patients had detected post-TKI ROS1 resistance mutations and six responded, including G2032R, L2026M, and S1986F/Y. Forty-three of 56 were not tested and five had no detected mutation or QC failure. The 6/8 result therefore describes a highly selected tested subset, not mutation-stratified effectiveness in the whole cohort.
- Safety populations: pooled safety used 351 patients with ROS1-positive NSCLC or other solid tumors treated at the RP2D, while a focused ROS1-positive NSCLC set used 264 patients. In the pooled set, grade 3-4 TEAEs occurred in 44% and grade 5 TEAEs in 4.8%. The text separately reports 24 patients (5%) with a fatal-outcome TEAE in the “total RP2D population,” but 24 is not reconcilable with 5% of n=351 and no alternative denominator is given on that page; the count, percentage, and n must not be recombined.
- Focused safety: among 264 ROS1-positive NSCLC patients, 33% had serious adverse reactions and 4.2% had fatal adverse reactions. Permanent discontinuations included dyspnea, pneumonitis, and muscular weakness; interruptions included CNS toxicity, dyspnea, and muscular weakness; reductions occurred for dizziness. Table 4 common adverse reactions included dizziness 63% (grade 3-4 1.9%), dysgeusia 48%, peripheral neuropathy 47% (1.9%), constipation 36%, dyspnea 30% (7%), ataxia 28% (0.4%), fatigue 24% (1.1%), cognitive disorders 23% (0.8%), and muscular weakness 21% (1.5%). These n=264 rates should not be mixed with the slightly different n=351 pooled rates in Table 5.
- Ocular uncertainty: in the pooled n=351 set, vision disorders occurred in 12%, including 0.3% grade 3-4. Eye and vision examinations were not required in TRIDENT-1, so the signal was not adequately characterized. FDA imposed a prospective postmarketing requirement to assess ocular risk factors, manifestations, and outcomes. The absence of a REMS does not mean ocular risk was excluded.
- Regulatory boundary: FDA considered the magnitude and durability of response sufficient for traditional approval in a rare molecularly defined population, while issuing commitments/requirements for final ORR/DOR, ocular safety, pediatric evaluation, hepatic impairment, drug-drug interactions, and a companion diagnostic. At approval, no approved companion diagnostic was available and local ROS1-fusion testing could be used pending development.
- Positive result: high and durable BICR response in the TKI-naive cohort and lower but clinically meaningful response in the defined one-prior-TKI cohort supported traditional approval.
- Negative/uncertain result: no randomized comparator, PFS benefit, or OS benefit was shown; intracranial and resistance-mutation analyses were small and confounded by incomplete testing or recent radiotherapy; ocular toxicity was incompletely characterized; and several postmarketing data needs remained.
- Claim ceiling: the source supports FDA's benefit-risk conclusion and durable single-arm response evidence for the approved ROS1-positive NSCLC population. It does not establish superiority, comparative survival benefit, definitive intracranial efficacy independent of radiotherapy, broad mutation-specific effectiveness, absence of serious toxicity, or complete ocular-risk characterization.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design/population: this retrospective case-control biomarker study measured urinary creatine riboside (CR) and N-acetylneuraminic acid (NANA) by targeted LC-MS/MS, normalized values to urinary creatinine, and assessed case-control discrimination and survival association in an exploratory and a nominally independent validation cohort. It was not a prospective screening study and did not evaluate biomarker-guided care.
- Cohorts and displayed denominators: the exploratory cohort comprised 846 participants: 375 lung-cancer cases (261 smokers, 114 nonsmokers) and 471 population controls (268 smokers, 203 nonsmokers). Table 1 and Figure 2 show 505 validation participants: 285 cases (206 smokers, 79 nonsmokers) and 220 controls (109 smokers, 111 nonsmokers). Figure 1B instead labels the 285 validation cases as 203 smokers and 82 nonsmokers, an unexplained 3-person redistribution. Table 1/Figure 2 and Figure 1B cannot be silently harmonized.
- Methods/statistics: distributions were compared nonparametrically; ROC models were adjusted for age, race, and sex. CR and NANA were dichotomized at cohort-specific medians for survival analysis: 0.7 and 14.6 micromol/L in the exploratory cohort and 0.8 and 15.4 micromol/L in validation. Kaplan-Meier/log-rank analyses and Cox models were used. Numerous subgroup, histology, stage, smoking, race, sex, and metabolite comparisons were made, with no clear family-wise or FDR control for the full analysis program.
- Diagnostic discrimination in nonsmokers: exploratory AUROCs were 0.91 (95% CI, 0.88-0.94) for CR, 0.94 (0.91-0.96) for NANA, and 0.94 (0.91-0.96) combined. Validation AUROCs fell to 0.80 (0.74-0.86), 0.80 (0.69-0.83), and 0.80 (0.74-0.86), respectively. The material drop from exploration to validation is necessary context even though discrimination remained above chance.
- Diagnostic discrimination in smokers: exploratory AUROCs were 0.76 (95% CI, 0.72-0.80) for CR, 0.76 (0.72-0.80) for NANA, and 0.81 (0.77-0.85) combined. Validation AUROCs were 0.70 (0.66-0.79), 0.70 (0.64-0.76), and 0.70 (0.65-0.78). The authors report combined-model PPV of 89% and 62% in exploratory and validation cohorts and NPV of 88%; because these are retrospective case-control samples with constructed disease prevalence, the predictive values are not transportable to a screening population.
- Kaplan-Meier prognosis in nonsmokers: in the exploratory cohort, CR was null (P=0.6), NANA was significant (P=0.01), and the combined high-both category was exactly P=0.05. In validation, CR (P=0.1), NANA (P=0.3), and the combined category (P=0.06) were not significant at the prespecified P<=0.05 threshold. The Results prose nevertheless says the combined validation group had “significantly” worse survival, which conflicts with Figure 3B's P=0.06 and is withheld.
- Cox prognosis in nonsmokers: in the exploratory cohort, combined high CR+NANA had univariable HR 1.79 (95% CI, 0.99-3.24; P=0.041) and multivariable HR 2.74 (1.40-5.38; P=0.001). In validation, the corresponding estimates were HR 3.37 (1.35-8.39; P=0.006) and HR 2.92 (1.13-7.50; P=0.022). These adjusted associations support prognostic association, but they do not validate causality or clinical decision thresholds.
- Additional statistical conflicts: the exploratory univariable CR+NANA estimate has a displayed CI including 1.00 while P=0.041. Validation adjusted stage has HR 2.71 with 95% CI 0.99-7.42 but P=0.042. These inferential inconsistencies are retained; neither result should be described solely by its P value. Validation univariable sex was borderline (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.19-0.99; P=0.050), while its adjusted estimate was null.
- Endpoint-label inconsistency: the abstract describes increased “cancer-specific death,” whereas Figure 3's caption labels the displayed curves as overall survival and Table 2 uses generic survival wording. Without a supplied supplement or event-definition table, the endpoint should be called “survival” with the display-specific label rather than harmonized as cancer-specific or overall survival across all analyses.
- Biological and clinical boundary: both metabolites were elevated in other malignancies in cited work, and the authors explicitly note that CR is not lung-cancer specific. This study did not test a causal metabolic pathway. Selected controls, retrospective sampling, cohort-specific medians, case-control prevalence, missing supplement, and multiple analyses limit calibration and generalizability. No treatment-response or patient-safety analysis was performed.
- Positive result: both metabolites showed reproducible case-control discrimination, particularly in nonsmokers, and the combined high category remained associated with worse survival in multivariable Cox models in both cohorts.
- Negative/uncertain result: validation AUROCs were materially lower, individual nonsmoker Kaplan-Meier findings did not reproduce, the combined validation log-rank result was P=0.06, several statistics are internally discordant, and prospective screening/prognostic utility remains untested.
- Claim ceiling: the source supports retrospective case-control diagnostic association and adjusted prognostic association for urinary CR/NANA. It does not establish population-screening performance, clinical utility, causal biology, a universal cutoff, biomarker-guided treatment benefit, or a validated individual-risk prediction tool.
