## PMID 40208070

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Ad-SGE-DKK3 Gene Therapy Overcomes Resistance to Immune Checkpoint Blockade in Pleural Mesothelioma*.
- Issue year/version: 2025; `author_manuscript`.
- Registered PDF: `Jang_Ad-SGE-DKK3_Gene_Therapy_Overcomes_Resistance_to_Immune_Checkpoint_Blockade_in_Pleural_Mesotheliom_PMID40208070.pdf`; SHA-256 `2901eb52be9132262b07e21635630793f3a4f53f977adbfe0be7db447f1391e2`; 33 physical pages.

- Section map: abstract/introduction pp1-3; methods pp3-8; Results pp8-13; Discussion pp13-15; references/admin pp15-20; Figures 1-7 and their continuations pp21-33.
- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 pp21-22; Figure 2 pp23-24; Figure 3 pp25-26; Figure 4 pp27-28; Figure 5 pp29-30; Figure 6 pp31-32; Figure 7 p33. No main table was present in the supplied PDF. Every panel, legend, survival axis, risk set, and clinical flow label was checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: a translational program linked human mesothelioma datasets, engineered mesothelioma cells, syngeneic mouse experiments, multi-omic and immune profiling, and an open-label single-center phase II study of intratumoral Ad-SGE-DKK3 plus nivolumab in chemotherapy-refractory epithelioid pleural mesothelioma. The clinical component was single-arm and cannot separate the gene-therapy contribution from nivolumab.
- Human and preclinical context: DKK3 expression was lower in epithelioid mesothelioma in the examined human datasets and inversely related to promoter methylation in TCGA. DKK3 also correlated with immune-checkpoint markers. Engineered DKK3 expression changed transcriptional, proteomic, and secretome programs involving p53, interferon, IL2/STAT5, glycolysis, extracellular-vesicle markers, and CSF1. In vitro proliferation was similar, whereas growth was slower in immunocompetent mice and tumors showed increased PD-L1, CD8-positive T cells, conventional dendritic cells and M1-like macrophages, with fewer M2-like/CD115-positive macrophages.
- Mouse efficacy and mechanistic boundary: engineered DKK3-expressing tumors combined with anti-PD-1 showed lower tumor burden and 100% survival at seven weeks versus less than 25% in controls. In the ICI-resistant AB1 model, neither Ad-SGE-DKK3 nor anti-PD-1 monotherapy effectively inhibited growth, whereas the combination reduced tumor volume and weight. Neoepitope burden and presentation of DKK3-derived MHC-I peptides were not increased, retaining a negative mechanistic result. Tumor eradication and synergy language applies only to these mouse models.
- Clinical analysis set: 16 patients were screened, four failed screening, and 12 received at least one combination dose. Nine completed all four scheduled intratumoral injections, two received three, and one received one; all received at least two nivolumab cycles and the median was 5.5 cycles. Discontinuation before all four gene-therapy doses occurred for disease progression in two and COVID-19 in one.
- Clinical activity: two of 12 patients achieved partial response (ORR 16.6%); five had stable disease during the six-month treatment period (41.7%). The authors define durable clinical benefit as PR plus SD within that interval, yielding 7/12 (58.3%). Five of 12 patients had some tumor-size reduction. At 17.8 months' follow-up, main text and Figure 5 report median PFS 4.3 months and median OS 14.5 months.
- Source inconsistency: the abstract reports median PFS of 4.5 months, whereas the Results, Figure 5 survival panel, and Figure 5 legend report 4.3 months. Both source locations are retained; the discrepancy is not silently resolved. The median OS of 14.5 months is concordant. The study was not designed to compare survival with historical controls.
- Clinical immune/omics findings: image mass cytometry suggested greater T-cell representation in durable-benefit than progressive-disease tumors, but tissue numbers were very small. Paired Olink analysis identified 36 proteins at `P<0.01` (20 increased and 16 decreased); DKK3, IFN-gamma and CXCL9/10/11 increased and CSF1 decreased. Hallmark analyses implicated IL6/JAK/STAT3 and interferon pathways. Soluble PD-1 decreased and soluble PD-L1 increased irrespective of response. Lower pretreatment IL6, CSF1 and mesothelin were observed in the durable-benefit group. These exploratory comparisons were not a prospectively validated predictor analysis, and the reported GSEA threshold included FDR below 0.25 with nominal `P<0.05`.
- Safety: all 12 patients had at least one treatment-related adverse event. Fever occurred in 91.7%, chills in 58.3%, and nausea in 58.3%. Five of 12 (41.7%) had grade 3 treatment-related events, including acute kidney injury, chest-wall hernia related to prior surgery, chronic cholecystitis, headache, and pericardial effusion. No grade 4 or grade 5 treatment-related event was reported.
- Claim ceiling: the clinical data support feasibility, a characterized safety profile, and uncontrolled antitumor activity of the combination in 12 selected patients. They do not establish efficacy beyond nivolumab, superiority to historical ICI outcomes, a predictive immune/protein biomarker, or translation of mouse eradication to patients. “Durable clinical benefit” must retain the paper's six-month PR-plus-SD definition rather than imply a randomized durable-benefit endpoint.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Discussion states that the trial was not designed to compare survival and that observed PFS/OS were not highly dissimilar from historical ICI controls (p14). The Results preserves the preclinical contrast that neither monotherapy effectively inhibited the resistant model whereas the combination did (p11).
- Safe template: “Although the single-arm combination produced responses and disease stabilization, the small uncontrolled cohort cannot isolate the contribution of [component] or establish improvement over checkpoint blockade; the activity and biomarker findings therefore warrant controlled validation.”
- Unsafe transfer: silently choosing 4.3 or 4.5 months without source labeling; calling 58.3% a conventional randomized durable-benefit endpoint; claiming clinical synergy, efficacy superiority, or a validated protein predictor; or transferring the mouse survival result to patients.

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *In Situ RAS:RAF Binding Correlates with Response to KRASG12C Inhibitors in KRASG12C-Mutant Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer*.
- Issue year/version: 2025; `author_manuscript`.
- Registered PDF: `Kato_In_Situ_RAS_RAF_Binding_Correlates_with_Response_to_KRASG12C_Inhibitors_in_KRASG12C-Mutant_Non-Sma_PMID39836411.pdf`; SHA-256 `906d4aeb1ee7d47886083cbc9a82285b0b66ede112cd569c35c66fee0c80b9f6`; 29 physical pages.

- Section map: abstract/introduction pp1-3; methods pp3-7; Results pp7-13; Discussion pp13-15; references/admin pp15-18; Figures 1-5 pp19-27; Table 1 pp28-29.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: a proximity-ligation assay (PLA) for in-situ pan-RAS:CRAF binding was developed and analytically challenged in cell lines, tested in 18 KRASG12C-mutant NSCLC patient-derived xenografts, and explored retrospectively in pretreatment samples from 15 patients receiving sotorasib. It is a method-development and association study, not prospective biomarker validation.
- Assay chain and controls: nine candidate KRAS antibodies failed immunofluorescence specificity after KRAS knockdown, motivating selection of pan-RAS antibodies and a CRAF antibody. PLA specificity was supported by omission/single-antibody, IgG, siRNA, and sotorasib perturbation controls. In 11 KRASG12C NSCLC cell lines plus PC9, higher PLA signal correlated with RAS-GTP, phospho-ERK, and sotorasib sensitivity; there was no significant association with phospho-AKT.
- PDX findings: among 18 PDX models, murine complete/partial responders had higher PLA than stable/progressive models (`P=0.005`). ROC AUC was 0.944. A data-derived cutoff of 0.4 foci/cell classified response in 10/11 high-PLA versus 2/7 low-PLA models. Mouse “PFS,” defined as time to tumor doubling, was not reached in 30 high-PLA tumors versus 62 days in 21 low-PLA tumors (`P<0.0001`). These tumor-level observations are not patient survival estimates.
- Clinical set and efficacy: all 15 patients had pretreatment FFPE tissue containing at least 100 tumor cells and RECIST assessment; 12 eventually progressed. ORR was 53.3%, DCR 73.3%, median PFS 6.5 months, and median OS 10.8 months. PLA correlated with PFS (`r=0.71`, `P=0.003`) and was higher with disease control than without (`P=0.02`).
- Clinical threshold and negative results: ROC AUC for PLA was 0.886 versus 0.636 for pan-RAS expression. At the internally selected cutoff of 0.55, median PFS was 7.5 months in nine high-PLA versus 1.9 months in six low-PLA patients (log-rank `P=0.03`). OS did not differ significantly. Pan-RAS expression itself was not associated with PFS (`r=0.23`, `P=0.40`; grouped comparison `P=0.49`).
- Negative comparator assay: EGFR:GRB2 PLA showed no relation to sotorasib sensitivity in cell lines (`r=0.02`, `P=0.96`), PDX response (`P=0.20`), or clinical PFS (`r=0.16`, `P=0.61`). These null findings support assay specificity but must not be dropped when summarizing the platform.
- Statistical/transfer boundary: PDX and clinical cutoffs were selected and evaluated in the same small datasets. The clinical cohort had heterogeneous sampling sites, prior/intervening treatments, no untreated or alternative-treatment comparator, no independent validation set, no multivariable adjustment, and no biomarker-treatment interaction test. Correlation with outcome cannot establish treatment-specific prediction or causal pathway dependence.
- Claim ceiling: in-situ RAS:RAF PLA is a biologically supported candidate biomarker associated with sotorasib response/PFS in preclinical models and a 15-patient retrospective series. It does not yet establish a reproducible cutoff, prospective utility, an OS association, or selection benefit for KRASG12C inhibitor treatment.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results uses correlation/association wording for clinical PLA and explicitly reports the absent OS and pan-RAS-expression associations (pp11-13). The Discussion calls for confirmation in larger cohorts and recognizes the 15-patient limitation (pp14-15).
- Safe template: “Higher [assay signal] correlated with longer PFS in the retrospective treated cohort; however, the internally derived threshold, small sample, and absence of a comparator arm make the finding a candidate treatment-response biomarker rather than a validated predictor.”
- Unsafe transfer: describing the cutoff as clinically validated; claiming PLA predicts benefit rather than outcome on treatment; omitting the negative OS/pan-RAS/EGFR:GRB2 results; or treating mouse tumor-doubling time as patient PFS.

### Six gates and verdict

- Verdict: `passed`.

## PMID 40465842

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Elevated ctDNA Tumor Fraction Is Associated with Improved Mutation Detection but Worse Overall Survival in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: A Lung-MAP Study*.
- Issue year/version: 2025; `author_manuscript`.
- Registered PDF: `Mack_Elevated_ctDNA_Tumor_Fraction_Is_Associated_with_Improved_Mutation_Detection_but_Worse_Overall_Sur_PMID40465842.pdf`; SHA-256 `a1139c78174c16d98f0e9f9cfe11d17f6cdd4c374b6e0f80d254763a8a68f9a3`; 25 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`; no separate supplement was treated as reviewed.
- Section map: abstract/introduction pp1-3; methods pp3-7; Results pp7-12; Discussion pp12-14; references/admin pp14-16; Figures 1-6 pp17-23; Table 1 pp24-25.
- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 pp17-18; Figure 2 p19; Figure 3 p20; Figure 4 p21; Figure 5 p22; Figure 6 p23; Table 1 pp24-25. Flow counts, tumor-fraction strata, agreement denominators, confidence intervals, regression axes, survival risk sets, and table footnotes were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: prospective paired fresh-tissue and plasma profiling embedded in Lung-MAP screening for previously treated advanced NSCLC. FoundationOne CDx tissue results were compared with FoundationOne Liquid/LIQUID CDx plasma results, and ctDNA tumor fraction (TF) was related to alteration detection, blood-versus-tissue TMB concordance, and overall survival.
- Cohort and denominator chain: 2,995 patients underwent Lung-MAP screening; 263 had blood collected, 236 were evaluable, and 39 were excluded because of timing or intervening treatment, yielding a paired comparison set of 197. TF was available for 194: 84 below 1%, 55 at 1%-9.9%, and 55 at least 10%. The 197 paired-analysis denominator and 194 TF/survival denominator must not be interchanged.
- Method/statistical chain: TF was estimated primarily by aneuploidy, with fragmentomics, maximum somatic allele fraction, or known somatic variants used when needed. Positive/negative percent agreement and predictive values used binomial methods and GEE robust variance; VAF relationships used regression/Lin concordance; survival used Kaplan-Meier/Cox methods. Survival was not reported from a multivariable clinical prognostic model and was not tied to a randomized treatment comparison.
- Short-variant detection: for KRAS, PPA rose from 59% below 1% TF to 94% at TF at least 1%; corresponding values were 69% to 99% for TP53 and 25% to 68% for STK11. Across short variants, PPA was 77% overall, 95% (91%-99%) at TF at least 1%, and 51% (42%-61%) below 1%. PPV was 71% overall and 74% versus 64% across those strata, without a statistically established difference.
- Negative/limited classes: copy-number alteration PPA was 9% (4%-14%) overall and only 14% (6%-22%) at TF at least 1%; none were detected below 1%, although PPV was 84% (68%-100%). Rearrangement PPA was 39% (18%-60%), with sparse and statistically indistinguishable stratum estimates; PPV was 47% (20%-74%). Higher TF therefore does not make all alteration classes reliably detectable.
- Quantitative concordance: tissue-versus-plasma VAF relationships were strongest for KRAS (slope 0.96, `R²=0.81`) and STK11 (0.86, `R²=0.92`) and weaker for TP53 (0.69, `R²=0.57`), with outliers compatible with clonal hematopoiesis. Among 182 tissue-TMB and 169 blood-TMB results, overall Lin concordance was 0.68; it was 0.73 at TF at least 1% and 0.23 below 1%.
- Prognosis: among 194 patients, TF below 1% had 62/84 deaths and median OS 16.2 months (12.1-19.6). TF 1%-9.9% had 48/55 deaths, median 7.6 months (5.7-9.8), and HR 2.45 (1.66-3.61; `P<0.001`) versus below 1%. TF at least 10% had 50/55 deaths, median 5.5 months (3.4-7.1), and HR 2.75 (1.89-4.03; `P<0.001`). The two higher-TF groups did not significantly differ from each other.
- Interpretation boundary: high TF was associated with improved plasma detection and worse prognosis, but no treatment interaction was tested. Low TF makes a negative liquid result unreliable and supports tissue reflex testing; it does not mean absence of the alteration. Copy-number and fusion detection remained limited, and clonal hematopoiesis can lower specificity. The authors' proposal to incorporate TF into interpretation and monitor response is clinically plausible but not an intervention validated in this report.
- Claim ceiling: TF is an assay-context variable and an adverse prognostic marker in this advanced-NSCLC cohort. It is not a therapy-response predictor, a stand-alone treatment selector, or evidence that TF-guided management improves outcomes.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results separately presents detection improvement and poorer survival, preserving the dual assay/prognostic interpretation (pp8-12). The Discussion advises tissue testing after low-TF negative plasma results and treats response monitoring as a future-use proposal (pp12-14).
- Safe template: “A tumor fraction of at least 1% was associated with higher short-variant agreement, whereas elevated tumor fraction was also associated with shorter OS; these findings support tumor fraction as both an interpretive assay variable and a prognostic marker, not a treatment-predictive biomarker.”
- Unsafe transfer: treating a low-TF negative liquid biopsy as a true negative; generalizing short-variant PPA to CNAs/rearrangements; calling TF predictive of treatment benefit; or implying that TF-guided monitoring improved survival.

### Six gates and verdict

- `coverage_check=pass`; `evidence_check=pass`; `results_check=pass`; `language_check=pass`; `traceability_check=pass`; `transfer_check=pass`.
- Verdict: `passed`.

## PMID 39561276

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Analysis of Shared Variants between Cancer Biospecimens*.
- Issue year/version: 2025; `author_manuscript`.
- Registered PDF: `Foote_Shared_variants_cancer_biospecimens_PMID39561276.pdf`; SHA-256 `03c30369b45594037665d129ff261388f690dde703b80f7cf924e6af34d9aae8`; 22 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`; no separate supplement was treated as reviewed.
- Section map: abstract/introduction pp1-3; methods pp3-6; Results pp6-10; Discussion pp10-12; references/admin pp12-14; Figures 1-5 pp15-20; Table 1 pp21-22.
- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 p15; Figure 2 p16; Figure 3 p17; Figure 4 pp18-19; Figure 5 p20; Table 1 pp21-22. Probability axes, cancer-type and hypermutation strata, confusion-matrix denominators, longitudinal reclassification examples, plasma comparisons, captions, and footnotes were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: a pan-cancer method study developed mutational profile similarity (MPS), which estimates how unlikely an observed set of shared variants is against empiric background sharing rates, then evaluated it in TCGA, a large MSK sequencing cohort, repeat tumors, lineage transformation, and selected plasma-versus-tumor comparisons. The target is clonal relatedness, not treatment efficacy or prognosis.
- Background sharing: among 10,000 cancer-agnostic TCGA pairs, 130 (1.3%) shared at least one variant; among 330,000 cancer-type-specific pairs, 33,035 (10.0%) did. Sharing at least two variants was below 0.3% in most settings but common in mismatch-repair-deficient (33.9%) and POLE-mutant (56.4%) tumors and more frequent in melanoma (6.6%) and low-grade glioma (1.0%). Conventional independence calculations underpredicted observed co-sharing by at least fivefold, supporting an empirical background model.
- MSK background and threshold: in 53,113 tumors, a cancer-agnostic background included 93,428 pairs from 28,215 individuals. The cancer-type-specific mismatch-repair-proficient analysis included 204,725 pairs from 32,172 patients: 87.4% shared no variant, 12.1% one, and 0.5% at least two. A single hotspot yielded lower MPS than a shared private variant. Calling any shared variant “similar” would classify 12.6% of random pairs, versus 4.6% at MPS at least 99% (`P<0.0001`).
- Source inconsistency: for mismatch-repair-deficient random pairs sharing KRAS G12D, the prose reports median MPS 26.7% with an IQR printed as 32%-40.9%. The median cannot fall outside its own IQR. The exact IQR is withheld rather than silently corrected; the qualitative lesson that a shared hotspot may be weak evidence in a high-background context remains defensible.
- Known-repeat validation: among nine ovarian-cancer patients with 33 samples, within-patient comparisons had median MPS 99.9% and between-patient comparisons 0.01%. In 2,012 patients with two tumors, 1,735 were initially considered the same cancer and 277 distinct. Variant sharing occurred in 96.5% versus 6.1%. MPS at least 99% had PPV 99.2% (1,556/1,568), sensitivity 90%, specificity 96%, and NPV 60%. The low NPV means low similarity does not itself prove distinct origin.
- Discordance and clinical evolution: MPS and the initial clinical classification disagreed in 191/2,012 (9.5%). High-MPS calls agreed with clinical diagnosis in 99.2%, whereas low-MPS calls agreed in only 59.7% (265/444). In a selected 80-patient longitudinally reviewed discordant subset, 22 (28%) later underwent a diagnostic change predicted by MPS; some changes altered treatment. This enriched discordant subset cannot yield a general-population reclassification rate or prove improved outcome.
- Histology/lineage checks: among 23 squamous-cell carcinoma pairs, 8/9 clinically distinct pairs had MPS below 1%; the remaining high-MPS pair was later judged metastatic. Of 14 initially considered the same, 13 had MPS at least 99%; the single nonsharing pair was later judged synchronous primary tumors. All 14 examined lineage-transformation cases had MPS at least 99%.
- Plasma extension: among 884 patients with 1,875 plasma/tumor pairs in the background analysis, two shared variants generally produced MPS above 99%, with lung and ovarian exceptions. In 14 patients with multiple solid tumors and one plasma sample, MPS identified the clinically persistent tumor in 10/14 (71%). The time between specimens did not correlate with MPS (`R=-0.03`). These small selected examples do not validate plasma source assignment broadly.
- Claim ceiling: MPS provides contextual evidence of clonal relatedness and can complement clinicopathologic review. It does not replace diagnosis, prove that low-MPS tumors are independent, or establish outcome benefit from MPS-guided decisions. Performance depends on panel content, tumor biology, detectable shared variants, appropriate cancer-specific backgrounds, and at least about 50 comparable background tumors; hypermutated settings, missing matched normal, germline/polymorphic variants, and the method's focus on shared rather than discordant variants are important limits.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results distinguishes high PPV/specificity from the much lower NPV and links clinical utility to later diagnostic review rather than treating MPS as an autonomous ground truth (pp8-10). The Discussion explicitly retains pathology/clinical context and limitations of panels, background size, hypermutation, and germline variation (pp10-12).
- Safe template: “A very high MPS supported a shared clonal origin with high positive predictive value, whereas a low score was less definitive; the result should therefore complement, rather than replace, clinicopathologic adjudication.”
- Unsafe transfer: copying the internally impossible 26.7%/32%-40.9% summary; treating MPS below threshold as proof of independent primary tumors; applying the 28% selected-discordance reclassification rate to all patients; or claiming that MPS-guided treatment improves outcomes.

### Six gates and verdict

- `coverage_check=pass`; `evidence_check=pass`; `results_check=pass`; `language_check=pass`; `traceability_check=pass`; `transfer_check=pass`.
- Verdict: `passed`, with the impossible median/IQR combination explicitly withheld.

- Issue year/version: 2025; `author_manuscript`.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: multicenter, open-label, single-arm phase I/II trial of weekly selinexor plus docetaxel every three weeks in previously treated advanced KRAS-mutant NSCLC. Phase I focused on dose-limiting toxicity and dose selection; phase II used RECIST response as the principal activity measure. Biomarker analyses were exploratory.
- Analysis sets: 40 patients were treated (seven dose escalation, 33 expansion) and entered safety analyses. Efficacy was evaluated in 32 who completed at least one cycle or stopped earlier for toxicity/progression. The phase II design planned approximately 35 patients and 85% power to distinguish a 10% from 35% response rate at two-sided alpha 0.05; exploratory biomarker tests were not multiplicity-adjusted.
- Dose selection: selinexor 60 mg weekly plus docetaxel 75 mg/m² every three weeks was selected as the recommended phase II dose. At 80 mg, the first three patients included one formal dose-limiting grade 4 neutropenia, but fatigue, nausea, vomiting, and neutropenia occurring beyond the protocol DLT window/criteria made that dose clinically infeasible. Formal DLT counting alone therefore understates tolerability.
- Activity: among 32 efficacy-evaluable patients, seven had PR (22%), 18 SD (56%), and seven PD (22%), for DCR 78%. Cross-trial comparison with docetaxel alone cannot establish an added selinexor effect.
- KRASG12C analysis: the treated cohort contained 11 KRASG12C and 29 non-G12C tumors. Response differences were not significant (`P=0.30`). Median PFS was 4.1 versus 4.0 months (reported HR 0.88, 0.43-1.83; `P=0.73`) and median OS 6.5 versus 7.5 months (HR 0.95, 0.43-2.10; `P=0.91`). These are negative subgroup results, not evidence of mutation-subtype equivalence.
- TP53 analysis and direction: NGS was available in 26 patients: 11 TP53-altered and 15 wild type. Response/DCR were 9%/27% in altered versus 27%/80% in wild type (`P=0.03` for the reported comparison). Median PFS was 1.8 versus 7.4 months and median OS 5.8 versus 15.7 months. The reported HRs of 0.22 (`P=0.003`) and 0.36 (`P=0.02`) are oriented as wild type relative to altered, consistent with the displayed survival curves. These exploratory associations cannot distinguish predictive selinexor sensitivity from prognosis or docetaxel response.
- Source inconsistencies and denominator hazards: the abstract calls “wild type TP53” 42%, but the Results gives 11/26 (42%) altered and 15/26 (58%) wild type; the denominator-defined body result is retained and the abstract statement treated as erroneous. The abstract reports grade at least 3 neutropenia in 60%, whereas the Results and Table 2 report 25/40 (63%); both source locations are disclosed rather than reconciled silently. Table 1 mixes percentage denominators: TP53 is printed 11 (28), apparently over all 40 despite NGS n=26, STK11 7 (27) is over 26, and KEAP1 2 (18) appears over a smaller subgroup. Counts and explicitly defined subset denominators take precedence; ambiguous percentages are not transferred.
- Other exploratory biomarkers: YAP immunohistochemistry was available in only 12 patients (six high, six low). Response was 17% in each, DCR 66% versus 83% (`P=1.0`), and PFS/OS comparisons were nonsignificant (`P=0.20` for each). RNA sequencing was available in 16; pathway results involving NF-kappaB, WNT, and p53 were exploratory. Cytokines were analyzable in 36/40. After the selinexor lead-in, LDH changed by +51% in TP53-altered versus -48% in wild-type tumors, but `P=0.06`; this must remain a nonsignificant trend.
- Safety: common all-grade events included nausea 73%, fatigue 70%, neutropenia 65%, diarrhea 58%, anorexia 50%, vomiting 45%, alopecia 45%, and anemia 40%. Results/Table 2 report grade at least 3 neutropenia in 25/40 (63%), hyponatremia 15%, dyspnea/respiratory failure 15%, anemia 10%, and diarrhea 10%. Selinexor required dose reduction/delay/discontinuation in 7/25/5 patients and docetaxel in 12/16/11; 13 received G-CSF. The main report does not clearly characterize deaths as treatment-related, so no stronger fatal-toxicity claim is made.
- Claim ceiling: the combination had single-arm activity and a defined tolerability profile, with 60 mg selected for further study. TP53 status is an exploratory prognostic/predictive candidate, not a validated selector. The design cannot attribute benefit to selinexor, establish superiority to docetaxel, or assign causality to lead-in biomarker changes.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results reports the KRASG12C and YAP comparisons as nonsignificant and presents LDH as a trend at `P=0.06` (pp8-10). The Discussion recognizes that small correlative subsets and absence of a control arm prevent separating prognostic from predictive TP53 effects (pp10-12).

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: preclinical development of a proprietary full-length human Trop-2.2 antibody and tetrazine/trans-cyclooctene pretargeting system for immunoPET. Evidence came from in-vitro chemistry/cell assays and mouse xenografts; there were no patients, no human diagnostic-accuracy cohort, and no clinical safety data.
- Method chain: random-lysine and engineered-cysteine site-specific antibody conjugation were compared. Direct imaging used zirconium-89-labeled Trop-2.2, while pretargeting tested zirconium-89 DFO-PEG7-tetrazine, fluorine-18 Al-NOTA-PEG7-tetrazine, and copper-64 Sar-tetrazine. Trop-2 expression models included BxPC3 high, PC3 low, Calu6 negative, and OVCAR3 positive cells. In-vitro technical experiments were generally triplicate.
- Direct-imaging results: radioconjugates were more than 90% stable in serum at 144 hours and more than 96% at 72 hours under the reported conditions. In BxPC3-bearing mice (typically n=5 per timepoint), direct zirconium-89 Trop-2.2 showed uptake by three hours, approximately 75%-100% injected dose/g by 24 hours, and maximal uptake around day 3. These values are model-specific biodistribution, not human sensitivity or dosimetry.
- Pretargeting results: at a 24-hour antibody-to-tetrazine interval, the zirconium-89 tetrazine had unfavorable distribution and low tumor uptake. Copper-64 Sar-tetrazine performed best among the tested agents, with rapid tumor visualization at four hours and background clearance by 24 hours. Fluorine-18 showed modest tumor uptake with residual gastrointestinal activity. Pretargeted groups were small, generally n=4.
- Negative engineering result: the engineered-cysteine, site-specific conjugate carried more TCO per antibody (2.42 versus 1.65) but had lower tumor uptake and did not improve imaging performance over random-lysine conjugation. This negative result constrains any general claim that site-specific conjugation was superior.
- Specificity/statistics boundary: specificity was supported by in-vitro blocking and the high/low/negative expression models, but a conventional in-vivo blocking or isotype-control experiment was not presented in the main PDF, and enhanced permeability/retention cannot be completely excluded. Most in-vivo results were descriptive, with small groups and no clearly specified multiplicity strategy.
- Claim ceiling: the copper-64 pretargeted platform is a promising preclinical candidate for rapid Trop-2 imaging. It does not establish human tumor detection, clinical sensitivity/specificity, patient selection for Trop-2 therapy, prediction of treatment response, acceptable human radiation exposure, or clinical safety. “Companion diagnostic” remains a future-use hypothesis.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results contrasts candidate radionuclides and explicitly retains the failure of site-specific conjugation to improve tumor uptake (pp8-9). The Discussion frames clinical use and companion-diagnostic potential as future translation, while recognizing pharmacokinetic and pretargeting constraints (pp9-11).
- Safe template: “Among the tested pretargeting agents, [agent] provided the most favorable mouse tumor-to-background profile; this preclinical comparison supports further translational evaluation but does not establish clinical diagnostic performance.”
- Unsafe transfer: calling the platform clinically validated; converting mouse `%ID/g` into human sensitivity; asserting site-specific conjugation superiority; claiming selection or outcome prediction for Trop-2 therapy; or implying human safety.

- Verdict: `passed`.

## PMID 39495173

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *YAP Regulates HER3 Signaling-Driven Adaptive Resistance to RET Inhibitors in RET-Aberrant Cancers*.
- Issue year/version: 2025; `not_classified_by_header`.
- Registered PDF: `Katayama_YAP_HER3_RET_adaptive_resistance_PMID39495173.pdf`; SHA-256 `abc7996da2620f455d56d394fee656c153cb0a0bb1eb70dd483aca4477186c6d`; 15 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_supplied`; no separate supplement was treated as reviewed.
- Section map: abstract/introduction pp1-3; methods pp2-4 and online-method references; Results pp3-11; Discussion pp11-13; references/admin pp13-15.
- Main displays visually checked: Figure 1 p4; Figure 2 p6; Figure 3 p8; Figure 4 p10; Figure 5 p11; Figure 6 p12. All cell-line labels, knockdown/combination contrasts, xenograft group sizes, survival risk sets, immunohistochemistry strata, legends, and negative-result annotations were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: mechanistic study of adaptive RET-inhibitor resistance using RET-aberrant lung, thyroid, and mesothelioma cell lines, RNA sequencing, phospho-RTK profiling, knockdown/overexpression, promoter assays, ChIP, drug combinations, and cell-line-derived xenografts, followed by a retrospective clinical association analysis in 23 patients with RET-fusion NSCLC treated with selpercatinib across 14 centers.
- Cellular mechanism: selpercatinib initially suppressed ERK in all examined models, but ERK reactivated in the less-sensitive high-YAP LC-2/ad and TPC1 lines and remained suppressed in the more-sensitive low-YAP EHMES10 and TT lines. HER3 abundance/phosphorylation increased in the less-sensitive lines. HER3 knockdown plus RET inhibition lowered viability and ERK/AKT signaling there. In the low-YAP lines, adding HER3 or YAP knockdown to RET inhibition was not significant, an important model-dependent negative result.
- Omics/regulatory chain: RNA sequencing identified 3,924 upregulated genes in LC-2/ad and 754 in TPC1, with 366 shared at FDR below 0.05; enrichment implicated TEAD/YAP programs. Nuclear YAP increased during RET inhibition. ChIP and promoter assays supported YAP/TEAD regulation of HER3/NRG1; YAP knockdown reduced those signals and adaptive resistance, whereas YAP overexpression increased resistance. Ajuba perturbation affected TPC1 but was only marginal in LC-2/ad, so the upstream mechanism was not uniform.
- Pharmacologic/preclinical findings: afatinib or YAP-TEAD inhibitors enhanced RET-TKI effects mainly in high-YAP models and had marginal effects in low-YAP models. Drug-tolerant cells relied on HER3, but adding selpercatinib to afatinib did not further reduce their viability versus afatinib alone. In xenografts (n=9/group), selpercatinib plus afatinib reduced tumor size more than selpercatinib alone, without discernible body-weight loss. Ki67 and phospho-ERK were lower with combination, whereas TUNEL was not significantly different.
- Clinical cohort: 23 patients had pretreatment tissue; 11 were treatment-naive and 12 previously treated. Fusions included KIF5B in 16, CCDC6 in four, TMF1 in one, and unknown partners in two. Cytoplasmic YAP was low in nine and high in 14. This small, retrospectively selected set supplied no untreated or alternate-treatment comparator and no multivariable adjustment.
- Clinical outcome and source-rhetoric boundary: high versus low cytoplasmic YAP had median PFS 10.1 months versus not reached, but the difference was not significant (`P=0.09`). For OS, both groups had median not reached, while the log-rank test was `P=0.04`. The paper describes OS as significantly shorter in one location but later summarizes a “trend toward shorter PFS and OS.” Both the fact that both OS medians were not reached and the nominal `P=0.04` are retained; neither is replaced by a fabricated median difference. Nuclear YAP and HER3 were not associated with PFS or OS, and cytoplasmic YAP did not correlate with tumor shrinkage.
- Prediction boundary: the paper uses predictive language, but the clinical analysis shows an association among selpercatinib-treated patients without a treatment interaction. It cannot distinguish treatment-specific prediction from prognosis or confounding. Clinical evidence pertains to RET-fusion NSCLC; the thyroid/mesothelioma extension in the title is supported only by preclinical models.
- Safety boundary: absence of discernible weight loss in mice does not establish human combination tolerability. No patients received the proposed RET/HER-family or YAP-targeted combination in this report.
- Claim ceiling: YAP-HER3 signaling is supported as a context-dependent adaptive-resistance mechanism in preclinical RET-aberrant models, and cytoplasmic YAP is a hypothesis-generating clinical outcome marker. The study does not validate cytoplasmic YAP as a predictive biomarker, prove human combination efficacy/safety, or generalize clinical findings across all RET-aberrant cancers.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results retains nonsignificant PFS, negative nuclear-YAP/HER3 analyses, and lack of tumor-shrinkage correlation alongside the nominal OS test (pp10-11). The Discussion emphasizes the small retrospective cohort and need for larger studies (pp11-13).
- Safe template: “High cytoplasmic YAP was associated with a nominal OS separation (`P=0.04`) although both medians were not reached, while PFS was not statistically significant (`P=0.09`); the small treated-only cohort therefore supports a candidate prognostic/predictive signal rather than a validated treatment selector.”
- Unsafe transfer: reporting a nonexistent OS median difference; omitting the nonsignificant PFS and negative nuclear-YAP/HER3 results; calling YAP predictive without a treatment interaction; inferring human combination safety from mouse weight; or extending the NSCLC clinical association to thyroid or mesothelioma patients.

### Six gates and verdict

- `coverage_check=pass`; `evidence_check=pass`; `results_check=pass`; `language_check=pass`; `traceability_check=pass`; `transfer_check=pass`.
- Verdict: `passed`, with both not-reached OS medians, the nominal `P=0.04`, and the paper's “significant” versus “trend” wording retained.

## PMID 40552922

### Identity and coverage

- Title: *Combined Targeting of PD-1 and TIM-3 in Patients with Locally Advanced or Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: AMBER Part 2B*.
- Issue year/version: 2025; `not_classified_by_header`.
- Registered PDF: `Davar_Combined_Targeting_of_PD-1_and_TIM-3_in_Patients_with_Locally_Advanced_or_Metastatic_Non-Small_Ce_PMID40552922.pdf`; SHA-256 `4f56f5d0b66936052dda520852f33b96b3f84a702817afa139bfa58f45149930`; 9 physical pages.
- Supplement status: `not_recorded`; no separate supplement was treated as reviewed.
- Section map: abstract/introduction pp1-2; methods pp2-3; Results pp3-6; Discussion pp6-8; references/admin pp8-9.
- Main displays visually checked: Table 1 p4; Tables 2 and 3 p5; Figures 1 and 2 p6; Figure 3 p7. Dose cohorts, response denominators, waterfall exclusions, survival risk sets, adverse-event grades/attribution, biomarker subset sizes, legends, and footnotes were checked.

### Evidence and result reconstruction

- Question/design: multicenter, open-label phase I AMBER part 2B evaluated cobolimab 100, 300, or 900 mg plus dostarlimab 500 mg every three weeks for up to two years in locally advanced/metastatic NSCLC that had progressed after anti-PD-(L)1 therapy. There was no randomization or dostarlimab-alone control, so the contribution of TIM-3 blockade cannot be isolated.
- Analysis sets/design: 85 patients enrolled and 84 were treated and included in safety/efficacy analyses: 14, 41, and 29 across the three dose cohorts. The primary endpoint was confirmed RECIST ORR; DCR required CR/PR or SD lasting at least 16 weeks. Tumors were assessed every eight weeks in year 1 and every 12 weeks thereafter. The Simon minimax plan targeted a 5% null versus 15% alternative ORR at one-sided alpha 10% and 80% power, but final allocation stopped below the planned roughly 44 patients per dose based on the evolving totality of occupancy, safety, and efficacy data.
- RECIST activity: overall confirmed ORR was 7/84 (8.3%, 95% CI 3.4%-16.4%) and DCR 18/84 (21.4%, 13.2%-31.7%). ORR by dose was 1/14 (7.1%), 4/41 (9.8%), and 2/29 (6.9%); DCR was 3/14 (21.4%), 9/41 (22.0%), and 6/29 (20.7%). Similar proportions across small nonrandomized cohorts do not demonstrate dose equivalence or a dose-response relation.
- Immune-response and display denominators: immune-related ORR was 8/84 (9.5%) and immune-related DCR 21/84 (25.0%). RECIST best overall response included 46 PD, six not evaluable, and 14 with no assessment. One patient classified as RECIST PD but immune-related PR later had an unconfirmed CR; that patient is not a confirmed RECIST response. Figure 1's lesion-change display uses n=69 because 15 patients lacked evaluable postbaseline measurements, whereas efficacy rates use n=84.
- Time-to-event outcomes: median PFS was 1.9 months (0.8-3.4), 2.1 months (1.9-3.8), and 2.1 months (1.9-2.2) across 100/300/900 mg. Median OS was 11.6 months (2.8-not reached), 6.2 months (4.1-12.9), and 9.0 months (3.7-14.7). No formal between-dose superiority comparison was established. Selection of 300 mg for further study relied on broader AMBER pharmacologic/occupancy, safety, and efficacy information, not superior survival in this report.
- Safety: 83/84 (98.8%) had a treatment-emergent adverse event, 44/84 (52.4%) a treatment-related event, 46/84 (54.8%) a grade at least 3 TEAE, and 11/84 (13.1%) a grade at least 3 treatment-related event. Serious TEAEs occurred in 38/84 (45.2%). Immune-related TEAEs occurred in 16/84 (19.0%) and serious immune-related events in two (2.4%: pneumonitis and pancreatitis). Five patients (6.0%) discontinued because of TEAEs and 18 (21.4%) had delays. Four fatal TEAEs occurred (4.8%), but none was considered treatment-related; the Results explicitly reports no treatment-related deaths.
- Exploratory biomarker findings: in the available biopsy subset, PD-L1 and TIM-3 were not correlated (`R=0.16`, `P=0.37`), and TIM-3 did not significantly differ by histology (`P=0.48`). TIM-3 was higher in combined PR/SD than PD samples by RECIST (`P=0.031`; PD n=25, PR+SD n=10) and immune-related criteria (`P=0.013`; PD n=22, irPR+irSD n=12). These post hoc, selected, small comparisons had no stated multiplicity correction, omit nonevaluable/no-assessment cases, and do not establish a cutoff or predictive interaction.
- Claim ceiling: the combination showed preliminary single-arm activity and a characterized safety profile after prior checkpoint inhibition. It does not establish efficacy over PD-1 re-challenge/dostarlimab, a dose-response effect, a survival advantage, or TIM-3 as a predictive biomarker. Fatal TEAEs must be reported separately from treatment-related deaths.

### Language and transfer

- Context-checked language: the Results gives exact treated/evaluable denominators and separates fatal TEAEs from treatment-related attribution (pp3-6). The Discussion describes activity as preliminary and the biomarker analysis as hypothesis-generating, while noting the absence of a randomized comparator (pp6-8).
- Safe template: “Cobolimab plus dostarlimab produced an 8.3% confirmed ORR in 84 previously checkpoint-treated patients; without a monotherapy or randomized control, the result indicates preliminary combination activity but cannot establish the contribution of TIM-3 blockade.”
- Unsafe transfer: interpreting n=69 waterfall plots as the efficacy denominator; calling the unconfirmed immune-related CR a confirmed RECIST response; claiming 300 mg was superior; omitting four fatal TEAEs or calling them treatment-related; or presenting the post hoc TIM-3 association as a validated predictor.

- Verdict: `passed`.
