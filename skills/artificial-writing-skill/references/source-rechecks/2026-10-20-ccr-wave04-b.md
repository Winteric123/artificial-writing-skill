### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: multicenter open-label randomized phase II study testing whether ceralasertib or adavosertib added to olaparib improved second- or third-line outcomes in PARP-inhibitor-naïve advanced/metastatic TNBC. Patients were assigned within BRCA1/2-mutated, non-BRCA1/2 homologous-recombination-repair-mutated (HRRm), or non-HRRm strata. Of 273 randomized patients, 114 received an olaparib assignment, 112 ceralasertib plus olaparib, and 47 adavosertib plus olaparib; the molecular-stratum totals were 96, 47, and 130, respectively.
- Comparator/analysis sets: the primary endpoint was BICR-assessed RECIST 1.1 PFS for ceralasertib plus olaparib versus olaparib within each stratum, using 90% CIs and a two-sided 10% significance level. The full analysis set comprised all randomized patients. The safety set comprised 265 treated patients: 110 olaparib, 109 ceralasertib plus olaparib, and 46 adavosertib plus olaparib. OS did not reach the planned 70% maturity in any stratum and was not reported. Exploratory archival FoundationOne CDx analyses evaluated selected oncogenic alterations in the non-HRRm stratum.

- Positive findings: a nominal non-HRRm ORR increase was observed, and ceralasertib plus olaparib was feasible at the studied schedule. These are placed after the negative PFS result and bounded by the lack of PFS corroboration.
- Negative/uncertain findings: no primary PFS comparison was significant; pooled ORR and the other stratum-specific ORRs were not significant; OS was immature and unreported; selected oncogenic alterations did not significantly distinguish efficacy; and the non-BRCA1/2m HRRm stratum was markedly underaccrued.
- Safety findings: ceralasertib increased grade ≥3 toxicity, discontinuation, and dose modification relative to olaparib; adavosertib plus olaparib produced the highest toxicity and was closed early. Four AE-associated deaths were reported but none was attributed to treatment.
- Source alerts: (1) the abstract phrase “no unexpected safety signals” applies to ceralasertib or olaparib and cannot erase the adavosertib safety closure; (2) the Discussion calls HR 0.54 “notable” if significant, but it was not significant and is retained only as an uncertain point estimate; (3) the non-HRRm ORR signal is explicitly subordinate to the corresponding negative PFS result; (4) OS is absent, not negative; and (5) no formal treatment-by-marker interaction validates the exploratory genomic subgroup.
- Limitations: early study termination; incomplete accrual/event targets in two strata; only the non-HRRm stratum fully accrued; small non-BRCA1/2m HRRm samples; OS immaturity; an early-closed comparator arm; multiple secondary/exploratory comparisons; very small response/DoR denominators; possible schedule inadequacy; archival selected-gene testing; no supplied supplementary file.
- Claim ceiling: at the tested dose and schedule, adding ceralasertib to olaparib did not significantly improve the primary PFS endpoint in any molecular stratum. A non-HRRm ORR signal was nominal and uncorroborated by PFS, and the exploratory oncogene panel did not establish prediction. Adavosertib-plus-olaparib efficacy is uninterpretable because the arm closed for toxicity.

### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/population: retrospective pan-cancer characterization of MYC-family focal/broad amplification architecture, genomic context, expression, and outcome. The MSK-IMPACT matched tumor-normal series began with 68,920 specimens; after FACETS quality/purity filtering and restriction to one sample per tumor type per patient, 45,468 tumors remained. TCGA began with 10,683 matched-normal exomes and retained 8,477 after quality filtering.

TCGA expression analyses were tumor-type-specific available-case comparisons, and cross-validated GLMNET selected correlates of MYC expression but was not an external predictive validation. OS began at sequencing in 1,432 metastatic patients with MYC amplification; separate multivariable models were fit for seven histologies with at least 40 cases.

- Positive findings: the study delineated frequent lineage-specific differences in prevalence, focality, amplitude, clonality, ecDNA inference, genomic context, and RNA output across a large tumor-normal sequencing cohort.
- Negative/uncertain findings: most OS associations did not survive correction; pancreatic associations were nominal only; several expression comparisons and MYC-target gene-set comparisons were null; focality did not uniformly affect expression; MYCL/MYCN analyses were limited by sample size; and no treatment data linked architecture to therapeutic benefit.
- Safety findings: not applicable; this was an observational genomic study without an intervention or adverse-event analysis.
- Source alerts: (1) the Discussion's “strong oncogenic drivers” wording exceeds what observational mutual-exclusivity/expression data alone prove and is not transferred as causal fact; (2) pancreatic OS nominal P values must always be paired with BH-adjusted P=0.14; (3) treatment was not collected, so associations are prognostic/contextual rather than predictive; and (4) cross-validated GLMNET variable selection is not external model validation.
- Limitations: retrospective single-institution MSK cohort; variable sampling time; no treatment data; unknown de novo versus acquired status; tumor-type/available-case selection; small MYCL/MYCN subsets, especially in TCGA; mRNA rather than direct protein dependency; proprietary ecDNA inference; multiple testing; pancreatic n=47; no external validation; no supplied supplementary file.
- Claim ceiling: MYC-family amplifications show lineage-specific genomic architectures and context-dependent transcriptional associations. The study supplies a landscape and hypotheses for trial eligibility, but it does not establish MYC dependency, therapeutic sensitivity, a predictive cutoff, or multiplicity-robust survival effects.

- Negative/uncertain findings: the comet comparison was nonsignificant; Rb-proficient and Rb-deficient responses were heterogeneous; some xenografts regrew; single agents did not significantly extend xenograft survival; ANC reduction was nonsignificant; and human omics did not test drug response.
- Safety findings: the limited mouse laboratory screen did not show major renal, hepatic, weight, or significant neutrophil toxicity, but platelets declined modestly. - Source alerts: (1) Results p14 overstates the comet assay relative to Figure 3D and Discussion p18; the visual/statistical null controls the wording; (2) the paper's “high therapeutic index,” “acceptable therapeutic window,” and “rapidly translated” statements are forward-looking speculation; (3) an athymic nude-mouse xenograft cannot demonstrate immune-mediated benefit from pyroptosis; (4) the in-vivo AID system used doxycycline without IAA, so depletion was incomplete; and (5) many inferential details reside in unsupplied supplementary tables.
- Limitations: selected cell lines; heterogeneous responses; live-cell n=40 and one-daughter tracking; a single clonally selected xenograft in immunodeficient mice; humane endpoint rather than clinical survival; incomplete in-vivo degradation; pharmacologic safety and genetic efficacy systems not matched; one time-point tolerability screen; Rb deficiency defined by HPV or median signature in human datasets; basal expression rather than response; multiple assays with supplementary-only statistics; no organoid, immunocompetent, clinical, or therapeutic-index validation.
- Claim ceiling: dual TRIP13/Aurora A perturbation produced a reproducible preclinical mitotic-death phenotype in selected Rb-deficient models and suppressed one xenograft. The work does not establish clinical efficacy, clinical safety, predictive Rb selection, immune-mediated benefit, or a ready therapeutic window.

### Evidence reconstruction and central-result checks

- Question/design/model: preclinical biophysical/translational study asking whether short-pulse selective irreversible electroporation (sIRE) can preferentially lyse malignant pleural mesothelioma cells while sparing CAR T cells, releasing chemoattractants, increasing intravenously administered CAR T-cell infiltration, and accelerating tumor clearance. Evidence included FEM simulations; suspension/3D collagen parameter screens; human MSTO-GM and murine AB12 models; MSLN CAR T cells from healthy donors; a syngeneic immunocompetent AB12 TIL experiment; and human xenograft/CAR T experiments in NSG mice.
- Comparator/analysis sets: sIRE was selected as 1,000 V/cm, 10 μs, 200 pulses and compared with sham and conventional IRE. CAR-only, sIRE-only, single sIRE plus CAR T, and repeated sIRE plus CAR T groups were used in vivo. Experiments were described as triplicate with duplicate measurements where possible; animal sample-size planning used alpha 0.05 and 80% power, but exact per-arm n was not stated in the main PDF. Many tests were conducted without a reported multiplicity plan, and the statistical paragraph ambiguously combines ANOVA/Kruskal–Wallis and unadjusted pairwise tests.

- Positive findings: sIRE produced model-specific differential lysis, chemoattractant release, greater early intratumoral CAR T accumulation, and faster primary-tumor clearance when given before CAR T cells.

- Safety findings: the main PDF does not provide a systemic toxicity, organ injury, procedural complication, or clinical safety analysis. T-cell sparing is a mechanistic selectivity endpoint, not a safety profile. Statements invoking the established clinical safety of conventional IRE cannot establish sIRE safety.
- Source alerts: (1) the Figure 6 title says sIRE “promotes robust systemic immunity,” but the CAR-only group also cleared both rechallenges and survived to the same endpoint; sIRE-specific immune memory is not identified; (2) the Results heading says the combination “prolongs survival,” yet all three CAR groups reached the endpoint, so the survival contrast is CAR-containing therapy versus sham/sIRE-only, not an incremental sIRE effect; (3) repeat sIRE produced mainly null or adverse-direction distribution findings despite a favorable interpretive sentence; (4) text cross-references “Figure 2h/2o” in the Figure 5 results are apparent citation errors and are read as Figure 5H/5O; and (5) exact animal denominators and a multiplicity strategy are absent from the main PDF.
- Limitations: single cancer/model family; subcutaneous rather than orthotopic disease; NSG xenograft for human CAR T efficacy; main-PDF group sizes unstated; no blinding because of visible treatment; multiple comparisons and unclear panel-wise test mapping; selected pulse geometry; no IRE cohort in some later experiments; semiquantitative cytokine array; no causal chemokine blockade; no macrophage/Treg assessment; no clinical scale, procedural toxicity, organ injury, or patient data; supplement status not recorded and no separate supplement supplied.
- Claim ceiling: sIRE is a preclinical pulse-optimization strategy that preferentially spared CAR T cells in selected mesothelioma systems and accelerated early tumor clearance when paired with CAR T therapy. It does not yet establish clinical feasibility/safety, cancer-agnostic selectivity, added survival benefit, or sIRE-specific systemic immune memory.
