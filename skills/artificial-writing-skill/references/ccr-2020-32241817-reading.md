## Research reconstruction

The study retrospectively asks whether NOTCH alteration classes identify differential benefit from checkpoint inhibition in NSCLC, with principal analyses restricted to EGFR/ALK-wild-type disease. It combines a 58-patient 3DMed clinical cohort with published Rizvi (240), POPLAR/OAK (853), Van Allen (56) and MSKCC (350) datasets, plus separate TCGA DNA/RNA analyses of pathway and immune correlates. The sum of cohort sizes is 1,557, but it is **not a verified count of unique ICI-treated people**: POPLAR/OAK contains docetaxel controls and the Methods acknowledges overlap between Rizvi and MSKCC. Different endpoints do not make overlapping people independent participants.

The 3DMed cohort used pretreatment plasma ctDNA targeted sequencing; public cohorts used tissue panels, plasma panels or WES. TCGA bulk RNA data support expression/GSEA and CIBERSORT analyses. This is integrated DNA, transcriptomic and clinical evidence across different datasets, not matched multi-omics in every treatment participant. PD-L1 IHC is a protein-marker assay, not proteomics. No new single-cell profiling, functional NOTCH perturbation or therapeutic NOTCH-inhibitor experiment was performed in this article.

The article distinguishes NOTCH1/2/3 from NOTCH4. Its operational del-NOTCH category combines truncating NOTCH1/2/3 mutations and missense mutations with PolyPhen-2 score ≥0.800. Lower-scoring NOTCH1/2/3 missense mutations and NOTCH4 mutations enter the non-deleterious class. This is an in-silico operational classification: PolyPhen-2 damage prediction does not establish gain versus loss of function, a limitation explicitly admitted on p12. The small local panel does not assay NOTCH4 (p11).

Evidence sequence: initial response/survival association → public-cohort endpoint-specific pooling → mutation-class definition → TMB/PD-L1 and bulk-transcriptomic correlates → retrospective biomarker-by-treatment comparison within POPLAR/OAK → multivariable outcome associations. Its strongest defensible conclusion is that a study-defined NOTCH class is a **candidate treatment-associated/predictive biomarker**, with a reported PFS treatment interaction, requiring prospective validation. The main article does not establish prospective prespecification of this classifier. It is not a clinically established treatment-selection test. The OS interaction was not significant, and proposed NOTCH inhibition plus ICI remains a hypothesis.

## Source alerts and interpretation limits

1. **No clinical combination test:** the abstract/TR/closing discussion proposes NOTCH inhibition plus ICI. This article does not demonstrate its efficacy or safety in patients, organoids, cell lines or xenografts.
2. **Prediction is endpoint-specific:** reported PFS interaction P=.012 contrasts with OS interaction P=.255. The title/Discussion's broad predictive-not-prognostic wording should be qualified. The null TCGA association is in a different-stage population, not proof of absent prognostic relevance.
3. **Numerical source conflicts:** PFS94% versus95% CI; 3DMed OS HR/CI across Fig1/2; docetaxel ORR9.3% versus5.6%; WT PFS P=.279 versus.297. Values with conflicts are recorded for audit, not offered as reusable exact results.
4. **Transcriptomic language is stronger than some displayed tests:** Fig3I MMR P=.053; Fig4A del-versus-WT IL12 P=.057 and IL12-family P=.077; steroid-metabolism P=.053. Fig4B IFNG P=.054 and LAG3 P=.094 are not conventionally significant. Do not describe every DDR/immune pathway or selected immune gene as significantly increased. Displayed P=0 is rounded software output, not literal zero probability. The study uses nominal significance across many tests; no unreported FDR control may be assumed.
5. **Mechanism remains inferred:** CIBERSORT estimates bulk-RNA cell fractions; GSEA captures signatures, not direct cell activation/function or experimentally proven mutagenesis. Higher DNA-repair expression does not directly measure repair proficiency.
6. **Functional classification is imperfect:** damage-score grouping is not experimentally established loss of function. Do not transfer the study-specific NOTCH classifier to STK11 or other genes.
7. **STK11 relevance is bounded:** direct STK11-adjusted outcome associations exist in Table1, making this useful secondary clinical context for STK11 writing. It is not a dedicated STK11 mechanism paper, a NOTCH–STK11 interaction study or a new STK11 framework highlight without explicit designation.
