## Research question and evidence chain

The paper asks why HER3 expression varies in EGFR-mutant NSCLC, whether acquired EGFR-TKI resistance and continued TKI exposure change HER3, and whether treatment-induced HER3 augmentation can support HER3-directed antibody–drug conjugate activity. Its evidence chain has distinct levels: paired clinical tissue and molecular associations → transcriptomic pathway analysis → pharmacological/genetic cell perturbations → colony and biochemical combination readouts. The clinical observations and cellular intervention evidence should not be collapsed into proven patient benefit or a completed clinical mechanism of action.

Independent molecular layers include targeted DNA profiling and bulk RNA sequencing in clinical material; public TCGA RNA and RPPA protein data supply additional cross-cohort context. HER3 IHC and immunoblots are targeted protein measurements, not a newly generated untargeted proteome. This is molecular multiomics, but not all 48 patients had every assay, and public protein cohorts are not the paired 48-patient clinical cohort. No STK11-focused analysis or perturbation is reported in the supplied main article.

## Population, analysis sets and design

- Retrospective 48 patients from three Japanese hospitals, with paired pretreatment and acquired-resistance FFPE specimens: 48 pairs, not 48 total specimens. The patients had initially responded to EGFR-TKI therapy. Specimens were collected during 2018–2020 (pp2,4).
- Membranous HER3 IHC uses a 0–300 H-score, scored by two blinded pulmonary pathologists. The formula weights the percentage of cells staining 1+, 2+ or 3+ by intensity (p2). Baseline HER3 positivity is 39/48 (81%) and post-resistance positivity 43/48 (90%). Positivity, H-score increase and copy-number gain are different endpoints.
- TSO500 DNA profiling covers variants, copy number and TMB; RNA panel analysis calls fusions/splicing, and a separate whole-transcriptome RNA-seq workflow supports expression and pathway analysis (p3). Main-text correlation of baseline HER3 IHC with RNA uses n=28, whereas baseline GSEA uses n=32; these cannot be treated as an identical matched assay set. Full QC/sample availability is referred to external S8 and is not resolved by the supplied article.
- Baseline EGFR copy-number comparison includes 47 evaluable samples: non-gain n=37 and gain n=10; copy-number gain threshold is at least four copies (p4). Do not silently include the 48th patient.
- Public analyses use distinct available-case cohorts: TCGA HER3 RNA/protein correlation n=360, EGFR-mutant subset n=26 reported with external S1, and HER3/E-cadherin protein correlation n=706 (pp4–6). These denominators concern different analyses; no single universal TCGA denominator should be used.
- The paper separates HER3 augmentation (27/48, 56.2%) from nonaugmentation (21/48). This differs from whether patients continued TKI after progression. The text states 28 continued/20 discontinued, but its paired IHC analysis subsequently gives n=26/n=22 (pp9–10). That discrepancy remains unresolved, not a validated subset relationship.
- Cell models include PC9, H1975, HCC4006, HCC827 and MET-amplified gefitinib-resistant HCC827GR5. Five-line time-course work, AKT knockdown and colony assays have different model sets and readouts. No new animal experiment is presented in this article.
- Statistics include Pearson correlation, linear regression, t tests, paired comparisons and GSEA. Nominal P and FDR q values must remain separate. The adjusted linear model concerns HER3 H-score change, not a hazard ratio, clinical response probability or randomized treatment effect.

## Figure-level reading and source alerts

1. Figure 1 (p5) connects tissue examples, patient H-scores and clinical/molecular annotations with separate RNA/RPPA correlations. Panel G is a HER3 RPPA axis even though its caption uses H-score wording. No dimensionless protein value should be transplanted as a 0–300 IHC score.
2. Figure 2 (p6) contains the actual GSEA table and correlation table. Its weak/FDR-nonsignificant WNT/EMT evidence and SNAI1 null are retained above. Patient cohort and TCGA correlations are not pooled. TGFβ perturbation is PC9-specific.
3. Figure 3 (p7) shows both augmentation and nonaugmentation, missing measurements and pre-existing resistance-related alterations. Example patients include #5 H-score 5→240 with T790M; #12 H-score 105→195 and MET copy 2.3→13.0; #39 H-score 60→280 and HER2 copy 2.4→10.0. These examples illustrate heterogeneity and do not establish population frequencies beyond the main denominator.
4. Table 1 (p8) and Figure 4 (p9) separate actual FDR-supported repression from positive but FDR-nonsignificant trends. The printed nominal P=.000 for WNT should not be copied as a literally zero probability. Figure 4F is a linear association plot, not a survival forest plot.
5. The continued/discontinued patient counts are inconsistent: 28/20 in narrative versus 26/22 for paired H-score results. This is a material denominator alert; the source must clarify it before exact stratified sample accounting is reused without qualification.
6. Colony duration is 14 days in Methods p3, but 10 days in Figure 5 caption p11. Preserve the conflict; do not author a precise reproducible exposure protocol by selecting one. Figure 5 caption also has mismatched panel letters in the F/G discussion and describes normalized output differently from the percent-of-control axes. No exact colony percentage is digitized here.
7. Figure 5 caption defines double asterisks as P<.05 under Dunnett testing; conventional P<.01 meaning must not be assumed. The qPCR panel reports four independent experiments with mean±SD; colony testing is reported as repeated three times, not automatically three independent patient replicates. The authors explicitly disclose a shared actin blot for panels A/C because they use the same samples.
8. The IHC Methods print section thickness as “4 µmol/L” (p2), a dimensionally inappropriate unit visible in the PDF. It is not safely resolved by text extraction; do not silently substitute µm in a transferred method. The procedural typo is held from reuse.
9. Discussion's disease-flare proportion 14/61 (23%) is from cited prior work, not the 48-patient present cohort. Prior in vivo experiments and ADC clinical responses are also cited evidence, not additional experiments performed here. The trial described as ongoing reflects 2021/2022 publication context, not verified 2026 status.

## Interpretation, limitations and source role

The strongest defensible conclusion is that treatment-associated HER3 changes in paired EGFR-mutant NSCLC tissue correlate with altered pathway signatures, while cell perturbations and combination assays provide a preclinical rationale for combining EGFR inhibition with HER3-directed ADC treatment. The evidence does not establish that continued TKI causes clinical HER3 augmentation, that all resistant tumors behave alike, that the combination improves clinical outcomes, or that it is free of toxicity.

Retrospective selection, nonrandom continuation of treatment, mixed resistance mechanisms, incompletely overlapping assay sets, uncertain subgroup accounting and multiple exploratory tests limit clinical inference. Baseline H-score is related to subsequent change and therefore requires careful statistical interpretation. The cell-line panel and limited combination experiments constrain generalization. Public RPPA corroboration is biologically informative but not independent clinical validation of a predictive biomarker.

Source roles: EGFR-targeted-therapy resistance context; HER3/ADC translational rationale; paired tissue multiomics design; vocabulary for membrane-target heterogeneity, pathway-signature associations and model-versus-patient evidence separation. No direct STK11 result was found in the main article; do not add it to a direct STK11 evidence set solely because it concerns NSCLC or PI3K/AKT signaling.

Supplementary Figures S1–S8 and Tables S1–S3 are referenced but not supplied. Physical p15 is an administrative publisher sheet with supplementary links, not the supplement itself. Main-text statements about these materials are distinguished from independently inspected supplementary evidence.
