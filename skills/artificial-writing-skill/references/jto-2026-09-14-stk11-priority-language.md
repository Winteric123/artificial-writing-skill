## PMID 41932614

**Article:** ERBB2-Activating Mutations and Co-Occurring Genomic Alterations Contribute to Disease Heterogeneity in Patients With ERBB2-Mutant Lung Cancer. DOI: 10.1016/j.jtho.2026.103708. *J Thorac Oncol.* 2026;21(8):103708. Uploaded source: `resource_2026_04_09_1775716680969.pdf`.

**Article classification:** genomic landscape; clinicogenomic heterogeneity; oncogenic-driver subclassification; co-mutation analysis; real-world treatment-outcome analysis; immunotherapy and targeted-therapy context. STK11 is a co-alteration and genomic-context variable, not the principal exposure.

**STK11 framework role:** use this paper as a driver-subtype architecture: define molecular classes, establish clinicopathologic differences, compare genomic burden and co-mutations, examine a biologically distinct double-mutant subgroup, and then connect molecular classes to treatment-specific outcomes. It is especially useful when an STK11 manuscript stratifies variants, allelic states, functional classes, or genomic backgrounds before analyzing prognosis or treatment outcomes.

**Evidence map:** journal cover p1; graphical abstract p2; title and administrative material p3; structured Abstract p4; Introduction and Methods start p5; Methods pp5-7; Results pp7-10; Discussion pp10-14; funding, contributions, and figure legends pp15-17; references pp17-18; main Tables 1-2 p19; supplement inventory p20; main Figures 1-4 pp21-24; contributor statement p25.

**Numerical and inference checks:** the GENIE cohort included 483 patients and the Flatiron Health-Foundation Medicine cohort included 286. In GENIE, ERBB2 alterations were TKD in 362 (75%), ECD in 88 (18%), and TMD/JMD in 33 (7%). ECD tumors had higher TMB than TMD/JMD and TKD tumors and were enriched for EGFR, KRAS, STK11, KEAP1, and SMARCA4 co-mutations. STK11 frequencies were 24.1%, 12.5%, and 4.6% in ECD, TMD/JMD, and TKD tumors, respectively (P<0.0001). In the independent clinical cohort, the reported 12-month PFS rates for ECD and TKD tumors were 35% and 18%; the unadjusted comparison favored ECD (HR, 0.72; 95% CI, 0.54-0.94; P=0.0259). The adjusted estimate retained the same direction but crossed the null (HR, 0.73; 95% CI, 0.53-1.01; P=0.06). No significant OS difference was reported among domain groups. These observational comparisons do not establish treatment-effect prediction.

**Source cautions:**

- The Abstract says the ECD-versus-TKD PFS difference persisted in multivariable analysis, whereas the Results report HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.53-1.01, P=0.06. State that the direction persisted but statistical significance did not.
- For immunotherapy alone, the Results text reports HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.28-1.16, while Figure 4B reports 0.28-1.17. Treat this as a rounding discrepancy and reopen the version of record or supplement before using the last decimal.
- The EGFR/ERBB2 double-mutant first-line EGFR-TKI analysis reports HR 5.3, 95% CI 0.6-50.8, P=0.0002 in a very small double-mutant group. The wide interval and P value are unusual together; do not use this estimate as a numerical model without checking the underlying analysis.
- The study uses `chemoimmunotherapy` broadly, but 44% of the clinical cohort received chemotherapy alone and 56% received immunotherapy with or without chemotherapy. Name the actual exposure for the analysis being described.
- GENIE co-mutation comparisons counted single-nucleotide variants; do not generalize those frequencies to every alteration class.
- PD-L1 data and independent mechanistic validation were unavailable, so the observed PFS patterns cannot be attributed to immune sensitivity.

## PMID 41619904

**Article:** Impact of KRAS Mutations and Co-Alterations on Outcomes in Stage III Nonsquamous NSCLC Treated With Chemoradiation and Immunotherapy. DOI: 10.1016/j.jtho.2026.103565. *J Thorac Oncol.* 2026;21(6):103565. Uploaded source: `resource_2026_02_02_1770019745991.pdf`.

**Article classification:** retrospective multicenter clinical analysis; stage III unresectable NSCLC; concurrent chemoradiation; consolidative durvalumab; KRAS genomics; STK11/CDKN2A co-alteration; PFS/OS; competing-risk relapse-pattern analysis; immunotherapy-era outcome stratification.

**STK11 framework role:** this is a direct clinical reference for STK11, although the principal survival stratum combines STK11 and/or CDKN2A. Use it as a treatment-era clinical-outcome architecture: establish the stage III cohort and treatment exposure, report global survival, analyze locoregional versus distant failure, then resolve KRAS heterogeneity through STK11/CDKN2A-defined NGS subgroups. It is particularly useful for Results ordering, competing-risk language, metastatic-pattern reporting, and prognostic-versus-predictive calibration.

**Evidence map:** journal cover pp1-2; title, affiliations, disclosures, and contributor material pp3-5; structured Abstract pp6-7; Introduction pp8-9; Methods pp9-12; Results pp12-16; Discussion pp17-21; Conclusion pp21-22; references and figure legends pp23-27; Table 1 p28; Table 2 p29; Figures 1-4 pp30-33; contributor statement p34.

**Numerical and inference checks:** 208 consecutive patients were included: 117 KRAS wild type and 91 KRAS mutant. Median PFS was 28 versus 16 months (HR, 1.48; P=0.024); the two-year PFS rates were 54% and 43%. The complete-case multivariable model included 177 patients and retained KRAS mutation as an adverse association (HR, 1.484; 95% CI, 1.021-2.156; P=0.038). OS did not differ (HR, 1.09; P=0.7). Two-year distant-metastasis incidence was 34% versus 44% (HR, 1.51; P=0.042), while locoregional recurrence did not differ significantly. Brain-metastasis incidence was higher with KRAS mutation (HR, 2.36; P=0.007). NGS was available for 171 patients, including 72 KRAS-mutant tumors; within the latter, STK11 was altered in 16, CDKN2A in 10, KEAP1 in 8, SMARCA4 in 3, and TP53 in 37. The combined KRAS plus STK11 and/or CDKN2A group contained 24 patients and had median PFS 9.5 months (95% CI, 7.8-19; HR versus KRAS wild type, 2.77; P<0.001). KRAS-mutant tumors without those co-alterations had median PFS 30 months (HR, 1.03; P=0.88 versus KRAS wild type).

**Source cautions:**

- The Results prose labels HR 2.79 (95% CI, 1.35-5.76) for CDKN2A and HR 2.25 (95% CI, 1.20-4.19) for STK11 as univariable estimates. Figure 4 shows CDKN2A HR 2.46 (1.21-5.00) and STK11 HR 2.25 (1.20-4.19) in the univariable panel, and CDKN2A HR 2.79 (1.35-5.76) and STK11 HR 2.31 (1.19-4.49) in the multivariable panel. Attribute estimates by Figure 4 and disclose the prose-panel mismatch when exact values matter.
- A bounded source recheck also found that physical PDF p14, numbered manuscript p12, line282 labels the multivariable outcome OS, whereas Table2 on physical p29 is explicitly a PFS model. Do not import that prose label as an OS finding; disclose the endpoint-label conflict. Figure4 is on physical p33. Physical PDF page and printed manuscript page are separate locators.
- Every patient initiated durvalumab after cCRT; no contemporaneous cCRT-without-durvalumab comparator was included. The study cannot isolate a durvalumab treatment effect or prove STK11-mediated durvalumab resistance.
- STK11 and CDKN2A were combined for the principal three-group Kaplan-Meier analysis. The combined result does not prove equal contribution or biological equivalence of the two genes.
- STK11 associations were evaluated within KRAS-mutant tumors; the KRAS-wild-type supplementary analysis does not substitute for a formal KRAS-by-STK11 interaction test.
- Brain imaging was obtained as clinically indicated rather than at uniform surveillance intervals, likely reducing ascertainment of asymptomatic brain metastases.
- Co-mutation subgroup sizes were small, especially for SMARCA4 and KEAP1, and no multiplicity correction was reported for the genomic subgroup analyses.
- PFS was measured from radiotherapy initiation, with a sensitivity analysis from first durvalumab infusion. Name the time origin in every reusable statement.

**Source cautions:**

- Most deletions were mono-allelic. Do not translate `deletion` as complete gene loss or functional null without the reported allelic state and supporting expression evidence.
- STK11, KEAP1, and SMARCA4 occupy chromosome 19p13.2-13.3 and were frequently co-deleted. Gene-specific associations are difficult to disentangle from locus-level loss; single-gene-only deletion subgroups were small, several with fewer than 10 patients.
- Corresponding mRNA and LKB1 protein analyses came from TCGA subsets, not the DFCI/MSKCC treatment cohorts. They support cross-dataset phenotypic correlation, not patient-level mediation of treatment outcome.
- The LKB1 difference was statistically significant but small on the reported z-score scale (median 0.27 versus 0.33; P=0.008), and STK11 mRNA correlated only modestly with LKB1 protein (r=0.25; P<0.001).
- The chemoimmunotherapy analysis was retrospective and treatment-exposed. It supports outcome association under the observed regimen, not randomized comparative efficacy.
- ICI-alone results differed across institutions. Do not generalize the DFCI KRAS-mutant signal to the combined population or omit the null MSKCC finding.
- The never-immunotherapy control cohort was observational and no formal randomized treatment-by-biomarker interaction was reported. Describe the predictive interpretation as suggestive, not established.
- FAA-adjusted models, KRAS-stratified models, stringent exclusion analyses, and most locus-level results are reported in unsupplied supplementary material and were not independently checked.
- Multiplex immunofluorescence confirmed lower PD-L1 TPS for STK11 and KEAP1 deletion, but no significant association was observed with the quantified CD8-positive, PD-1-positive, CD8/PD-1-double-positive, or FOXP3-positive cell subsets.
- The proposed haploinsufficiency mechanism and treatment intensification strategies are hypotheses. Mono-allelic deletion does not by itself prove haploinsufficiency, and no prospective intensification strategy was tested.

### Combined manuscript architecture

Use only modules supported by the user's data:

1. **Cohort and molecular definition:** show inclusion flow, STK11 assay, sequence-variant and copy-number criteria, allelic state, analysis sets, and treatment era.
2. **Clinicopathologic distribution:** report prevalence and patient/tumor characteristics without treating imbalance as mechanism.
3. **Molecular heterogeneity:** separate STK11 variant class, mono-allelic deletion, bi-allelic inactivation, RNA abundance, LKB1 protein expression, and co-mutational background when measured.
4. **Genomic context:** present TMB, copy-number burden, KRAS/KEAP1/SMARCA4/ATM/CDKN2A context, physical-locus co-deletion, multiplicity control, and assay coverage.
5. **Overall clinical outcome:** report PFS before OS when PFS is primary; provide denominators, time origin, HR, CI, and follow-up.
6. **Failure pattern:** distinguish locoregional recurrence, distant metastasis, and organ-specific events using competing-risk language where appropriate.
7. **STK11-conditioned analyses:** define the exact reference group, keep STK11-only and combined-gene strata distinct, and report interaction tests if treatment prediction is claimed.
8. **Analytical cross-check:** when available, compare genomic loss with RNA, protein, IHC, or another orthogonal measure while preserving separate cohorts and assay denominators.
9. **Robustness:** include adjusted models, alternative time origins, sensitivity analyses, independent cohorts, and paired progression samples without inflating sparse or supplementary-only findings.
10. **Discussion synthesis:** connect genomic context to outcomes, then state which mechanisms remain hypotheses and which validation design is needed.
