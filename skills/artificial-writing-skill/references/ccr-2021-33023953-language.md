### Evidence reconstruction and numerical checks

| Evidence unit | Source-grounded reconstruction | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Patient discovery | NMF of TCGA/OAK and related bulk expression data defined mucinous (MUC), proliferative (PRO), and mesenchymal (MES) states and a 113-gene classifier. | Bulk subtype labels reflect dominant expression states, not pure cell populations. |
| Genotype relationship | KRAS and EGFR alterations occurred across states; no single driver uniquely defined a transcriptional subtype. | Distributed mutations do not mean genotype is biologically irrelevant. |
| STK11/KEAP1 | PRO was enriched for functional signatures of STK11/KEAP1 deficiency; genomic mutation status alone incompletely captured that state. | Expression-defined deficiency is not equivalent to mutation, biallelic loss, or direct pathway assay. |
| Multiregion stability | Twelve samples from six patients generally retained subtype labels despite genomic divergence; only one patient contributed a metastatic-site pair. | Very small sample; not evidence of universal temporal or metastatic stability. |
| Models | The classifier was applied to 175 cell lines, 232 PDXs, and KRAS-driven genetically engineered mouse models. | Cross-model concordance does not guarantee preservation of human immune/stromal context. |
| Drug screen | 526 compounds were screened across 89 lines; subtype-associated response patterns highlighted differential MAPK-pathway sensitivity. | In-vitro associations require dose/exposure and in-vivo validation. |
| PDX validation | Cobimetinib was tested across 37 PDX models with subtype-dependent activity. | PDXs lack a complete immune system and are not randomized clinical evidence. |
| Clinical subset | Only 20 phase Ib cobimetinib–atezolizumab patients were subtyped; MES showed greater activity in the reported descriptive analysis. | Tiny combination cohort, no biomarker-treatment interaction, and no attribution to cobimetinib alone. |

### Source alerts and transfer limits

1. Functional STK11/KEAP1 deficiency is an expression phenotype and must not be relabeled as mutation-positive disease.
2. The 12-sample multiregion analysis includes six patients and only one metastatic comparison; “stable” must remain local to that sample.
3. The compound screen is associative and model based; it does not establish a clinical treatment rule.
4. The phase Ib biomarker subset contains only 20 patients and evaluates a combination, not MEK inhibition alone.
5. MUC behavior differs across preclinical and clinical contexts, so a single universal subtype-sensitivity statement is unsafe.
6. Subtypes occur in both KRAS-mutant and KRAS-wild-type disease and should not be collapsed into genotype labels.
