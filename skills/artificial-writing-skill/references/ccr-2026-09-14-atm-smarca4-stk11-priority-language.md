## PMID 32709715

The Genomic Landscape of SMARCA4 Alterations and Associations with Outcomes in Patients with Lung Cancer. DOI: 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-20-1825. Formal year 2020. Official section: Translational Cancer Mechanisms and Therapy, verified from the publisher-PDF header on 2026-09-14. Sixteen physical pages in the supplied NIH author manuscript.

Evidence map: title and administrative material p1; Abstract p2; Introduction pp2–3; Methods pp3–4; Results pp5–6; Discussion pp6–8; references pp8–10; Translational Relevance p11; main figures pp12–15; Table 1 p16. The study integrates targeted tumor sequencing, SMARCA4 immunohistochemistry, clinicopathologic annotation, survival models, and retrospective immune-checkpoint-inhibitor outcomes. It does not report a primary transcriptomic, single-cell, spatial-omics, or proteomic workflow.

Numerical and inference checks: SMARCA4 alterations were detected in 407 of 4,813 NSCLC tumors (8%): 212 class 1 and 195 class 2. Protein-expression analysis included 86 cases; complete loss occurred in 50, all class 1, and in 81% of class 1 versus 0% of class 2 cases (P<0.001). Frequent co-alterations were TP53 56%, KEAP1 41%, STK11 39%, and KRAS 36%. STK11 and KEAP1 were each enriched in SMARCA4-altered versus wild-type tumors with P<0.001 and q<0.001. STK11 was more frequent in class 1 than class 2 tumors (P<0.001; q=0.08); analogous class comparisons for NKX2-1 and KEAP1 did not pass the study's q<0.10 threshold. In the 1,288-patient advanced-disease model, class 2 SMARCA4, class 1 SMARCA4, STK11, and KEAP1 alterations were independently associated with shorter overall survival. In the KRAS-mutant subgroup of 374 patients, the KEAP1 interval included the null. Among patients with SMARCA4 alterations, receipt of an immune checkpoint inhibitor was associated with longer survival in a propensity-score-adjusted observational analysis (HR, 0.67; 95% CI, 0.48–0.92; P=0.01). A higher unadjusted response rate for class 1 tumors did not translate into class-specific PFS or OS differences. Do not convert treatment exposure into randomized comparative benefit, response-rate association into survival prediction, co-occurrence into cooperation, or mutation class into proven functional loss. A supplementary triple-alteration survival figure was described in the article but was not independently inspected.

## PMID 37097610

Clinicopathologic, Genomic, and Immunophenotypic Landscape of ATM Mutations in Non–Small Cell Lung Cancer. DOI: 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-22-3413. Formal year 2023. Official section: Translational Cancer Mechanisms and Therapy. Twenty-two physical pages.

Evidence map: Abstract p2; Introduction pp2–3; Methods pp3–6; Results pp6–11; Discussion pp11–14; references pp14–16; Translational Relevance p17; main figures pp18–22. The study combines two institutional genomic cohorts, ATM immunohistochemistry, loss-of-heterozygosity analysis, clinicogenomic treatment cohorts, multiplex immunofluorescence, and TCGA bulk-RNA immune deconvolution. It does not report primary single-cell or spatial-transcriptomic sequencing.

Numerical and inference checks: 648 of 5,172 patients (12.5%) carried 714 ATM mutations; 562 mutations in 503 patients (9.7%) were classified as deleterious. Class I consisted of 209 nonsense, insertion/deletion, or splice-site events; class II consisted of 353 missense events predicted deleterious. Tumor-only sequencing yielded 226 potentially germline variants in 192 of 3,800 patients (5%); these are not confirmed germline calls. In the DFCI cohort, ATM-mutant tumors were enriched for KRAS, STK11, and ARID2 and depleted for TP53 and EGFR; directions for KRAS, STK11, and TP53 were assessed in TCGA. Among 182 evaluable tumors, complete ATM-expression loss occurred in 76 (41.8%). In the deleterious subset, loss was more frequent for class I than class II mutations (58.8% vs 21.3%; P<0.0001). Putative loss of heterozygosity was identified in 65 of 461 tumors (14.1%) and was associated with protein loss among IHC-evaluable cases (65.2% vs 38.4%; P=0.02). In the 1,522-patient ICI-monotherapy cohort, ATM status was not associated with ORR, PFS, or OS. In the 951-patient ICI-plus-chemotherapy cohort, ORR, PFS, and OS differences were also nonsignificant. ATM/TP53 co-mutation had higher monotherapy ORR and longer PFS in omnibus subgroup analyses, but OS was not different and no treatment interaction was established. ATM/STK11 and ATM/KEAP1 subgroup analyses were nonsignificant, but small groups and absent interaction tests do not establish no effect. The abstract reports 562 deleterious mutations, whereas the Figure 1 caption refers to 485; retain this source discrepancy rather than silently reconciling it. Computational pathogenicity prediction, tumor-only germline inference, bulk-RNA deconvolution, and protein-expression loss are distinct evidence types.

The main reported multi-omic results emphasize mutations, TMB, mutational signatures, and reverse-phase-protein-array data; availability of RNA-seq, TCR-seq, multiplex immunofluorescence, and flow cytometry should not be misrepresented as a new finding from every modality.

Numerical and inference checks: 2,980 of 26,587 tumors (11.2%) carried nonsynonymous ATM mutations across five cohorts. Molecular landscape analyses included all nonsynonymous variants; clinical analyses used a curated likely functional subset. Meta-analysis showed ATM co-enrichment with KRAS (OR, 2.24; q<0.0001) and depletion with EGFR (OR, 0.52; q<0.001). STK11 was co-enriched overall. Within KRAS-mutant tumors, ATM was mutually exclusive with TP53 and KEAP1 and did not differ by STK11 status; within KRAS-wild-type tumors, ATM co-occurred with STK11, KEAP1, and SWI/SNF alterations. Apparent mutational-signature differences disappeared after KRAS stratification. ATM mRNA correlated only weakly with protein abundance. In the 4,339-patient clinicogenomic cohort, 238 (5.5%) had curated pathogenic ATM alterations. Multivariable analyses associated ATM alteration with longer OS only in the ICI-plus-chemotherapy cohort, not ICI monotherapy or chemotherapy alone; the retrospective treatment cohorts do not establish interaction or randomized benefit. The main text reports for the truncating-variant subgroup HR 0.731 with 95% CI 0.899–1.038, an impossible point-estimate/interval combination; quarantine the trio and do not reuse or recalculate it. In isogenic murine KRAS-mutant cells, ATM loss did not increase direct cytotoxic sensitivity to cisplatin, gemcitabine, or docetaxel and increased the IC50 for pemetrexed, topotecan, and SN-38. Chemotherapy induced relatively greater cGAS/STING-pathway signaling in ATM-knockout cells, but baseline pathway differences were limited and CXCL10 was lower before treatment. Human H2030 and H23 cell comparisons were non-isogenic. Do not convert pathway induction into generalized chemotherapy sensitivity, two-line comparison into ATM causality, or retrospective survival association into a validated therapeutic vulnerability.
