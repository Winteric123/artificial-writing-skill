### Coverage and evidence map

- Abstract p1; Introduction pp1-2; Methods pp2-3; Results pp3-10; Discussion pp10-11 closing synthesis; Translational Relevance p2; administrative/references pp11-12. Main Figures 1-6 visually inspected on pp4-9, including all captions. No main table or separate Conclusion heading. All main scientific text read; separate supplements and linked analysis code were not independently audited.
- TCGA discovery: 1,905 tumor plus 179 normal samples (2,084 total) across breast, colon, glioblastoma, LUAD and pancreatic cancer. Array QC excludes sex-chromosome, SNP, blacklist and high-detection-P probes; functional normalization/Noob; limma differential criteria FDR<0.05 and absolute delta-beta>0.2. SVA finding no latent factors does not rule out every source of confounding or tumor-purity effects.
- MHIC summarizes the fraction of CpGs in an intermediate beta range (0.2-0.8), used mainly for exploration. VarCGI is a sample-level root-mean-variance summary; cfDNA adaptation summarizes individual-read variances. VarRead is the individual-read Bernoulli variance p(1-p), using reads with >=5 CpGs, without the sample-level square-root aggregation. These are related but not interchangeable metrics, and epigenetic instability here is an operational variability measure rather than directly observed longitudinal evolution.
- Boruta on within-region variability first selects 553 CGIs; restricting polycomb-associated CGIs with SD>0.2 produces the locked B-269 regions (~3,500 probes). These regions are locked from TCGA before later cfDNA classifier fitting; external evidence concerns the regions/signature, while new classifiers are trained in the cfDNA datasets. Do not describe one fixed clinical model as externally validated unchanged.
- Independent tissue WGBS: lung n=7 (three normal/four cancer) and liver n=11 (five/six), confirmed visually in Figure 3. B-269 metrics distinguish these small groups; they do not establish population-scale diagnostic accuracy. Breast cfDNA dataset n=60: 30 controls, 15 early nonmetastatic and 15 advanced metastatic cases. LUAD stage-specific counts are relegated to unsupplied Supplementary Table S2 and are not invented here.
- Bismark alignment/deduplication and RLM read-level extraction are used; main Methods filter MAPQ>30. One high-methylation normal specimen was retained after conversion-efficiency assessment (95%), PCA and sensitivity analysis. This supports transparent outlier handling, not proof that all technical artifacts are absent.
- Classifiers: random forests, stated 80:20 train/test split and fivefold cross-validation; Figure 6A includes KNN imputation. Main text does not fully specify patient-level leakage safeguards, feature alignment across variable read counts, imputation fitting scope or the effective ROC denominator. No code reproduction or independent leakage-free certification is claimed.
- Figure 6B: all breast cancer AUC 0.87 (95% CI 0.83-0.90) versus mean-methylation comparator 0.77 (0.73-0.85), but the one-sided DeLong difference is not significant. Figure 6D: early breast AUC 0.74 (0.67-0.82) versus 0.67 (0.60-0.73), P<0.05; advanced breast both AUC 1.00 (0.99-1.00), nonsignificant difference. Perfect discrimination in this small dataset is not proof of perfect clinical performance.
- Figure 6C: all LUAD AUC 0.96 (0.95-0.97) versus TCGA differential-methylation 0.80 (0.78-0.83) and Liang-region mean methylation 0.75 (0.71-0.78), both one-sided DeLong P<0.001. Figure 6F: stage I AUC 0.92 (0.90-0.95), both comparators approximately 0.76. Figure 6G: stages II/III AUC 0.99 (0.99-1.00); comparator significance symbols and narrative are not fully aligned, so exact P thresholds for these two comparisons are not reused without clarification.
- Abstract reports about 81% sensitivity for stage IA LUAD and 68% for early breast cancer at 95% specificity. Results/Discussion attach the approximately 81% value to all LUAD, while Figure 6F groups stage I, not IA alone. The stage-IA-specific 81% claim is therefore not independently established by the main results. Retain the source-specific distinction rather than exporting an unqualified stage-IA detection rate.
- CancerDetector benchmarking produced high estimated tumor fractions even in normal samples; these are model outputs, not confirmed tumor DNA in healthy people. Reported comparator failure in this implementation does not prove universal inferiority of the method. Cross-publication comparisons with different cancers, stages, regions and specificity cutoffs are contextual only.
- Strongest defensible claim: selected methylation-variability features discriminate cases from controls in these retrospective datasets and justify further analytical and prospective clinical validation. No evidence here establishes general-population PPV, clinical utility, tissue-of-origin accuracy, STK11 specificity, or therapy-response prediction.
