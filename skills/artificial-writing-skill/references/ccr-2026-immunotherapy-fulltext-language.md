# CCR 2026 immunotherapy full-text language assets

## Purpose and source discipline

This reference was curated on 2026-09-08 from the complete main text of 17 lung-cancer-related CCR articles published in 2026. Sixteen report original clinical, translational, computational, or preclinical analyses; one is an FDA Approval Summary. The set supports vocabulary, collocations, sentence frames, paragraph architecture, and claim calibration across immunotherapy, radiation, multi-omics, liquid biopsy, bioinformatics, machine learning, cellular therapy, pharmacokinetics, safety, and regulatory communication.

Use the entries as functional language patterns, not as source sentences to reproduce. Rebuild every statement from the user's own design, population or model, comparator, endpoint, estimate, uncertainty, and analysis status. Never transfer an article's number, assay, causal link, or treatment conclusion into another study.

The FDA Approval Summary is retained as a separate regulatory genre. It is not original research and must not be pooled with research-article evidence. No commentary, editorial, author reply, rebuttal, or response-only correspondence contributed language to this asset.

## Modality coverage boundary

Together with the five-paper translational-mechanisms reference, the current 22-paper deep-read set directly supports language for:

- bulk, single-cell, and spatial transcriptomics;
- B-cell and T-cell receptor repertoire profiling and clone tracking;
- targeted DNA sequencing, whole-exome sequencing, copy-number analysis, and tumor mutational burden;
- error-corrected plasma sequencing, ctDNA tumor fraction, matched-WBC filtering, and methylation-based MRD;
- shotgun fecal metagenomics and untargeted serum metabolomics;
- radiomics, digital pathology, PET uptake metrics, and spatial immune-pattern assessment;
- conventional and multiplex immunofluorescence, flow cytometry, cytokine assays, and receptor-occupancy analyses;
- pharmacokinetics, immunogenicity, early-phase dose selection, safety, and regulatory benefit-risk writing;
- cell culture, coculture, finite-element simulation, three-dimensional tumor mimics, xenografts, syngeneic models, and tumor rechallenge.

This set still contains no primary discovery or quantitative mass-spectrometry **proteomics** workflow. Do not relabel flow cytometry, immunofluorescence, cytokine panels, immunoblotting, receptor occupancy, or metabolomics as proteomics. Use source-grounded wording for proteomic studies until a dedicated full-text proteomics set is registered.

## Evidence-first labels

Before drafting, label each evidence unit on six axes:

1. **Design:** randomized trial, single-arm trial, post hoc trial analysis, prospective observational cohort, retrospective cohort, regulatory summary, or preclinical study.
2. **System:** patient, biospecimen, tissue region, single cell, plasma, microbiome, cell line, three-dimensional model, or mouse.
3. **Measurement:** directly measured, centrally reviewed, clinician documented, pathologist annotated, sequencing derived, computationally estimated, modeled, or inferred.
4. **Time structure:** baseline, on-treatment, landmark, longitudinal, perioperative, post-cCRT, post-ICI, or rechallenge.
5. **Analysis status:** primary, secondary, exploratory, post hoc, subgroup, sensitivity, internally validated, or independently externally validated.
6. **Claim tier:** descriptive, associative, prognostic, mechanistic, treatment-predictive, comparative efficacy, clinical utility, or regulatory.

Use the lowest defensible tier. Multiple orthogonal assays can strengthen biological coherence without establishing causality. A treated-only cohort can identify prognostic enrichment but generally cannot establish treatment prediction. A randomized source trial does not make every post hoc biomarker model confirmatory.

## Statistical and validation language

- `multivariable-adjusted hazard ratio` - 多变量校正风险比
- `prespecified covariate` / `data-driven covariate selection` - 预设协变量 / 数据驱动协变量选择
- `proportional-hazards assumption` - 比例风险假设
- `Schoenfeld-residual assessment` - Schoenfeld 残差检验
- `risk-set adjustment for left truncation` - 针对左截断的风险集校正
- `inverse probability of treatment weighting` - 治疗逆概率加权
- `standardized mean difference` - 标准化均数差
- `confounding by indication` - 适应证混杂
- `likelihood-ratio improvement` - 似然比改善
- `receiver operating characteristic curve` - 受试者工作特征曲线
- `area under the curve` - 曲线下面积
- `clinical sensitivity` / `clinical specificity` - 临床敏感度 / 特异度
- `positive predictive value` / `negative predictive value` - 阳性 / 阴性预测值
- `data-derived cutoff` / `externally prespecified cutoff` - 数据推导 / 外部预设阈值
- `nominal P value without multiplicity control` - 未经多重性校正的名义 P 值
- `wide confidence interval` / `imprecise estimate` - 宽置信区间 / 不精确估计
- `formal sample-size calculation was not performed` - 未进行正式样本量估计
- `descriptive analysis without formal between-group testing` - 未作正式组间检验的描述性分析
- `test-dependent statistical significance` - 统计显著性依赖所选检验
- `interaction test` / `treatment-by-biomarker interaction` - 交互作用检验 / 治疗与生物标志物交互
- `internal validation` / `independent external validation` - 内部验证 / 独立外部验证
- `model discrimination` / `calibration` / `clinical utility` - 模型区分度 / 校准度 / 临床效用

Report estimates and confidence intervals before P values. A significant result in one subgroup and a nonsignificant result in another does not itself demonstrate interaction. Report ROC-derived cutoffs as exploratory unless independently validated. Never use `validated` without naming what was validated, against which reference, and in which independent population.

## Section-specific sentence frames

All sentences below are synthetic templates.

### Introduction

- `[Therapy] has changed the management of [disease setting], yet outcomes remain heterogeneous and are incompletely captured by [current biomarker].`
- `The biological determinants of [response, resistance, or toxicity] remain poorly defined, limiting [patient selection, early monitoring, or treatment adaptation].`
- `[Cell population or tissue structure] has emerged as a potential coordinator of antitumor immunity, but its spatial and longitudinal behavior during [therapy] is unclear.`
- `Whether a marker measured in [blood, tissue, or imaging] reflects [intratumoral process] or merely correlates with disease burden remains unresolved.`
- `To address this gap, we integrated [clinical outcome data] with [omics or experimental modalities] across [time points or cohorts].`

### Methods: design and analysis set

- `This [prospective or retrospective] [single-center or multicenter] study included patients with [eligibility criteria] who received [treatment] between [dates].`
- `The biomarker-evaluable population comprised [n] patients with paired [specimen type] collected at [time points].`
- `The analysis was post hoc and exploratory; no formal sample-size calculation was performed for the biomarker endpoints.`
- `Patients were assigned to exposure strata according to [measurement], without randomization of the exposure.`
- `Response was assessed by [investigator or central review] according to [criteria], whereas real-world progression was abstracted from [source].`

### Methods: multi-omics and computation

- `We integrated [assay 1], [assay 2], and [assay 3] to characterize [biological process] at tissue and systemic levels.`
- `Cell states were annotated after quality control, doublet removal, normalization, dimensionality reduction, and cluster-specific marker assessment.`
- `Spatial regions were annotated by a pathologist using histomorphology together with prespecified marker expression.`
- `Cell-type proportions were inferred from spatial spots using [method] and should not be interpreted as direct single-cell counts.`
- `Plasma variants were assigned a likely cellular origin using matched-WBC sequencing and fragment-level features.`
- `Model development and hyperparameter tuning were nested within the resampling procedure to limit optimistic performance estimates.`
- `Out-of-fold predictions were aggregated across [number] repeats of [k]-fold cross-validation.`

### Results: cohort and endpoint opening

- `Among [N] enrolled patients, [n] received [treatment], and [n] had evaluable paired specimens.`
- `At a median follow-up of [time], median [endpoint] was [estimate] (95% CI, [interval]).`
- `The primary endpoint was met, with [effect estimate and interval]; [secondary endpoint] remained immature at the data cutoff.`
- `No formal comparison was planned; efficacy outcomes are therefore summarized descriptively.`
- `The median was not reached, and the corresponding confidence interval remained wide because few events had occurred.`

### Results: comparative and subgroup statements

- `[Outcome] was longer in [group A] than in [group B] (median, [x] vs [y]; HR, [estimate]; 95% CI, [interval]).`
- `The association remained after adjustment for [prespecified covariates], although residual confounding cannot be excluded.`
- `The direction of effect was consistent across subgroups, but no treatment-by-subgroup interaction was demonstrated.`
- `A treatment-by-biomarker interaction was observed for [endpoint], supporting treatment-effect heterogeneity within this observational dataset.`
- `The subgroup estimate was exploratory and imprecise, as reflected by the wide confidence interval.`
- `Although the Wilcoxon test was significant, the corresponding log-rank comparison did not reach the prespecified threshold.`

### Results: longitudinal and multi-omic statements

- `[Cell population] increased after [first treatment phase] and was followed by an increase in [second population] after [second phase].`
- `Changes in [marker A] correlated with subsequent changes in [marker B] (rho = [value], P = [value]).`
- `Baseline [repertoire feature] differed by pathologic response, whereas the difference was no longer evident after treatment.`
- `Treatment increased [quantitative tissue feature], but this feature did not differ between response groups.`
- `Pathway analysis indicated enrichment of [program] in [group]; this computational result was supported by [orthogonal assay].`
- `The inferred interaction was consistent with [mechanistic hypothesis] but was not directly tested by perturbation.`
- `Responders were enriched for [taxon or metabolite], whereas longitudinal within-group change was significant only for [feature].`

### Results: diagnostic, liquid-biopsy, and machine-learning statements

- `At the prespecified landmark, molecular response was associated with longer [endpoint] after adjustment for [covariates].`
- `Matched-WBC filtering increased sensitivity by removing variants attributable to clonal hematopoiesis.`
- `The model achieved an ROC AUC of [value] in the held-out set, indicating [weak, modest, or strong] discrimination.`
- `Nested cross-validation provided an internally validated performance estimate; independent external validation was not performed.`
- `The model ranked patients by estimated treatment benefit and identified a top-ranked subgroup with a lower hazard under [treatment].`
- `Because the subgroup and cutoff were model derived, the result should be considered hypothesis generating.`

### Results: preclinical experiments

- `In [model], [intervention] reduced tumor-cell viability while preserving [cell population] under the tested parameters.`
- `Supernatant from treated tumor cells increased CAR T-cell migration in a Boyden-chamber assay.`
- `Pretreatment with [intervention] was associated with greater intratumoral CAR T-cell infiltration and earlier tumor clearance in mice.`
- `All active CAR T-cell groups reached the designated survival endpoint; therefore, no survival advantage specific to the combination was established.`
- `The finding was reproduced across simulation, three-dimensional culture, and in vivo assays, supporting preclinical feasibility.`

### Results: pharmacokinetics and safety

- `Exposure increased approximately proportionally over the tested dose range.`
- `The geometric mean trough concentration was higher after thigh than abdominal administration.`
- `No dose-limiting toxicities were observed, and the maximum tolerated dose was not reached.`
- `The recommended phase II dose was selected by integrating safety, pharmacokinetics, peripheral receptor occupancy, and modeled intratumoral exposure.`
- `Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events occurred in [n/N] patients; no grade 4 or 5 treatment-related events were reported.`
- `Treatment-emergent antidrug antibodies were detected in [proportion], with [observed or no observed] effect on exposure.`

### Results: regulatory summary

- `On [date], the FDA granted accelerated approval to [drug] for [exact indication].`
- `Approval was based on [endpoint] assessed by [review method] in [analysis population].`
- `The observed response rate and durability were considered clinically meaningful in a setting with limited treatment options.`
- `The benefit-risk assessment also incorporated [major toxicities], resulting in [risk-management action].`
- `Continued approval is contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in a confirmatory trial.`

### Discussion

- `The principal finding is that [observation], within the constraints of a [design] study.`
- `These data support an association between [marker] and [outcome], but they do not establish that the marker modifies treatment benefit.`
- `The concordance across [modalities] strengthens biological plausibility without proving causality.`
- `The absence of a randomized comparator precludes attribution of the observed outcome to [intervention or exposure].`
- `The small high-resolution subset limits subgroup inference and requires validation in a larger independent cohort.`
- `Residual confounding, treatment heterogeneity, and data-derived thresholds limit immediate clinical application.`

### Conclusion

- `The assay may support future risk stratification, but prospective clinical-utility testing is required before treatment adaptation.`
- `The findings provide a rationale for prospective evaluation rather than a basis for current treatment selection.`
- `In [population or model], [bounded primary finding] warrants [specific validation or next study] without establishing [unsupported claim].`

## Paragraph architecture

### Clinical-results paragraph

`analysis population and follow-up -> primary endpoint -> effect estimate with uncertainty -> key secondary endpoint -> safety or maturity qualifier -> inference boundary`

### Longitudinal immune-results paragraph

`sampling time points -> measured population change -> temporal relation between populations -> outcome association -> tissue-surrogate limitation`

### Multi-omics-results paragraph

`cohort and modality-specific denominators -> result within each modality -> convergent or divergent signal -> direct versus inferred measurement -> multiplicity and sample-size caveat`

### Machine-learning-results paragraph

`target estimand -> development data -> validation scheme -> discrimination or ranking metric -> subgroup estimate -> external-validation and clinical-utility boundary`

### Preclinical-mechanism paragraph

`biophysical or biological rationale -> in vitro perturbation -> orthogonal assay -> in vivo effect -> unresolved alternative mechanism -> clinical-translation boundary`

### Regulatory-summary paragraph

`exact approval and indication -> pivotal analysis population -> response and durability -> major safety risks -> benefit-risk rationale -> confirmatory requirement`

## Mandatory misuse checks learned from this set

1. Do not write `improved PFS` for the single-arm SBRT-atezolizumab-tiragolumab study; write the observed median PFS or preliminary activity.
2. Do not call a post hoc early-detriment model practice informing when its held-out discrimination is modest and prospective validation is absent.
3. Do not call very high PD-L1, TMB, MTAP loss, or an immune state treatment predictive merely because it is associated with outcome among ICI-treated patients.
4. Do not infer regimen superiority from retrospective treatment selection, even after multivariable adjustment.
5. Do not equate nested cross-validation with independent external validation or patient-level treatment utility.
6. Do not call an inferred whole-tumor immune state directly measured when it was predicted from one bulk-RNA sample.
7. Do not use a response variable to impute a baseline biomarker without flagging post-baseline information leakage.
8. Do not convert a data-derived dose stratum into a causal therapeutic window; the 1-3 Gy ILDR signal remains observational.
9. Do not convert taxonomic and metabolite correlations into proof of microbial production, trafficking, or immune mediation.
10. Do not call a derivation-cohort AUC a validated clinical biomarker, and do not call ctDNA or MRD a surrogate without formal evidence.
11. Do not recommend treatment escalation or de-escalation from exploratory MRD, ctDNA, immune-cell, or tumor-fraction analyses.
12. Do not claim early detection of ICI myocarditis when myocarditis was not adjudicated against a clinical reference standard.
13. Do not interpret `MTD not reached` or absence of a specific event in a small cohort as proof of safety or equivalence.
14. Do not present complete tumor clearance in mice as a patient response or claim a combination-specific survival effect when active groups share the same survival endpoint.
15. Do not present the FDA Approval Summary as primary trial evidence or imply that accelerated approval confirms OS benefit.
16. Do not merge the FDA regulatory genre with the research-article phrase pool when drafting an original manuscript.
17. Do not label metabolomics, flow cytometry, mIF, cytokine assays, or receptor occupancy as proteomics.
