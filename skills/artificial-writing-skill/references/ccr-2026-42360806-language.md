Evidence reconstruction:
- Question: how disease subtype, molecular actionability and serial co-alterations relate to access to and outcomes with targeted therapy in BTC.
- Design: retrospective clinical-outcome analysis of a prospectively maintained sequencing database; 1,254 patients/1,285 samples, comprising 767 iCCA, 210 eCCA and 277 gallbladder cancers. Exclusion of low-purity/no-somatic-alteration samples limits representativeness. The landscape generally uses one prioritized sample per patient, while resistance analyses use serial samples.
- Methods pp3–6: MSK-IMPACT panel versions cover 341–505 genes; gene-specific denominators matter. FACETS estimates purity-adjusted copy number and cancer-cell fraction. OncoKB versions 1.8/4.7 correspond to 2017/July2023 knowledge; actionability tiers are date-dependent, not interchangeable with approved treatment.
- Figure2 and pp6–8: 514 potentially actionable cases become 413 clinically eligible after follow-up/outcome exclusions; 176 received matched therapy versus 237 unmatched. Second-line OS compares 100 versus 113; PFS compares 99 versus 25 fluoropyrimidine-treated patients, not the same comparison population.
- Matched second-line PFS 6.6 versus 3.3 months, P=.005 in prose/.0052 in figure; OS 17 versus 11 months, HR .8, P=.16. Preserve the null OS finding, nonrandomized comparison, treatment-line restriction and residual selection/guarantee-time concerns.
- Molecular eligibility pp6–7/Table1: broad OncoKB actionability and level1/2 proportions use different tier definitions. MTAP deletion 12.8% uses the 336 assessable samples, not all 1,254 patients.
- Co-alteration analyses Figure3/Table2 are exploratory. Do not claim validated predictive resistance from TP53/RAS-RAF/SMAD4 associations. Table2 multivariable HRs shown for gene-defined subsets have CIs crossing1 and uncertain contrast labels; they must not be assigned as confirmed gene-specific independent effects.
- Longitudinal analysis pp8–10/Figure4 distinguishes retention of FGFR2/IDH/NTRK drivers from loss of an ERBB2 mutation versus loss of amplification. Two clinical trajectories illustrate bypass alterations and treatment responses; these are cases, not functional rescue experiments or proof of drug sensitivity.
