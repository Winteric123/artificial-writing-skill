## Design and causal scope
VGT-309 combines a phenoxymethyl ketone electrophile, ICG fluorophore and quencher. Covalent active-cathepsin binding releases quenching. Western blots and fluorescent SDS-PAGE, inhibitor competition, lysosomal colocalization, mouse xenograft imaging, flow cytometry, spontaneous canine tumors, a placebo-controlled healthy-volunteer study and two illustrative surgical patients form separate translational stages. This is not a therapeutic efficacy trial or an omics discovery study. A reported NGS confirmation of a lung metastasis in one case is not a general genomic cohort.

## Main figures and denominators
Figure 1 p19: cathepsins B/L/S/X in four human NSCLC lines plus KB control, lysosomal fluorescence and dose-dependent inhibitor-sensitive labeling. The text/methods identify A549 for SDS-PAGE, whereas Figure 1C caption identifies H1264. Text uses two-hour concentration incubation while panels E and D captions describe one hour; retain source-level timing/cell-line discrepancies. Broad inhibitor blockade supports cathepsin-activity dependence, not absolute cancer-cell specificity.

Figure 2 p20: dose/time optimization in KB flank xenografts, five mice per dose, with three collected at 24 hours and two at 48 hours. Maximum reported SBR 3.12 for 2 mg/kg versus 2.26 for 4 mg/kg; greater dose does not necessarily yield better contrast. Normal kidneys/liver/spleen fluoresce. Serial sample depletion and very small biological groups constrain precision; ROI triplicates are technical measurements, not additional animals. Figure 3 p21: four NSCLC xenograft types, three per type, and KB inhibitor/vehicle controls show signal suppression with pan-cathepsin blockade, tumor/background contrast and concordant tissue histology. Cell-line-derived xenografts are not PDXs; occasional text 'allograft' wording does not change their human-in-mouse identity.

Figure 4 p22: four mice per treatment. Text reports ICG-positive cells 12.9% versus 0.4%, P=.010, and GFP-tumor versus CD45-leukocyte fractions 77.6% versus 19.6%, P=.006. The actual plotted P values are .0034 and .0011, respectively: report direction robustly but verify discrepancy before quoting exact statistics. More than 80% of dye-labeled CD45 cells are CD11b/F4/80 macrophages; this is conditional on the leukocyte gate, not 80% of all labeled cells. Tumor cells predominate but macrophages also label.

Figure 5 p23 and Table 1 p26: four dogs, two receiving 0.1 and two 0.3 mg/kg, three imaged after three hours and one after 18 hours. In-situ SBR 2.15–3.71 and ex-vivo 2.03–2.64; no statistically established dose difference. Mean tumor/background MFI 144.97/55.34 in situ, 141.75/53.76 ex vivo. Lack of adverse reactions in four animals is feasibility evidence, not broad safety assurance. Histologic spatial concordance does not yield validated sensitivity/specificity without adequate malignant and benign denominators.

Table 2 pp27–28: 30 healthy volunteers, 23 active and seven placebo across four ascending dose cohorts. Fifteen TEAEs in nine subjects, 13 active and two placebo events; four related events in two active recipients. No serious AE reported, but a moderate infusion reaction required interruption. Text '15 mild, 2 moderate' conflicts with 15 total; tabulated counts appear 13 mild plus two moderate. Do not repeat the impossible severity sum. No elevated tryptase/complement narrows mechanism but cannot prove all allergic etiologies absent. PK characterization was limited at lower doses; terminal half-lives 7.8 and 9.5 hours apply to the higher cohorts, not every recipient.

Figure 6 pp24–25: two illustrative phase-II patients, not full trial results. Patient 1006's 45-mm tumor WAS visible and palpable, SBR7.18 at .16mg/kg; patient1011's 17-mm tumor was not located by white light/palpation, SBR2.83 at .32mg/kg. Abstract erroneously implies both tumors were occult/nonpalpable. One successful fluorescence-assisted wedge resection is not a randomized reduction in thoracotomy, recurrence or mortality. Margins were not changed solely on fluorescence; standard frozen-section principles applied. Three-month recurrence-free imaging of the latter case is short individual follow-up.

## Writing use and limits
Strong source for activity-dependent probes, inhibitor competition, biodistribution, conditional flow gates, technical versus biological replication, preclinical-to-clinical translation and spatial histologic concordance. Preserve differences among healthy safety participants, canine tumors and selected cancer cases. 'Safe at all doses' should be calibrated to observed tolerability and infusion reaction. Other-organ generalization and improved local control remain hypotheses, especially where normal cathepsin expression increases background. No therapeutic response, survival benefit or STK11-specific conclusion is shown; do not add to STK11 highlight automatically.
