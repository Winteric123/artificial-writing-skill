### Evidence and transfer boundaries

- Screened 5921 NSCLC and 350 SCLC; MSI-assessable denominators 5171 and 315. MSI-H identified in 21/5171 (0.41%) and 6/315 (1.9%). Do not divide by the screened total or describe all as assayed by every confirmation test.
- Tissue 5345 cases: 5286 MSS, 33 indeterminate, 26 MSI-H; plasma 141: one MSI-H. Of 33 indeterminate, 15 tested MSS/MMR-proficient; 18 lacked residual material and remain unresolved, even though genomic findings suggest MSS. All 13 tested MSI-H specimens were MMR-deficient by IHC; all 16 tested were MSI-H by PCR. These are selected confirmation subsets, not validation sensitivity estimates for all tumors.
- MSIsensor and MiMSI were used for tissue, ADMIE for plasma. TMB/signatures are not validated/analyzable from the plasma panel here. Tissue cutoffs are assay-specific: MSIsensor <3, 3–<10, >=10; TCGA cutoffs and adjudication differ. Calling methods are not interchangeable without validation.
- NSCLC comparator: 164 smoking-exposed MSS cases, whereas smoking-history comparison within LUAD used 840 MSS cases; ICI comparator has 160 treated cases. Main TMB median 37.4 versus 8.5 muts/Mb, P<.0001; PD-L1 and TIL differences nonsignificant. Dominant MMR signature 9/21 versus 0/63, not 0/164.
- NSCLC pathogenic MMR events in 14/21; MLH1 altered in 12, biallelic in 11 (nine mutation+LOH, two homozygous deletions). Four tested NSCLC cases lacked MLH1 promoter hypermethylation. SCLC: pathogenic MMR changes in three; two other cases had promoter hypermethylation. Do not equate promoter methylation, protein loss, heterozygous mutation and biallelic genetic loss.
- TCGA: six MSI-H among 1043 after signature-assisted adjudication of two initially indeterminate cases. Two MSI-H cases had MLH1 loss-of-function+LOH; two MSS cases also did. Thus the MSK-specific absence of biallelic MLH1 in non-MSI-H cases is not universal specificity. MLH1 mRNA findings are secondary analysis, not protein quantification.
- Six patients underwent germline analysis; one had germline MSH2 exon1–2 deletion plus somatic second hit. This is not a population-wide estimate of Lynch syndrome, and clinical criteria do not substitute for testing.
- ICI durable clinical benefit: 3/8 NSCLC (38%) versus 54/160 MSS (34%), P=1; this is DCB, not ORR or equivalence. DCB includes CR/PR or SD >6 months. SCLC 2/2 benefited but received different combinations. STK11/KEAP1/JAK1 changes occurred in nonresponders, absent in three responders: exploratory descriptive finding, not a validated resistance classifier or proof each gene causes failure.
- JAK1 enrichment q=.01 after Benjamini-Hochberg; STK11 NSCLC q=.7 and SCLC q=.06 are not significant. Smoking association does not establish MLH1 mutations were caused by tobacco: no MLH1 G:C>T:A transversions identified. Retrospective, selected tested material, small rare subgroup, differing comparator sets and limited ICI sample restrict causal/predictive conclusions.
- Main text says MSI-ind comparison uses MSI-H median TMB 32.5 (p9), while overall NSCLC comparison uses 37.4 (pp6,8). Preserve the comparison-specific value/denominator instead of harmonizing without data. Table 2 lists promoter methylation alongside mutation categories; label it epigenetic rather than a sequence variant.
