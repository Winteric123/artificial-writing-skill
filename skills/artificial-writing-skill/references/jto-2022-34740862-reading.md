## Research question and design

The study asks whether the association between STK11/KEAP1 loss-of-function mutations and outcomes during PD-(L)1 blockade differs by KRAS mutation status, whether STK11 and KEAP1 have separable associations despite their co-occurrence, and whether transcriptomic profiles provide biological context. It also asks whether adverse outcomes extend to chemotherapy; that secondary cohort is relevant to the prognosis-versus-prediction distinction.

This is retrospective, multicenter, clinicogenomic cohort research, not a randomized treatment comparison. The discovery cohort comprised 620 patients from DFCI/MGH and the validation cohort 641 patients from MSKCC/MDACC, totaling 1,261 patients with advanced lung adenocarcinoma treated with PD-(L)1 inhibitors. Abstract dates for clinical/genomic collection are September 2013–September 2020. In the combined cohort, median age was 61 years (range 22–92), 708 were women (56.1%), and 1,065 were smokers (84.4%). The separate DFCI first-line platinum-doublet cohort comprised 248 patients. TCGA RNA-seq reanalysis comprised 513 LUADs; it is a separate molecular dataset, not paired pretreatment RNA-seq from the 1,261 ICI-treated patients.

STK11/KEAP1 mutant groups included nonsense, frameshift, insertion/deletion, splice-site, and pathogenic missense loss-of-function alterations. This is a sequence-alteration study, not direct measurement of LKB1 protein or proof of biallelic inactivation. TMB was measured with OncoPanel/MSK-IMPACT and harmonized across platforms. PD-L1 tumor proportion score was missing in 35.9% of samples; the Discussion reports inverse-probability weighting in Cox regression. Full covariate sets, endpoint ascertainment, harmonization/statistical details, model-specific denominators, median follow-up, and exact adjusted CIs require the unavailable supplement and should not be invented.

Reported endpoints are ORR, PFS, and OS; the main text does not establish one prospectively prespecified primary endpoint. KRAS stratification, co-mutation categories, PD-L1 subgroups, and sensitivity analyses are exploratory retrospective analyses. No adverse-event/safety results are presented in these main PDFs. The article reports no randomized biomarker-by-treatment interaction.

## Negative findings, controls, and treatment-prediction boundary

- Within KRAS-wild-type disease, the principal ORR/PFS/OS comparisons did not reach conventional significance; this is not proof that the genes have no effect. The reported sensitivity analyses excluding EGFR/ALK-positive cases or never smokers also did not show an adverse association; these are main-text reports only because Supplementary Figures 14–15 were not supplied.
- The TCGA analysis did not find a significant DFS or OS effect. It is a different population from advanced ICI-treated cases and cannot provide a randomized treatment control (VOR p6 / AM p5; supplementary plots unreviewed).
- In the n=248 first-line platinum-doublet cohort, the article reports shorter PFS associated with STK11/KEAP1 alterations within KRAS-mutant but not KRAS-wild-type disease (VOR pp6–7 / AM p5). No numerical chemotherapy HR, CI, or denominator by mutation subgroup is available in the supplied main text. This cross-treatment adverse association argues against an exclusively ICI-specific explanation, without resolving a predictive-versus-prognostic decomposition.
- Separate-group significance is not a formal effect-modification test. The supplied main text contains an STK11-by-KEAP1 interaction report but does not supply a definitive KRAS-by-gene interaction estimate or a biomarker-by-randomized-treatment interaction. Do not interchange these three tests.
- The Discussion's proposed treatment sequencing and cited KRAS-G12C inhibitor combinations are historical hypotheses/citations, not interventions tested here or current recommendations.

## STK11 use, limitations, and strongest defensible claim

STK11 is a direct clinical exposure in this study, alongside KEAP1; KRAS is a stratification context. The paper supports studying STK11 and KEAP1 separately, then evaluating their overlap and KRAS context. It does not support lumping their union into a single causal mechanism.

The most defensible synthesis is that **in two retrospective ICI-treated LUAD cohorts, adverse STK11/KEAP1 associations were observed primarily within KRAS-mutant disease; double-mutant cases had particularly poor observed outcomes, and independent TCGA analyses showed associated immune-transcriptomic differences. Chemotherapy associations and absence of a randomized treatment interaction prevent a claim of validated ICI-specific predictive resistance.**

Limits include retrospective selection/confounding, heterogeneous assays and clinical settings, PD-L1 missingness, small single-mutant subgroups, numerous exploratory subgroup/endpoint comparisons, incomplete main-text statistical reporting, no randomized ICI-versus-chemotherapy validation, nonpaired TCGA/clinical datasets, computational cell inference, and source inconsistencies above. Inverse-probability weighting addresses modeled missingness; it cannot remove unmeasured confounding. The review does not certify supplementary analyses, causal mechanisms, clinical treatment selection, or current trial status.
