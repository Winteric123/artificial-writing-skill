# Communication scenarios

Apply these architectures after resolving the journal, disease or tumor type, audience, and evidence-domain selections. Preserve the evidence ledger and four mandatory audits in every scenario.

## Scientific or technical report

Use only the components needed by the request:

1. purpose or decision question;
2. data and analysis scope;
3. methods sufficient to interpret the findings;
4. findings separated from interpretation;
5. limitations and unresolved issues;
6. evidence-bounded conclusions or next steps.

Label observed results, derived calculations, interpretation, and recommendations distinctly. Preserve source or dataset provenance when combining evidence.

## Standalone results statement

When available, include:

- population, model, or analysis set;
- comparison or reference condition;
- endpoint or measured feature;
- direction and magnitude;
- uncertainty, statistical evidence, and adjustment status;
- time point or follow-up;
- exploratory, post hoc, validation, or multiplicity status;
- one inference boundary when overinterpretation is plausible.

Lead with the result, not background. Do not equate statistical significance with biological or clinical importance, and do not describe a null result as equivalence without an appropriate design.

## Slide, oral presentation, poster, or briefing

- Write each headline as one evidence-supported takeaway rather than a topic label when the evidence allows.
- Pair the takeaway with the minimum decisive evidence: denominator, comparator, effect size, uncertainty, and time point as applicable.
- Keep material caveats visible rather than hiding them only in speaker notes.
- Adjust terminology depth to the audience without changing the scientific claim.
- Preserve a negative primary endpoint and distinguish it from favorable secondary or exploratory findings.
- For oral delivery, provide a logical bridge from question to evidence to interpretation to next step.

## Literature synthesis

- Preserve article-level attribution and distinguish primary evidence from reviews or commentaries.
- Group findings by question, design, population, intervention, endpoint, or mechanism rather than by rhetorical convenience.
- Separate agreement, heterogeneity, contradiction, and evidence gaps.
- Do not merge estimates or infer consensus without an appropriate synthesis method.

## Response or decision brief

- State the question or concern directly.
- Give the answer or action first when the audience requires it.
- Link each response to evidence, change, or limitation.
- Distinguish completed actions, proposed actions, and unsupported requests.
- Retain a traceable mapping to reviewer comments, report sections, figures, tables, or source records.

## Scenario conflicts

When journal convention conflicts with a report template or presentation constraint, follow the explicit user requirement first, then mandatory reporting guidance, then the selected journal profile, then generic scenario preferences. Never resolve a formatting conflict by altering data or claim strength.
