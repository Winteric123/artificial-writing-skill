### Abstract background
- `Single biomarkers may incompletely capture the tumor–immune features associated with treatment outcomes.`
### Abstract methods
- `Whole-genome and bulk-transcriptome profiles were integrated with retrospective treatment and survival data.`
### Abstract results
- `Genomic and immune-expression markers showed endpoint-specific associations, while a combined score provided exploratory risk stratification.`
### Abstract conclusion
- `The integrated approach warrants prospective evaluation with prespecified thresholds and clinically relevant comparators.`

### Results frames
- `The marker was associated with [endpoint] in univariable analysis but did not add information after inclusion of the other covariates.`
- `The higher median in the benefit group did not reach statistical significance before adjustment.`
- `Alterations identified in a progressing lesion were absent from an earlier biopsy at another site, leaving temporal acquisition and spatial heterogeneity unresolved.`
### Discussion frames
- `Outcome associations within treated patients cannot by themselves separate general prognosis from treatment-specific prediction.`
- `A score constructed from expression weights should not be interpreted as a direct ratio of immune-cell counts.`
- `Failure to reproduce the association in an external cohort limits claims of generalizability.`
### Conclusion frames
- `Prospective validation should test whether combining molecular features improves decisions beyond existing biomarkers.`
### Translational relevance frames
- `Integrated profiling may support biomarker development without establishing that an existing clinical test should be abandoned.`
### Results paragraph
- `Separate naive and previously treated biopsies, define endpoint-specific analysis populations, compare individual and combined markers, retain failed replication, and end with a clearly labeled resistance case.`
### Discussion paragraph
- `Explain complementary biomarker biology, distinguish association from prediction, address threshold tuning and biopsy context, and frame lesion-level resistance as a hypothesis rather than a proven acquired mechanism.`
