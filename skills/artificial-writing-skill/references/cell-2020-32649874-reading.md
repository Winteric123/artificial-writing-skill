## Identity and evidence type

- **Article:** *Proteogenomic Characterization Reveals Therapeutic Vulnerabilities in Lung Adenocarcinoma*.
- **Journal/year/DOI:** *Cell*, 2020; 10.1016/j.cell.2020.06.013.
- **Local source:** 84-page NIH/HHS author manuscript; SHA-256 `e9ae7ed13ea2fa7c1a30f90a40fa1a0b7a2d31eca83f1c34bfdeb17388b8caa4`.
- **Evidence type:** original prospective human-tumor proteogenomic resource study with paired normal-adjacent tissue (NAT), plus integrative reanalyses and external functional annotations. It is not a therapeutic trial and does not experimentally prove the STK11 immune mechanism.
- **Registration state:** first-pass main-text deep reading complete; independent review and all acceptance gates remain pending.

## Question, cohort, and assays

The CPTAC study asked what deep proteomic and post-translational-modification data add to genomics in treatment-naive lung adenocarcinoma, including molecular taxonomy, driver consequences, immune states, smoking-associated biology, and therapeutic hypotheses.

- **Human cohort:** 110 treatment-naive LUAD tumors and 101 paired NATs, prospectively collected across 13 tissue-source sites in eight countries; 111 participants are described in STAR Methods, with analyzable modality counts varying by assay. Complete common data were available for 101 tumors and 96 NATs.
- **Original omics:** WES (nominal 150×), WGS (nominal 15×), RNA-seq, miRNA-seq, DNA methylation, TMT global proteomics, phosphoproteomics, and acetylproteomics.
- **Original tissue validation:** expert pathology review and selected immunohistochemistry, including ALK/pALK.
- **Reanalysis/external resources:** TCGA expression subtypes, Connectivity Map perturbation signatures, DepMap RNAi/CRISPR dependency, xCell and ESTIMATE deconvolution, and published pathway/signature collections. These should not be presented as new wet-lab experiments in this paper.

## Central results

1. **Four multi-omics clusters linked drivers, geography, sex, and biology.** The four NMF clusters recapitulated and refined established transcriptomic subtypes. Cluster 3, a proximal-proliferative group, was enriched for Vietnamese patients and STK11 alterations, whereas Cluster 4 was enriched for EGFR-mutant, female, Chinese cases and was nearly devoid of KRAS/STK11 alterations (Results, physical pp. 4-6; Figure 1, pp. 67-68). These are cohort associations entangled with geography and smoking, not causal ancestry effects.

2. **Proteomics exposed consequences invisible or damped at RNA level.** Across CNA analyses, 6,043 RNA, 2,354 protein, and 244 phosphoprotein positive cis-correlations were found, with only 156 shared across all three layers. This attenuation supports measuring proteins/PTMs rather than assuming DNA/RNA changes propagate unchanged (physical p. 7; Figure 3).

3. **STK11 alterations reduced the cognate protein/PTM signal and associated with downstream changes.** Among selected drivers, STK11, RBM10, RB1, NF1, and KEAP1 mutations were associated with lower cognate protein and phosphosite abundance. STK11 mutation also associated with higher SMAD4 S138 phosphorylation, and STK11-mutant tumor/NAT comparisons were enriched for amino-acid and nitric-oxide metabolic proteins, suggesting urea-cycle perturbation. These are discovery associations, not proof of pathway dependence (physical pp. 8, 15; Figures 4 and 7).

4. **The principal STK11 contribution is an immune-cold human-tumor phenotype.** xCell defined hot tumor-enriched (HTE), cold tumor-enriched (CTE), and NAT-enriched groups. HTE tumors had stronger B-, CD4-, CD8-, dendritic-cell, and macrophage signatures plus higher PD1/PD-L1, but also T-regulatory and suppressive cytokine signals. STK11-mutant tumors showed the strongest immune downregulation, with reduced inferred dendritic-cell, NKT-cell, and macrophage signatures at FDR<0.1. The direction was reversed in matched NATs, where macrophage and dendritic-cell signatures were enriched (physical pp. 10-12; Figure 5A-D).

5. **The cold phenotype was not explained by low mutation burden.** NMF Cluster 3, enriched for STK11 mutation, ranked second-highest in somatic mutation burden. ESTIMATE immune scores were lowest particularly among STK11-mutant/KRAS-WT tumors. A histopathology deep-learning classifier distinguished STK11-mutant from WT slides with reported 94% slide-level accuracy, with mutant tiles dominated by tumor epithelium and WT tiles by immune cells. The classifier is an internal discovery result, not clinical diagnostic validation (physical p. 12; Figure 5D-E).

6. **Neutrophil degranulation was proposed as a protein-level suppressive mechanism.** Independent-component analysis identified an STK11-enriched protein cluster whose most significant pathway was neutrophil degranulation; all 16 measured proteins assigned to that process were coherently overexpressed, including CAMP, LTF, BPI, MMP8, MMP9, MPO, LCN2, ELANE, and ARG1. The signal was absent at RNA level, consistent with storage of translated proteins in granules. Importantly, inferred neutrophil abundance itself was not enriched or depleted, so this is a proteomic functional signature, not proof of greater neutrophil number or causal immunosuppression (physical p. 12; Figure 5F-G).

7. **Therapeutic outputs were nomination, not treatment evidence.** Phosphorylation outliers suggested SOS1 inhibition in KRAS-mutant disease and PTPN11/SHP2 inhibition in EGFR-mutant or ALK-fusion disease; mutation-specific kinase outliers were annotated using external dependency/drug databases. No patients were treated on the basis of these nominations (physical pp. 9-10; Figure 4).

## Key negative findings and cautions

- After covariate adjustment, linear models did not yield coherent sex-, stage-, or histology-associated marker sets; smoking effects were also difficult to isolate from correlated variables.
- STK11-associated immune downregulation was not attributable to lower mutation burden.
- Neutrophil-degranulation proteins were not mirrored at RNA level, and inferred neutrophil abundance did not change; equating this signature with cell number would be incorrect.
- Bulk tissue cannot separate epithelial and microenvironmental sources or resolve spatial heterogeneity.
- The study explicitly characterizes its associations as hypothesis-generating; druggable outliers are not validated therapeutic dependencies in patients.

## Limitations and claim ceiling

- Mutation, ancestry/geography, sex, and smoking are interdependent; the cohort is not large enough to fully disentangle them.
- Multiple testing and discovery-oriented modeling increase false-positive risk despite FDR control.
- Missingness differs across modalities; some cross-omic comparisons use smaller subsets.
- xCell/ESTIMATE and pathology deep learning are computational inference. The paper contains no STK11-isogenic perturbation proving that LKB1 loss causes neutrophil degranulation or immune exclusion.
- The appropriate STK11 claim is: **in treatment-naive human LUAD, STK11 mutation is associated with a proteomically visible immune-cold state and a candidate neutrophil-degranulation mechanism**. It is not a validated predictive biomarker or causal therapeutic mechanism.

## STK11-topic role

This is a foundational **human proteogenomic association/resource paper**. It supplies the tumor/NAT and protein-versus-RNA evidence that supports STK11 immune-cold biology, while leaving causality and clinical actionability to mechanistic and prospective studies.
