## Research question and evidence sequence

Can protein-level phenotypes stratify NSCLC across histological categories, reveal immune-state differences not captured by mutation alone, and support a practical classifier? The study first profiles resected human tumors, identifies six proteome subtypes, integrates previously generated transcript/methylation data and new targeted sequencing, examines immune and noncanonical-peptide patterns, then focuses on an STK11-associated FGL1/CPS1/HNF1A phenotype. Limited cell-model perturbations support an STK11/AMPK relationship to protein expression. Finally, cohort and single-sample classifiers are evaluated internally, on public datasets and on two newly measured cohorts.

STK11 is directly relevant through mutation enrichment, protein-level correlations, expression-defined STK11-WT subsets and functional cell-model evidence. It is not merely a background citation. Nonetheless, this is not a prospective STK11-selected treatment trial, and the six-class classifier is not a mutation detector. Phenotype, sequence mutation, low STK11 abundance and inferred pathway activity are distinct quantities. FGL1-LAG3-mediated immune escape is a mechanistic hypothesis informed partly by earlier literature; this paper does not newly demonstrate clinical benefit from LAG3/FGL1 or mTOR blockade, nor directly measure systemic immune suppression by FGL1.

## Assay provenance and study classification

Original data: discovery deep DDA quantitative tissue proteome (PXD020191); discovery/late-stage DIA (PXD020548); validation DIA (PXD025560); targeted370-gene DNA sequencing (EGAS00001005482); tissue IHC and morphology; limited cultured-cell protein readouts. Previously measured discovery expression microarrays and DNA methylation arrays are integrated reanalyses, not newly generated RNA-seq or methylation sequencing. Public TCGA, GEO, GDSC and external proteome datasets provide distinct corroborating analyses. No original single-cell RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, ATAC-seq, HLA immunopeptidomics or direct antigen-specific T-cell assay is reported. Use of Seurat/UMAP and a cell-atlas-derived marker list does not make the assay single-cell.

Study roles: clinical-correlative proteogenomics, computational classification and a limited preclinical mechanism component. Treatment context is exploratory cell-line drug association (docetaxel/mTOR inhibitors), protein-level AMPK-pathway perturbation, and proposed checkpoint-related stratification. There is no prospective intervention cohort, demonstrated LAG3/mTOR combination efficacy, or patient dosing recommendation. Mutation enrichment across subtypes is not pairwise co-mutation synergy; STK11/KEAP1/SMARCA4 enrichment does not prove every listed gene co-occurs in every subtype4 tumor.

## Alerts, negative findings and claim ceiling

1. Candidate NCPs: carry the104-reference validation and36 incorrect identifications alongside the nominal FDR. Do not call all651 validated immunogenic neoantigens or equate total-tissue MS with HLA-presented immunopeptidomics (Results p.7; ED6 pp.29-30).
2. TP53-NCP regression P=0.057 and TCGA subtype OS P=0.216 are nonsignificant. Retain these negatives; prognosis does not establish adjuvant-treatment benefit (Fig.3D p.48; ED9B p.35; Results pp.7,10).
3. Classifier yield versus accuracy:208→188→175 and84→61→58 are sample-selection/classification flows.94%/87% are internal repeated-test averages, not prospective external diagnostic sensitivity/specificity (Figs.7-8; ED8-10).
4. Complete-pairing error: the141 proteomes,140 sequenced,139 TMB,118 RNA,113 methylation and selected IHC subsets are not interchangeable. Source collection192 is not analyzed n141; only a subset of late-stage specimens can be classified (Methods pp.14-16; Figs.1-3,8).
5. Causal ceiling: FGL1-LAG3 binding and immune suppression are cited background, while this article supplies expression/correlation and limited upstream perturbation evidence. No direct demonstration here of systemic immune evasion, clinical anti-LAG3 efficacy or synergistic mTOR/checkpoint therapy (Results pp.8-9; Discussion pp.12-14; Fig.6).
6. Association-based regulation and classification: arbitrary FGL1/CPS1 cutoffs, uncorrected Fig.5 pairwise tests and Fig.6 drug-screen comparisons require exploratory wording. Mutation alone was not sufficient for the reported drug association. Protein expression is not a validated surrogate for STK11 enzymatic activity.
7. CTLA4 in the checkpoint display is RNA, not measured protein. Bulk signatures/UMAP are not single-cell or spatial-transcriptomic measurements (Fig.4 pp.50-51; Methods pp.17-18).
8. Caption/graphic inconsistencies: Fig.5 caption p.53 labels the final boxplot f although displayed panel is h; Fig.2 caption uses CXL9 instead of CXCL9; Fig.6E graphic p.54 labels alpha-tubulin while its caption p.55 says beta-tubulin; ED7D graphic says1st/99th percentiles while its caption says5th/95th; ED10I graphic says5,123 proteins while its caption says5,124. Refer to displayed identities where unambiguous, but withhold the unresolved control, percentile cutoff and late-stage total rather than silently harmonizing them.
9. Fig.6C caption p.55 names alpha-actin normalization, but the displayed blot p.54 labels beta-actin. Withhold exact loading-control identity for that panel unless the original source data resolves it. This does not license rewriting the blot.
10. Discussion p.13 calls TCF1 a mouse equivalent of HNF1A, while the cited context is T-cell signaling; TCF1 is ambiguous nomenclature. NCBI lists TCF1/TCF-1 aliases for both distinct records, [HNF1A Gene6927](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/6927/) and [TCF7 Gene6932](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/6932). Do not equate HNF1A with TCF7 or transfer a T-cell transcription-factor mechanism based on that sentence without source clarification.
11. Prior-cohort count discrepancy: introduction summarizes Gillette n110, displayed reanalysis n111. Preserve the latter as this paper's displayed analysis and do not silently harmonize publication-level participant counts (p.3; ED9 p.35).
12. Main scientific limitations: predominance of resected early-stage tumors, tumor/stroma admixture, nonrandom selection of pathology subsets, partial assay overlap, modest cell-model scope, data-derived labels, incomplete classification of biopsies, high candidate-NCP error and no prospective treatment endpoint. No clinical toxicity/safety evidence is generated.
