## Design and measurement
Nonrandomized phase I/Ib modified 3+3 dose escalation: 25 monotherapy and 25 combination recipients. Monotherapy doses 80–640 mg twice daily; combination 160–320 mg twice daily plus spartalizumab 400 mg every four weeks. Monotherapy MTD 480 mg twice daily, combination MTD 240 mg twice daily. DLT observation windows differed, 28 versus 56 days; a generic cycle-1 MTD definition later in the methods should not erase this distinction. The abstract says efficacy was neither a primary nor secondary endpoint, whereas the methods explicitly identify preliminary efficacy among secondary objectives: preserve this source discrepancy. Eligibility ECOG 0–1 contrasts with one monotherapy patient recorded as ECOG 2 in Table 1.

Plasma LC-MS/MS, noncompartmental pharmacokinetics, 22C3 PD-L1 TPS, CD8/CD33 immunohistochemistry and NanoString PanCancer Immune Profiling were used. This is targeted transcriptional profiling, not whole-transcriptome or single-cell sequencing. The adenosine signature contains IL1B, PTGS2 and CXCL1/2/3/5/6/8, imported from earlier RCC work. Baseline KRAS/EGFR/MET records are descriptive and mutation categories can overlap; no STK11-specific analysis is established.

## Main results and visual evidence
Figure 1, p14: concentration-time profiles demonstrate nonlinear, dose- and time-dependent exposure, with a 0.5–3-hour median time to peak, approximately threefold accumulation after a week, reported half-lives of 7–61 hours and substantial interpatient variability. Exceeding an in-vitro binding concentration at doses of at least 160 mg is not direct evidence of intratumoral receptor occupancy.

Table 1, pp17–18: prior immunotherapy is documented for 19/25 monotherapy and 9/25 combination patients. The remaining history categories include unavailable information; do not automatically call all remaining patients proven immunotherapy-naive. These imbalances and nonrandom assignment preclude between-arm causal efficacy comparisons. All 50 experienced adverse events. Related events occurred in 84% versus 60%, but grade 3–4 related events in 16% versus 36%, so a lower all-grade rate does not establish a safer combination. Two monotherapy and four combination DLTs were recorded. Six on-study deaths, two versus four, were considered unrelated. Absence of treatment-related deaths is not absence of deaths. Pneumonitis was not confined to combination therapy: the monotherapy CR case also had suspected related grade-3 pneumonitis.

Figures 2–3, pp15–16 and Table 2, p19: each cohort had one CR and one PR, all reported in immunotherapy-naive patients. Response-evaluable denominators are 21 and 24, yielding ORR 9.5% (95% CI 1.2–30.4) and 8.3% (1.0–27.0), not an intention-to-treat ORR based on 25 each. DCR is 9/21 (42.9%) and 16/24 (66.7%) in Table 2; discussion all-treated DCR is 36% and 64%. Preserve denominator labels. Monotherapy CR and PR durations were 1.1 and 6.4 months; combination CR and PR durations 15.9 and 16.6 months, ongoing. A CR can show less than 100% reduction in a waterfall because pathologic nodes became subcentimeter rather than disappearing. One monotherapy SD case is described as on study 36.7 months despite an aggregate monotherapy exposure maximum of 12.0 months; flag rather than silently reconcile. Median OS 9.7 versus 5.4 months and PFS 3.9 versus 2.8 months are descriptive, not comparative efficacy estimates.

Biomarker-evaluable populations differ: baseline PD-L1 linked with best change 12 and 17; CD8/CD33 five matched pairs, without a demonstrated change; RNA 11 combination pairs, nine SD, one PR, one PD. Increased IRF1, GBP2 and JAK2 expression suggests treatment-associated immune signaling but does not demonstrate mediation of response. Main text does not fully specify normalization and multiplicity control; do not invent them. Neither baseline nor on-treatment adenosine signature was associated with survival, and response analysis was limited by imbalance. Lack of observed PD-L1 association in a tiny sample is not a validated negative treatment-selection biomarker.

## Interpretation and learning boundary
The discussion cites a completed subsequent phase-II study, NCT03207867, with insufficient activity in NSCLC and no further pursuit of this combination in that indication at publication. This is a cited subsequent result, not primary phase-I data or a current-development-status search. It should accompany, not be omitted from, a summary of early biological signals. CD73/EGFR hypotheses are discussion-level literature, not newly generated genomic evidence. Useful language covers nonrandomized dose escalation, evaluable populations, concentration-dependent exposure, paired targeted immune profiling, modest activity and development discontinuation. Reusable frames are newly composed and must retain safety, denominator, null-result and comparator limitations. This paper is not promoted to the STK11 highlight list.
