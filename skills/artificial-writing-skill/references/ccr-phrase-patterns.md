## Usage rules

Use these as abstract functional patterns, not sentences to copy. Replace every bracketed field with user-supplied evidence. Choose a pattern only after identifying the section, study design, and claim tier.

- Do not optimize prose by inserting high-frequency transitions.
- Do not use a phrase as a quota or journal requirement.
- Keep one principal claim per sentence when practical.
- Verify that `respectively` maps two ordered lists one to one.
- Use `statistically significant` only for an explicit statistical comparison.
- Prefer the shortest pattern that preserves the full evidence ledger.

## Gap and objective

Suitable mainly for Abstract and Introduction:

- `[Clinical problem] remains a major limitation in [population].`
- `The role of [factor] in [context] remains unclear.`
- `Whether [specific question] has not been established.`
- `Evidence for [use] is limited in [population or setting].`
- `However, [precise unresolved gap].`
- `To address this gap, we evaluated [question] in [design/population].`
- `We aimed to assess [endpoint or relationship] in [population/model].`
- `We sought to characterize [phenotype, mechanism, or resistance state].`
- `This study examined whether [testable question].`

Do not use `novel`, `first`, or `largest` unless the scope is verifiable and material.

## Methods and definitions

Suitable for Methods:

- `[Population] was defined as [operational definition].`
- `Patients were included if [criteria].`
- `Patients were excluded if [criteria].`
- `[Specimens/data] were collected at [time point].`
- `[Assay/analysis] was performed according to [specified protocol].`
- `[Endpoint] was assessed by [method and schedule].`
- `The primary endpoint was [endpoint].`
- `[Analysis] was prespecified in [protocol/plan].`
- `[Analysis] was conducted post hoc.`
- `P values were adjusted for multiple comparisons using [method].`
- `No adjustment for multiplicity was performed.`
- `The model was evaluated in an independent [temporal/external] cohort.`

Do not add a protocol, analysis status, cutoff, or validation cohort absent from the source.

## Quantified Results

Suitable for Results and the Results portion of an Abstract:

- `A total of [N] patients were included.`
- `Among [N] evaluable patients, [n] ([%]) had [outcome].`
- `At a median follow-up of [time], [event/outcome] was [estimate].`
- `[Outcome] was observed in [n/N] patients.`
- `[Group A] had [estimate] compared with [estimate] in [Group B].`
- `The estimates were [A] and [B], respectively.`
- `[Exposure] was associated with [outcome] (HR, [x]; 95% CI, [a-b]).`
- `After adjustment for [covariates], the association remained [description].`
- `No statistically significant difference was observed between the groups ([estimate and CI]).`
- `The point estimate favored [group], but the confidence interval included no effect.`
- `[Outcome] was numerically higher in [group], although the estimate was imprecise.`
- `Results were consistent in [specified sensitivity or validation analysis].`
- `At data cutoff, [maturity/follow-up statement].`

Use `associated with` rather than causal improvement for observational data. Do not use `equivalent` for ordinary nonsignificance.

## Comparison and transition

Use only when the logical relation is real:

- `However, [limitation or opposing result].`
- `Although [concession], [main clause].`
- `In contrast to [evidence], [current observation].`
- `[Outcome A] increased, whereas [Outcome B] remained unchanged.`
- `These results were consistent with [specific prior or orthogonal evidence].`
- `Therefore, [inference directly supported by prior clauses].`

Avoid decorative `however`, `notably`, `importantly`, and `therefore`.

## Interpretation and evidence alignment

Suitable mainly for Discussion:

- `These findings suggest that [bounded interpretation].`
- `The data support a model in which [mechanism bounded to evidence].`
- `[Mechanism] may contribute to [observed phenotype].`
- `[Observation] could reflect [plausible explanation].`
- `This interpretation is consistent with [specific evidence].`
- `Taken together, these data support [narrow conclusion].`
- `One possible explanation is [mechanism], although [uncertainty].`
- `The observed association does not establish [causal or predictive claim].`
- `The clinical significance of [finding] remains to be determined.`

Reserve `demonstrate` for direct evidence that actually establishes the stated phenomenon; do not use it as a stronger synonym for `suggest`.

## Limitations and validation

Suitable for Discussion and Conclusions:

- `This study was limited by [specific limitation], which may [effect on inference].`
- `The retrospective design may have introduced [selection/confounding bias].`
- `The small sample size limited the precision of [estimate].`
- `The short follow-up limited assessment of [late or survival outcome].`
- `These findings should be interpreted with caution because [reason].`
- `Prospective validation in [population/setting] is required.`
- `Independent external validation is warranted before [intended use].`
- `A randomized comparison is needed to determine [comparative question].`
- `Additional mechanistic studies are needed to distinguish [alternatives].`
- `The exploratory finding is hypothesis-generating.`
- `Because multiplicity was not controlled, the P value should be considered nominal.`

Name the inference affected by a limitation. Do not append a generic limitations list unrelated to the claims.

## Translational implications and conclusions

Choose one specific use supported by the evidence:

- `These findings support prospective evaluation of [intervention/strategy].`
- `[Marker] may inform risk stratification after [clinical event].`
- `[Assay] has potential as a monitoring approach, pending [validation].`
- `The results provide a rationale for testing [strategy] in [population].`
- `[Finding] may help identify patients for [specified research or trial context].`
- `Integration into clinical practice requires [analytical/clinical/prospective] validation.`
- `Further development should evaluate [specific safety, dose, utility, or comparative question].`

Do not write `establishes clinical utility`, `should change practice`, or `improves patient outcomes` unless the design directly supports that claim.

## Synthetic calibration examples

These examples are constructed templates, not quotations from the corpus.

**Observational biomarker**

- Calibrated: `[Marker] was associated with longer PFS (HR, [x]; 95% CI, [a-b]); prospective validation is required.`
- Overstated: `[Marker] improved survival and predicts treatment benefit.`

**Null comparison**

- Calibrated: `No statistically significant difference was observed; the CI included clinically relevant effects in both directions.`
- Overstated: `The groups were equivalent.`

**Exploratory subgroup**

- Calibrated: `The exploratory subgroup finding was based on a nominal P value without multiplicity adjustment and is hypothesis-generating.`
- Overstated: `The treatment was effective in the subgroup.`

**Early single-arm study**

- Calibrated: `Preliminary antitumor activity was observed, supporting further evaluation in a controlled study.`
- Overstated: `The treatment was superior to standard care.`

**Internally validated model**

- Calibrated: `The model showed discrimination in internal cross-validation; calibration and independent external validation remain necessary.`
- Overstated: `The model has established clinical utility.`
