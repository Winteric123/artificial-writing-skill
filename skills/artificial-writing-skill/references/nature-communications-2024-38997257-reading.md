## Question and design

The study tested whether G6PD-mediated oxidative pentose-phosphate activity is required selectively in KRAS/LKB1 (KL) lung tumors and examined the downstream redox, p53, lipid, and serine-metabolism consequences. Conditional G6pd deletion was introduced at tumor initiation in KP, KL, and KPL genetically engineered mouse models. The authors also used tumor-derived cell lines, allografts, isotope tracing, lipidomics/metabolomics, diet manipulation, bulk RNA sequencing, and an exploratory pooled human survival analysis.

## Main findings with source anchors

1. **G6PD deletion suppressed KL, but not KP, tumorigenesis.** In KP mice, wet lung weight, tumor burden, proliferation and survival were not significantly changed; median survival was 155 versus 167.5 days (P=0.0861). In KL mice, deletion markedly reduced tumor number/burden and extended median survival from 143 to 173 days (P=0.0036) (Fig. 1, physical p. 3). This supports genotype-selective preclinical dependence.

2. **The KL effect coincided with impaired redox homeostasis.** KL G6pd deletion lowered NADPH and the NADPH/NADP+ ratio, changed glutathione redox balance, and increased NRF2/NQO1, 8-oxo-dG and γH2AX staining (Fig. 2, physical p. 4). Tumor-derived KL cells were more sensitive to G6PD inhibition, hydrogen peroxide, and high-dose vitamin C; the vitamin-C allograft result remained a short, high-dose preclinical experiment (Fig. 3, physical pp. 5-6).

3. **p53 was required for the tumor-growth phenotype.** G6PD-deficient KL tumors showed increased p53, p21 and cleaved caspase-3. When Trp53 was also deleted (KPL), G6PD loss no longer produced significant differences in wet lung weight, burden, Ki67 or survival (median 89 versus 89.5 days; P=0.2212), although oxidative-stress markers remained elevated (Fig. 4, physical p. 7). Thus, oxidative stress alone was insufficient for growth suppression without p53 in this model.

4. **G6PD loss impaired de novo lipid synthesis, and dietary lipid partly rescued growth.** Deuterium tracing showed reduced C16:0 labeling and lower lipid pools in KL tumors. A high-fat diet increased tumor growth/proliferation in G6PD-deficient KL mice and partially reduced the genotype difference, but did not normalize every endpoint or tumor initiation (Fig. 5, physical pp. 8-9).

5. **Central glucose-carbon labeling was largely preserved.** [U-13C6]glucose tracing did not reveal a broad loss of glycolytic or TCA-cycle labeling after G6PD deletion, despite changes in selected metabolite pool sizes (Fig. 6, physical p. 9). The result argues for a more specific redox/biosynthetic adaptation rather than global collapse of glucose metabolism.

6. **Serine metabolism was adaptively reprogrammed.** Glucose-derived serine/glycine labeling declined while tumor serine/glycine pools were maintained. KL G6PD-deficient cells increased serine consumption; in vivo labeled-serine experiments supported serine-derived NADPH production. Removing serine/glycine preferentially impaired G6PD-deficient KL cells (Fig. 7, physical p. 10). This is an adaptive route, not evidence that a serine-restricted diet is clinically effective.

7. **Human survival evidence was exploratory and prognostic.** A pooled cBioPortal analysis across 28 studies used median expression splits and associated high G6PD expression with poorer overall survival in KRAS/LKB1 but not KRAS/TP53 cases. The cross-study, retrospective mRNA analysis does not demonstrate treatment prediction or validate a clinical cutoff.

## Methods and quality notes

- Both sexes were used; animals were randomly assigned, group allocation/outcome assessment were described as blinded, and a power calculation was reported.
- Bulk-tumor RNA sequencing used eight wild-type and seven knockout KL tumors.
- Figure legends provide endpoint-specific denominators; image counts, biological samples, clones, and animals must not be interchanged.
- Simultaneous tumor initiation and G6pd deletion models dependency during development, not pharmacologic inhibition of established disease.
- The KPL model uses Trp53 deletion and does not capture diverse human TP53 missense gain-of-function states.
- Neither a selective clinical G6PD inhibitor nor a serine/glycine intervention was tested in patients; proposed combinations remain hypotheses.

## Interpretation boundary

The paper supports G6PD as a p53-contingent metabolic dependency in KL mouse tumors, linking oxidative-pentose-phosphate activity to redox control and lipid synthesis. It also identifies serine metabolism as an adaptive NADPH source after G6PD loss. These findings do not yet establish the safety, efficacy, dose, or biomarker performance of G6PD inhibition in patients.

## Transfer-ready synthesis

Conditional G6pd deletion produced a strong KL-selective antitumor phenotype while leaving KP tumors largely unaffected. Mechanistically, loss of G6PD increased oxidative stress, activated a p53-dependent suppressive response, reduced de novo lipogenesis, and induced compensatory serine use for NADPH production. The work defines a genotype- and p53-contextualized preclinical vulnerability rather than a clinically validated therapy.
