## Study and denominator map
- Retrospective exploratory biomarker analysis nested in randomized phase III ALEX, not random allocation to a cfDNA concentration. Parent ITT 303 (alectinib 152, crizotinib 151); baseline cfDNA biomarker-evaluable population 276 (137/139); FoundationACT NGS subset 222 (107/115). The multivariable forest uses the NGS subset, not all 276.
- Total plasma cfDNA measured by TapeStation D1000, ng/mL, is not ctDNA tumor fraction. Tumor DNA positivity means a detectable somatic alteration; a negative plasma result is not proof of absence of a tissue driver. All participants were ALK-positive by tissue IHC; 77/222 had no detectable ALK rearrangement in plasma.
- Overall median cfDNA 11.53 ng/mL was a cohort-derived dichotomization, not an independently validated clinical threshold. Baseline only; longitudinal monitoring is a proposed future application.
- Response denominators in Table 2 are 71/65 crizotinib and 62/67 alectinib for low/high cfDNA, smaller than Table 1 BEP groups 72/67 and 66/71. DoR analyses are responder-selected, not the ITT cohort.

## Results and inference
- Higher cfDNA correlated with disease burden; correlations were modest. Figure 2 gives Spearman 0.31 for lesion count and 0.38 for both organ count and SLD; Results prose gives 0.30 for lesion count. Do not mix Pearson (0.20/0.31/0.20) and Spearman coefficients.
- Alectinib improved PFS relative to crizotinib in both low- and high-cfDNA groups, HR 0.38 (95% CI 0.23–0.61) and 0.43 (0.29–0.65), respectively. This does not establish a cfDNA-by-treatment interaction.
- Within-arm high-versus-low cfDNA adjusted PFS HRs were 2.04 (1.07–3.89), P=0.0305, with alectinib and 1.83 (1.11–3.00), P=0.0169, with crizotinib. These are prognostic associations conditional on measured covariates, not proof cfDNA causes resistance.
- Detectable ctDNA had unadjusted PFS associations, but the adjusted associations were not significant: alectinib HR 1.95 (0.78–4.83), P=0.1506; crizotinib HR 1.52 (0.88–2.63), P=0.1323. Do not substitute these for significant total-cfDNA concentration findings.
- ORR was not significantly different between cfDNA groups in either arm (Table 2 P=0.3720 and 0.9191). OS was immature and used a later cutoff than PFS. Adjusted OS HRs for high/low cfDNA were 2.52 (1.08–5.88) and 2.63 (1.27–5.47); body unadjusted HRs 2.58/3.53 are different models, not inconsistent transcription.
- Figure 4: crizotinib TP53 P=0.0515 and alectinib SLD P=0.0513 are not significant at 0.05 despite rounded CI bounds of 1.00. Alectinib liver-lesion HR 0.44, P=0.0496 is exploratory and must not be interpreted as a causal protective effect of metastases.

## Source alerts
- Results p2 states cfDNA range 1.6–64.5 ng/mL, while Figures 1–2 visibly plot observations above 100 and 500 ng/mL on axes labelled cfDNA concentration. Preserve as an unresolved range/normalization inconsistency; do not silently repair the upper bound.
- Figure 2 caption describes its cross-sectional correlation curves as hazard functions from Cox analyses. This is inconsistent with the scatterplot variables; do not transfer the caption as a Methods template for hazard estimation.
- Median-cutoff selection, biomarker missingness, retrospective analyses, multiple exploratory tests, immature OS and no prospective prognostic validation limit clinical use. No demonstrated benefit from treatment intensification or immunotherapy was tested here.
